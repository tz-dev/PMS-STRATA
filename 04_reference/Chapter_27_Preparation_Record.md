## 3. Contract Lock

### 3.1 Required core claim

Chapter 27 must establish:

> `DECOMPOSE` is admissible only where added distinctions produce praxis purchase, the finer reconstruction remains within source-supported precision, source reference remains identifiable, component relations are reconstructible, and the coarser function remains traceable or is explicitly revised. Finer resolution has no automatic epistemic priority.

The local boundary assessment must declare:

- the tested source object and source claim;
- source and target granularity;
- expected additional praxis difference;
- actual changed or unchanged dimensions;
- source-support status;
- source-reference status;
- component and relation status;
- component sensitivity findings;
- coarser-function status;
- type-integrity status;
- lower- and upper-boundary status;
- Stop, Claim Reduction, Failure, or Non-Capture pressure;
- canonical Loss and unresolved structure.

### 3.2 Lower SUB Boundary

The lower boundary is crossed downward when the finer distinction is valid or possible but lacks praxeological purchase for the tested claim. Typical indicators include:

- more timestamps without a changed temporal relation;
- more component names without changed roles or relations;
- additional descriptive precision without changed action corridors, costs, binding, alternatives, expectations, source function, Loss, or uncertainty;
- repeated Resolution Neutrality without a new comparison basis;
- differences visible only at instrument or interface granularity;
- distinctions whose removal changes no warranted reconstruction.

```text
additional distinction
+
no supported change to a praxis-relevant claim dimension
→
lower-boundary pressure
```

A lower-boundary result does not imply that the detail is false. It means the detail cannot carry the current transformation claim. The coarser reconstruction may remain preferable, and Optional Stop may be the correct positive outcome.

### 3.3 Resolution without Purchase

Resolution without Purchase must remain distinct from unsupported detail. The former may be source-supported and correctly reconstructed while making no difference to the tested claim. The latter lacks sufficient source or relation support.

```text
supported detail + no changed warranted claim
→ Resolution Neutrality candidate

unsupported detail
→ support, reduction, failure, or Stop pressure
```

Chapter 27 must not convert every no-gain result into failure. It must also prevent a large body of neutral detail from being presented as a stronger transformation merely because the representation is denser, more technical, or more interactive.

### 3.4 Upper SUB Boundary

The upper boundary is crossed when finer reconstruction ceases to remain reconstructibly anchored to the source object and its tested function. Typical indicators include:

- the source object disappears into an inventory of parts;
- components are named but their relations are missing;
- relations are asserted beyond source support;
- the source reference fragments into several objects without a declared new operation;
- the coarser function cannot be confirmed, revised, or explicitly rejected;
- the origin type is retained only nominally;
- microdescription proliferates beyond reconstructive load;
- model completion fills source gaps.

```text
more parts or finer relations
+
loss of source reference or coarser-function traceability
→
upper-boundary pressure
```

The upper boundary is not identical to the system-wide Traceability Ceiling, though it locally applies the same anti-abstraction logic within `DECOMPOSE`. Here the failure is fragmentation without reconstruction rather than a high-level label without source load.

### 3.5 Fragmentation without Reconstruction

Fragmentation occurs where a decomposition produces components without a defensible reconstruction of how they relate to the source object. It may take several forms:

- component enumeration without relational structure;
- multiple local maps with no shared reference;
- source-type retention by label only;
- finer events without the event, path, trajectory, or occurrence function they supposedly carry;
- graph nodes whose edges express layout or similarity rather than source-supported relations;
- parts treated as self-sufficient explanations when their function depends on the whole relation map.

Fragmentation may require Claim Reduction if a weaker component-level finding survives. It may require `failed_transformation` when the decomposition claim itself collapses. It may produce `non_capture` when rival relational reconstructions remain equally supported.

## 4. Source Ceiling

### 4.1 Local Source-Ceiling rule

A finer reconstruction may not be semantically or structurally more precise than its sources. Chapter 27 uses the following local support statuses:

```text
supported
partially_supported
underdetermined
speculative
unavailable
```

These are local source-status findings, not canonical Output Classes and not a universal scale. They must remain claim-specific and dimension-specific.

The Source Ceiling is reached when the next finer claim would require:

- an unrecorded component;
- an unobserved relation;
- a precise order where only coexistence is supported;
- a role or threshold inferred only from the coarse type;
- a Non-Event inferred from missing records;
- an intention inferred from structural delay;
- a source identity reconstructed from model convenience;
- an irrecoverable historical detail presented as recovered.

### 4.2 Source Ceiling versus Non-Capture

```text
Source Ceiling reached
≠ non_capture automatically
```

If a bounded weaker claim survives, Claim Reduction may be sufficient. If the tested component relation cannot be supported and no weaker transformation remains, Failure or Mandatory Stop may follow. `non_capture` is appropriate when the question remains legitimate but the available sources cannot discriminate among multiple responsible reconstructions.

### 4.3 Source Ceiling versus Missing Information

Missing Information is a source condition. Source Ceiling is the inferential boundary imposed by that condition. Neither permits filling the gap through formal elegance, graph completion, model priors, analogy, or contextual usefulness.

```text
missing source structure
≠ negative finding automatically
≠ positive component automatically
≠ permission to infer the most coherent relation
```

## 5. Counterfactual Component Test

### 5.1 Governing question

The local test asks:

> Would removing, disabling, delaying, replacing, or materially changing this supported component alter the reconstructed source function, while the remaining relation map is held fixed as far as responsibly possible?

The prepared component-sensitivity findings are:

```text
constitutive
strongly_modulating
weakly_modulating
replaceable
incidental
underdetermined
```

These findings are local analytical classes, not new PMS primitives, not operator types, not person properties, and not canonical Output Classes.

### 5.2 Test discipline

The test requires:

- a supported component candidate;
- a declared source function or bounded source claim;
- an explicit intervention variant;
- the relation map held fixed only within source-supported limits;
- expected functional difference;
- rival substitution or compensation possibilities;
- uncertainty and Loss;
- a reduction or Stop condition.

The test does not establish universal causality. It tests the component’s load within a bounded reconstruction. A component may be replaceable in one frame, constitutive in another, or underdetermined at the current Source Ceiling.

### 5.3 Non-compensation

Strong support for one component cannot compensate for unsupported relations elsewhere. A constitutive finding does not rescue a broken source reference. A plausible replacement does not erase inherited Loss. Formal counterfactual completeness does not establish historical availability.

## 6. Coarser-Function Traceability

The coarser function remains traceable where:

- the source object remains identifiable;
- load-bearing components are distinguished from incidental detail;
- their relations are source-supported;
- changes in those components have bounded functional consequences;
- internal heterogeneity is reconstructed rather than merely listed;
- finer findings can be translated back to the coarse claim;
- the coarse claim is confirmed, refined, internally differentiated, partially preserved, rejected, or left underdetermined.

The following remain distinct:

```text
source type preserved
≠ source function confirmed

component role identified
≠ coarser function reconstructed

coarser function rejected
≠ source object erased
```

A successful decomposition may reject the original coarser-function claim. That rejection can be a gain in reconstructive warrant and may map to `claim_reduction_required`, `admissible_with_bounded_claim`, or another canonical class depending on the full claim record.

## 7. Type Preservation and Revision

Type preservation means that an eligible typed occurrence or composite is not silently replaced by its components. It does not mean the original type claim must survive regardless of evidence.

```text
Frame-typed occurrence
→ finer Frame relations
≠ Frame operator type decomposed

Attractor-typed occurrence
→ recurrence and maintenance structure
≠ Attractor operator type decomposed

Asymmetry-typed occurrence
→ distributed access, cost, or exposure relations
≠ Ω operator type decomposed
```

The finer reconstruction may:

- confirm the source type;
- refine its occurrence-level content;
- restrict it to a bounded dimension;
- show partial preservation;
- reject the source-typing claim;
- leave it underdetermined.

Type integrity therefore requires explicit revision rather than forced survival.

## 8. No Privilege of Fine Resolution

Chapter 27 must state explicitly that finer reconstruction can be:

```text
better
co-equal
worse
incomparable
```

for the current tested claim.

A coarser representation may remain analytically preferable when it:

- preserves the relevant source function with lower Loss;
- avoids unsupported component relations;
- better matches source precision;
- communicates the tested distinction without fragmentation;
- supports comparison across cases;
- prevents false certainty.

This is not a defence of coarse claims against counterevidence. Where finer evidence burdens the coarse claim, the burden remains. The no-privilege rule prohibits automatic promotion of detail, not responsible revision.

## 9. Stop, Claim Reduction, Failure, and Non-Capture

### 9.1 Optional Stop

Optional Stop is appropriate where a supported bounded result is complete for the current claim and further distinction would only repeat Neutrality or add detail without purchase.

### 9.2 Mandatory Stop

Mandatory Stop applies before continuation would require:

- distinctions below the Relevance Floor;
- components or relations beyond Source Ceiling;
- nominal parts without reconstructive function;
- retention of source identity after a reference break;
- filling missing information through inference;
- preserving a source type regardless of counterevidence;
- shifting granularity to evade an earlier failure;
- converting unresolved `PROJECT_AS` pressure into SUB;
- person, motive, legitimacy, recommendation, sanction, or authority inference.

### 9.3 Claim Reduction

Claim Reduction is required where a weaker supported finding survives but the original decomposition claim is too strong. Typical cases include:

- only some component relations are supported;
- source type survives but source function does not;
- the coarser function is traceable only in one dimension;
- the Source Ceiling blocks a stronger ordering or causal claim;
- a component is strongly modulating rather than constitutive.

### 9.4 Failure

`failed_transformation` is appropriate where the claimed decomposition cannot preserve source reference, reconstruct component relations, or support any bounded coarser-function result.

### 9.5 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains appropriate where:

- no stable internal relation map can be reconstructed;
- rival decompositions remain equally supported;
- sources leave decisive components unavailable;
- every finer map preserves relevant difference only by destroying the source function;
- type and function revisions cannot be responsibly discriminated.

```text
non_capture
≠ coarse claim confirmed
≠ compromise decomposition invented
≠ missing structure filled
```

## 10. Granularity Escape and Re-entry

A new granularity does not erase a prior counterexample, Failure, Claim Reduction, or Stop. Chapter 27 inherits Chapter 25’s anti-immunization rule:

```text
prior claim burdened or failed
+
new finer reconstruction
→
prior disposition preserved
+
new testable claim required
```

Re-entry after Stop requires a materially new basis, such as new sources, a repaired relation map, a revised bounded claim, or a demonstrably different source object. More compute, more nodes, a different layout, or a lower nominal granularity is insufficient.
