## 5. Path Dependence as a Property

### 5.1 Property identity

Path Dependence qualifies how a present configuration or bounded continuation field depends on its prior Path. It does not create another historical object beside Path and Trajectory.

```text
Trajectory = historically sedimented object
Path Dependence = property of historical indispensability
```

A property claim must name:

- the reference object;
- the present analytical cut;
- the current feature or continuation dimension being tested;
- the prior Path segment claimed to remain indispensable;
- the historical carrier;
- the current-condition baseline;
- the source basis;
- the claim ceiling.

### 5.2 Eligible source objects

A dependence test may begin from:

- a warranted Path where only weak order dependence is claimed;
- a warranted Trajectory where strong Path Dependence is tested;
- a bounded segment of a Trajectory where the property is local rather than whole-object-wide;
- competing warranted Trajectory constructions tested separately.

A chronology, isolated endpoint, macro-label, or failed Trajectory is not a sufficient strong-dependence input.

### 5.3 Property scope

Path Dependence may be claimed for one or more bounded dimensions:

- meaning or interpretive credibility;
- cost or friction;
- role allocation;
- exposure or exit condition;
- expectation or default continuation;
- access, authorization, affordability, coordination feasibility, or reversibility;
- evidentiary burden;
- continuation reachability.

```text
Path Dependence in one dimension
≠ whole-object dependence in every dimension
```

## 6. Weak Order Dependence

Weak order dependence is present where sequence changes a local reconstruction but detailed history is not indispensable to the current configuration as a whole.

Required elements:

- a warranted order relation;
- an identifiable local difference produced by reordering or omission;
- limited present effect;
- low or bounded sedimented load;
- substantial current-state reconstructibility without the full Path;
- no strong reachability or cost claim beyond the sources.

Examples of weak effects may include:

- the same statement carrying different credibility because of one earlier transition;
- a local procedural cost that depends on order but does not structure the broader continuation field;
- a bounded role interpretation that remains revisable without major historical reconstruction;
- a limited delay effect that does not materially constrain later alternatives.

Weak order dependence may be analytically important. It does not become strong merely because the history is long, emotionally salient, institutionally visible, or narratively coherent.

```text
history improves local interpretation
≠ history is indispensable to the present object
```

## 7. Strong Path Dependence

### 7.1 Strong threshold

Strong Path Dependence requires more than a Trajectory and more than historical contribution. It requires that removing, materially altering, or replacing the relevant prior Path would make the current reconstruction materially inadequate.

The minimum burden is conjunctive:

1. warranted Path/Trajectory input;
2. declared present analytical cut;
3. declared dependence dimension;
4. traceable historical carrier;
5. sedimented relation across time;
6. present effect not adequately explained by current conditions alone;
7. Source–Result Dependency under relevant source variation;
8. source-bounded alternative-history or omission test;
9. preserved contingency and non-determinism;
10. claim, source, loss, Stop, and Non-Capture controls.

### 7.2 Historical indispensability

Historical indispensability means:

```text
without the relevant prior Path,
the current meaning, cost, role, credibility,
accessibility, reachability, or continuation field
would be materially misdescribed or underdescribed.
```

It does not mean:

- the present has one cause;
- the prior Path was necessary in all possible worlds;
- the current state could not have arisen otherwise;
- alternatives are impossible now;
- future continuation is determined;
- the Path is morally or legally decisive.

### 7.3 Same or similar present, different history

The contract-required pressure test holds a declared set of present features approximately constant while varying prior Path history.

This comparison must declare:

- which present features are treated as similar;
- which differences remain and are not suppressed;
- whether the cases are actual, reconstructed, or source-bounded counterfactual;
- which prior transition differs;
- which current cost, role, meaning, credibility, or reachability changes;
- whether the comparison remains within source and claim ceilings.

```text
approximately similar present features
≠ identical object
≠ experimental causal isolation
```

### 7.4 Current-state sufficiency challenge

Every strong-dependence claim must face the rival hypothesis:

```text
The current configuration may be adequately reconstructed
from current conditions without detailed Path history.
```

The claim fails or reduces where:

- current rules, resources, roles, prices, constraints, or institutions explain the relevant present effect;
- historical detail changes narrative richness but not discrimination;
- the alleged carrier is no longer active;
- source variation does not alter the result;
- the counterfactual comparison is unsupported.

## 8. Dependence-Bearing Sedimentation

Chapter 11 owns Historical Sedimentation as a Trajectory burden. Chapter 12 asks whether that sedimentation carries enough discriminative load to make history indispensable.

### 8.1 Contribution versus indispensability

| Result | Historical role | Dependence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| background history | context only | no material dependence at tested level |
| local order effect | improves bounded interpretation | weak order dependence |
| sedimented present load | materially co-determines current praxis | Trajectory support; strong dependence still separately tested |
| indispensable historical load | current reconstruction materially fails without prior Path | strong Path Dependence candidate |

### 8.2 Carrier test

A dependence-bearing carrier must be:

- identifiable in source material;
- temporally connected to the prior Path;
- present-bearing at the analytical cut;
- sensitive to material source changes;
- distinguishable from a current-only condition;
- bounded in dimension and scope.

### 8.3 Non-compensation

```text
rich sedimentation narrative
+ no present discriminative difference
= no strong Path Dependence
```

Likewise:

```text
strong current constraint
+ no historical Source–Result Dependency
= current-state constraint, not established Path Dependence
```

## 9. Operator-Combination Load

### 9.1 `Α + Θ` — Attractor dependence

A bounded `Α + Θ` relation may support Path Dependence where repeated prior configurations changed current local friction, default expectation, visibility, or continuation accessibility such that the present cannot be adequately reconstructed from current rules alone.

Required pressure:

- identify the repeated pattern;
- identify the temporal retention mechanism;
- show changed current friction or default accessibility;
- test whether current conditions alone suffice;
- preserve alternative continuation and no inevitability claim.

Failure forms include:

- repetition without changed current accessibility;
- current habit described without source history;
- broad attractor label insensitive to relevant Path variation;
- inferred inevitability.

### 9.2 `Ω + Θ` — Asymmetry dependence

A bounded `Ω + Θ` relation may support Path Dependence where accumulated differential costs, roles, exposure, access, information, capacity, burden, or exit conditions remain indispensable to the present reconstruction.

Required pressure:

- trace the distribution across time;
- distinguish accumulated from current-only Asymmetry;
- show how a different prior distribution would change the present claim;
- avoid person ranking, moral verdict, legitimacy, or sanction inference.

### 9.3 `Ψ + Θ` — Binding dependence

A bounded `Ψ + Θ` relation may support Path Dependence where prior commitments, reliance, coordination investment, institutionalization, breach costs, or reopening conditions materially structure the present.

Required pressure:

- identify concrete commitment or binding occurrences;
- trace retention, transfer, conflict, release, or dormancy;
- distinguish structural Binding from psychological intention or moral obligation;
- test current enforceability/coordination conditions separately;
- preserve possible revision or exit.

### 9.4 `Λ + Θ` — Residual dependence

A bounded `Λ + Θ` relation may support Path Dependence where warranted expectation-bound Non-Events, missed windows, repeated non-decision, delayed repair, or blocked transition leave present-bearing residue.

Required pressure:

- identify the expectation frame;
- identify the bounded realization window;
- establish non-occurrence rather than missing information;
- trace the residue into current costs, roles, credibility, or reachability;
- test later repair, closure, or changed frame;
- avoid turning every absence into historical dependence.

### 9.5 Cross-profile relations

Profiles may interact, for example:

```text
Α + Θ stabilizes a default
Ω + Θ distributes its cost
Ψ + Θ binds reliance to it
Λ + Θ preserves unresolved residue
```

But:

```text
profile interaction
≠ operator fusion
≠ universal causal chain
≠ additive dependence score
```

Each profile retains its own carrier, source basis, current effect, counterpressure, uncertainty, and claim ceiling.

### 9.6 `Θ` alone is insufficient

PMS Base gives `Θ` the capacity to structure sequence, trajectory, and path dependence. STRATA occurrence-level warrant still requires the additional historical carrier and present-load burden.

```text
Θ type capacity
≠ Θ occurrence proves Path Dependence
```

Duration, order, repetition, persistence, and historical narration remain insufficient without traceable discriminative load.

## 10. `Φ` under Path Dependence

### 10.1 Recontextualization changes meaning

A warranted `Φ` occurrence may:

- reinterpret earlier Path material;
- move a historical carrier into a new frame;
- reduce, amplify, redirect, or redistribute its present load;
- create new continuation possibilities;
- expose previously hidden costs or alternatives.

### 10.2 Recontextualization is not reset

A reset claim requires positive evidence that the relevant historical carrier no longer changes current meaning, cost, role, credibility, accessibility, or reachability.

Insufficient reset indicators include:

- a new label;
- formal reorganization;
- declared restart;
- changed narrative;
- renamed role;
- new policy without changed practice;
- physical relocation without changed binding/cost structure;
- formal exit with retained obligations or residue.

```text
new frame
≠ erased Path

recontextualized load
≠ absent load
```

### 10.3 New claim rule

A new frame or recontextualization creates a new testable dependence claim. It does not inherit success or failure automatically.

## 11. `Χ`, `Σ`, and Later `Ψ` as Modifiers

### 11.1 Modifier meaning

Later operator occurrences may alter a Trajectory or its dependence:

- `Χ` may interrupt repetition, create distance, lower reactivity, or reopen alternatives;
- `Σ` may integrate contradictory historical loads into a more workable present configuration;
- a later `Ψ` occurrence may stabilize a revised commitment, release condition, repair, or new self-binding structure.

### 11.2 Modifier is not erasure

A modifier may:

- attenuate dependence;
- redistribute it;
- change its dimension;
- terminate one carrier while leaving residue;
- create partial restoration;
- produce a new segment with different continuation conditions.

It does not automatically:

- reset the Path;
- remove earlier costs;
- erase source lineage;
- convert strong dependence into no dependence;
- legitimate the prior structure;
- authorize a target function.

### 11.3 Occurrence separation

```text
earlier Ψ + Θ Binding Accumulation
≠ later Ψ modifier occurrence
```

The operator type remains the same; occurrence, temporal position, reference, and function differ. Chapter 12 must keep these records separate.

## 12. Path Dependence without Determinism

### 12.1 Non-deterministic dependence

Path Dependence changes the field of costs, meanings, expectations, credibility, accessibility, and reachability. It does not determine one future action.

```text
historically altered continuation field
≠ fixed continuation
```

### 12.2 Alternatives remain possible

Strong Path Dependence may coexist with:

- costly exit;
- difficult but available revision;
- low-probability or low-visibility alternatives;
- new events that open a path;
- repair that attenuates one carrier;
- recontextualization that changes meaning;
- external intervention or resource change;
- local reversal.

### 12.3 Probability boundary

The chapter may use qualitative language such as more accessible, less credible, more costly, or harder to reverse only where supported. It may not infer numerical probabilities without appropriate empirical data and method.

### 12.4 No fate, essence, or person typing

Path Dependence is a property of a bounded historical reconstruction. It is not:

- a trait of a person;
- an institutional essence;
- moral destiny;
- a diagnosis;
- proof of incapacity;
- justification for irreversible labeling or intervention.

## 13. Path-Dependence Test Architecture

### 13.1 Input integrity

Verify:

- warranted Path or Trajectory reference;
- boundary and analytical cut;
- source basis and uncertainty;
- no unresolved upstream Failure that is being laundered.

### 13.2 Tested dimension

Declare exactly what depends on history:

- meaning;
- cost/friction;
- role;
- credibility/expectation;
- access/authorization;
- exposure/exit;
- coordination feasibility;
- reversibility;
- evidentiary burden;
- continuation reachability.

### 13.3 Current-state baseline

Construct the best source-supported current-only explanation:

- current rules;
- current resources;
- current role allocation;
- current institutional structure;
- current material constraints;
- current frame;
- current alternatives.

### 13.4 Historical-omission test

Ask:

```text
What material part of the present reconstruction
becomes wrong, incomplete, or misleading
if the prior Path is omitted?
```

A richer story is not enough. The omission must change a warranted reconstruction.

### 13.5 Source-bounded alternative-history test

Ask:

```text
Would a relevant source-supported change in the prior Path
alter the present meaning, cost, role, credibility,
accessibility, reachability, or continuation claim?
```

The test must state whether the variation is:

- an actual comparator;
- a competing source-supported reconstruction;
- a bounded historical counterfactual;
- underdetermined.

### 13.6 Carrier and profile test

Identify:

- the historical carrier;
- the relevant operator-profile relation;
- its temporal trace;
- its present effect;
- its interaction with current conditions;
- its uncertainty and Loss.

### 13.7 Strength finding

The chapter may record a local finding such as:

- no material historical dependence at the tested level;
- weak order dependence;
- strong Path Dependence candidate;
- warranted strong Path Dependence;
- underdetermined dependence;

These labels remain local diagnostics/property findings. They must map to one of the ten canonical Output Classes when the Record is closed.

## 14. Failure to Establish Path Dependence

A strong Path-Dependence claim fails or reduces where:

- only duration is shown;
- repetition is shown without present effect;
- a Trajectory is present but current reconstruction remains adequately current-state-based;
- historical narration adds coherence but no discrimination;
- the historical carrier is unspecified or source-unsupported;
- current conditions fully account for the tested feature;
- the alternative-history comparison is invented or non-comparable;
- source changes leave the result arbitrarily unchanged;
- the claim depends on missing information treated as `Λ`;
- a modifier or recontextualization is assumed to erase history without a new test;
- the dependence dimension shifts during argument;
- strong dependence is inferred from one dramatic event without sedimented load;
- the claim exceeds source, temporal, or contextual scope.

### 14.1 Claim reduction routes

Possible reductions include:

```text
strong Path Dependence
→ weak order dependence
→ Trajectory without established dependence
→ Path without established Trajectory
→ sequence or chronology
```

Each reduction preserves warranted upstream findings and the failed stronger claim in lineage.

### 14.2 Resolution neutrality

A detailed dependence test may produce no additional praxeological difference. This may map to `resolution_neutral` where the tested current reconstruction remains unchanged and no stronger unsupported claim is retained.

### 14.3 Failed claim is not erased history

```text
failed strong-dependence claim
≠ no history
≠ no Path
≠ no Trajectory
≠ no current constraint
```

## 16. Failure, Stop, and Non-Capture Architecture

### 16.1 Claim reduction

Use `claim_reduction_required` where a strong dependence claim fails but a narrower weak-order, Trajectory, Path, or sequence claim remains testable and supportable.

### 16.2 Mandatory Stop

`mandatory_stop` applies where a known failed, unsupported, or non-traceable dependence claim is used to:

- infer inevitability, destiny, prediction, or impossibility;
- justify a target function;
- authorize intervention, sanction, diagnosis, person typing, legitimacy, or irreversible labeling;
- erase an upstream failed Path or Trajectory claim;
- claim that formal model output proves history;
- continue recursive testing after Source or Claim Ceiling is exceeded.

### 16.3 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where:

- current-condition and historical explanations remain materially rival and source-responsible;
- two dependence-bearing Trajectory constructions remain undecidable;
- relevant alternative history cannot be responsibly reconstructed;
- the historical carrier is plausible but not sufficiently traceable;
- source gaps prevent grading weak versus strong dependence;
- every available simplification destroys a material distinction.

Non-Capture does not protect a weak claim from reduction and does not create authority.

### 16.4 Re-entry

Re-entry requires:

- explicit reference to the earlier Record and result;
- new source, corrected Path/Trajectory input, revised boundary, improved current-state baseline, or narrower tested dimension;
- a new Source–Result test;
- preserved Failure, Stop, Non-Capture, and Loss lineage;
- no inherited output, operation, target function, or authority.
