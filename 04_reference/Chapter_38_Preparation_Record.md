## 6. Scientific Pressure and Counterfactual Architecture

The error taxonomy must remain vulnerable to at least the following pressures:

1. **Type-restoration pressure:** does the claim become coherent when origin type and target function are restored separately?
2. **Context-completion pressure:** does an explicit target context, level, object, temporal window, and claim scope remove the defect?
3. **Relation-marking pressure:** does declaring the cross-level or cross-granular relation convert mixing into a testable bounded claim?
4. **Evidence-transfer pressure:** does micro evidence actually carry the macro relation, or is entailment merely assumed?
5. **Metaphor pressure:** is the phrase explanatory shorthand, analogy, or a formal projection claim?
6. **Rescue pressure:** is a failed claim retained while a genuinely new projection is separately declared?
7. **Person-boundary pressure:** is a configuration or composite function being rewritten as an essence, diagnosis, or stable trait?
8. **Primitive pressure:** is a local target function being inserted into PMS Base or the Δ–Ψ dependency order?
9. **Scope pressure:** do context, population, level, temporal, or object boundaries widen without new warrant?
10. **Temporal pressure:** is an historically formed object flattened into a timeless property?
11. **Loss pressure:** are foregrounding, backgrounding, compression, exclusion, uncertainty, and irrecoverability declared?
12. **Reduction pressure:** can a narrower claim survive without preserving the invalid stronger claim?
13. **Stop pressure:** would continuation require repeated relocation, relabeling, evidence erasure, person typing, primitive creation, or authority inheritance?
14. **Non-Capture pressure:** is the natural-language claim too ambiguous to classify responsibly without inventing its intended formal status?

Counterfactual sensitivity does not prove empirical truth, motive, causality, person type, or legitimacy. It only tests whether the error finding responds to material changes in type, context, level, granularity, scope, time, Loss, or claim form.

## 9. Loss, Preservation, and Failure Continuity

Every Chapter-38 claim retains the canonical Loss structure:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

Projection additionally makes foregrounding and backgrounding explicit. These are descriptions of visibility change, not sixth and seventh Loss fields.

A detected error does not delete:

- the independently warranted source object;
- the source origin type and prior disposition;
- valid lower-level or finer-grained evidence;
- a bounded metaphor or analogy that makes a weaker claim;
- a separately recorded operation;
- a narrower context-bound function that survives.

```text
invalid stronger claim
≠ every source-side statement false
```

The original failure remains attached to the original claim. A changed context, level, granularity, scope, time, operation, evidence packet, or target function creates a new claim and new Loss calculation.
