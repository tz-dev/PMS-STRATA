## 1. Preparation Status and Scope

This record originated as the preparation control for Chapter 21 and now records completed execution through WP4 and provisional lock. It binds the chapter to a controlled comparative architecture for concrete occurrences typed through PMS operators and records that canonical §§21.1–21.12 apply the generic `DECOMPOSE` procedure to Frame, Attractor, Asymmetry, Impulse, and Binding families, including family-specific Failure, Mandatory Stop, Non-Capture, source-function-effect, and Chapter-22 handoff logic. It does not create a standalone case artifact, revise an operator definition, make the five families exhaustive, infer person properties, or lock any part of SUB.

The chapter must answer one governing question:

> How can a concrete operator-typed occurrence be opened into source-supported production and maintenance conditions without treating the operator type itself as a material aggregate?

The prepared answer is bounded:

```text
operator type
≠ operator occurrence

production and maintenance conditions of an occurrence
≠ constituents of the operator type

finer reconstruction of an occurrence
≠ redefinition of PMS Base
```

Chapter 21 is not permitted to ask what `□`, `Α`, `Ω`, `∇`, or `Ψ` are “made of.” It may ask which finer practices, relations, temporalities, non-events, roles, costs, thresholds, commitments, and source-supported conditions produce, sustain, disturb, or fail to sustain one concrete occurrence currently typed through one of those operators.

The chapter remains an application chapter. Generic source entry belongs to Chapter 18, granularity relation to Chapter 19, and the complete operation procedure to Chapter 20. Chapter 21 adds occurrence-family discrimination and tests whether the same generic procedure remains stable across different operator-typed source occurrences.

## 3. Contract Lock

### 3.2 Variation rule

Stable coarse function may coexist with:

- internal variation;
- internal conflict;
- unequal reproduction work;
- changing role occupancy;
- local rule variation;
- component substitution;
- partial failure;
- non-events and residual structure.

Therefore:

```text
stable coarse function
≠ internally homogeneous occurrence
```

The reverse inference is equally prohibited:

```text
internal variation
≠ automatic failure of coarse typing
```

### 3.3 Failure rule

Finer evidence may revise or reject the occurrence typing. Preservation protects the source question and reference lineage, not the answer.

A decomposition fails as an occurrence-family reconstruction when it:

- treats the operator type as an empirical object;
- merely redescribes the operator definition;
- forces every local feature to resemble the coarse type;
- suppresses disturbing or rival components;
- infers person properties from occurrence structure;
- converts a dynamic occurrence into a target function without `PROJECT_AS`;
- or protects the original typing against source-supported counterevidence.

## 5. Occurrence-Typing Architecture

### 5.1 Source-side typing

An occurrence typing is a bounded analytical claim about one source object in one Frame, granularity, temporal scope, and source basis. It is not an assertion that the occurrence possesses an immutable essence.

The record must keep separate:

```text
object category
operator typing
current coarse function
source-function effect after decomposition
```

One object may carry multiple compatible or rival occurrence typings. Chapter 21 must not force an exclusive single type where sources support a layered configuration.

### 5.2 Typing under test

For every family, the chapter must state:

- the bounded source reference;
- the occurrence typing under test;
- the coarse function associated with that typing;
- the finer distinction family;
- the source route;
- the relations expected to matter;
- the no-gain condition;
- the stop condition;
- the possible source-function effects.

### 5.3 Type preservation without typing immunity

The operator type remains unchanged even when the occurrence typing is rejected.

```text
this occurrence is not adequately Frame-typed
≠ Frame operator invalid

this occurrence is not a stable Attractor occurrence
≠ Attractor operator decomposed or rejected
```

The chapter must protect operator identity while permitting occurrence-level revision.

## 6. Family Selection and Comparative Method

The representative family set is:

1. Frame-typed occurrence;
2. Attractor-typed occurrence;
3. Asymmetry-typed occurrence;
4. Impulse-typed occurrence;
5. Binding-typed occurrence.

The first three are mandatory for completion. Impulse and Binding are required by the Structure and provide important edge pressure: Impulse tests temporally extended activation without decomposing `∇`; Binding tests durable commitments without converting `Ψ` into a person trait.

The family comparison uses the same seven questions:

1. What is the concrete source occurrence?
2. What coarse function is under test?
3. Which finer structures are source-supported?
4. Which relations organize those structures?
5. Which temporalities are constitutive?
6. Which internal variations or counterevidence matter?
7. What is the bounded source-function effect?

The comparison must not turn into a common numerical scale, strength score, or operator hierarchy.

## 7. Frame-Typed Occurrence Preparation

### 7.1 Primary question

Not:

> What is `□` made of?

But:

> Which source-supported practices, relations, exclusions, roles, temporal routines, Non-Events, bindings, and stabilized interpretations produce or maintain this concrete Frame-typed occurrence?

### 7.2 Candidate production conditions

A concrete Frame occurrence may be carried through:

- repeated selection practices;
- admission and exclusion rules;
- agenda control;
- role allocation;
- access conditions;
- temporal routines;
- recurring Non-Events;
- institutional commitments;
- stabilized interpretive cues;
- distributed reproduction work.

These are not universal constituents of `□`. They are candidate conditions for one occurrence and must be source-supported.

### 7.3 Frame formation versus Frame function

The chapter must distinguish:

```text
how the occurrence is produced and maintained
≠ what coarse Frame function it performs
```

Production conditions may be heterogeneous while the coarse function remains stable. They may also reveal that the claimed boundary is partial, contested, selectively applied, or unsupported.

### 7.4 Stable function and internal variation

A stable Frame function may coexist with:

- conflict over admission;
- changing role occupants;
- unequal boundary-maintenance work;
- local exceptions;
- failed exclusion attempts;
- counter-practices;
- residual outside claims;
- component substitution.

The chapter must not infer that every local structure is Frame-like merely because the occurrence is coarsely Frame-typed.

### 7.5 Minimal anchor example

The preferred continuity example remains the documented review arrangement used in Chapters 18–20. Chapter 21 should now ask specifically whether the bounded review arrangement is maintained as a Frame occurrence through agenda rules, chair authority, objection entry, closure declarations, revisit windows, follow-up routing, and repeated non-reopening.

The example must preserve two possible outcomes:

- the Frame typing is confirmed or refined despite heterogeneous maintenance;
- finer evidence shows that the claimed boundary is only partial or that another typing carries more of the source load.

## 8. Attractor-Typed Occurrence Preparation

### 8.1 Primary question

> Which repeated configurations, friction differences, expectations, rewards, costs, role repetitions, Non-Events, thresholds, and exit conditions produce or maintain this concrete Attractor-typed occurrence?

### 8.2 Reproduction conditions

A concrete Attractor occurrence may be carried by:

- repeated accessibility of one configuration;
- lower local friction;
- expectation stabilization;
- asymmetric cost distribution;
- repeated role occupation;
- delayed or absent alternatives;
- reinforcement through successful repetition;
- threshold effects;
- increased exit costs.

### 8.3 Dynamic Attractor occurrence

An Attractor occurrence may stabilize not only a state but a recurrent transition form. A representative cycle may include approach, intensification, binding expectation, withdrawal, reframing, and renewed approach.

The chapter may decompose the internal transition sequence and its reproduction conditions. It must not conclude that the cycle is already a higher-level Attractor-function in another context.

```text
dynamic Attractor-typed occurrence
≠ projected Attractor-function
```

A later target-function claim requires `PROJECT_AS` and belongs to RETYPE.

### 8.4 Attractor counterpressure

Repetition alone is insufficient. The chapter must test whether:

- the repeated pattern actually lowers friction or changes reachability;
- alternatives become more costly or less visible;
- the current state can be explained without historical repetition;
- the pattern is only a selected chronology;
- or the supposed Attractor disappears under finer variation.

## 9. Asymmetry-Typed Occurrence Preparation

### 9.1 Primary question

> Which source-supported differences in role, resource, access, exposure, exit cost, information, temporal burden, or responsibility capacity constitute this concrete Asymmetry-typed occurrence, and how are those differences related?

### 9.2 Distributed asymmetry

A coarse Asymmetry occurrence may be carried by many local differences. The chapter must test whether they:

- align in one direction;
- reinforce one another;
- offset one another;
- remain temporally stable;
- affect the same roles or different roles;
- or fail to form one coordinated occurrence.

```text
many local asymmetries
≠ coordinated macro-asymmetry automatically
```

### 9.3 Composition and projection boundary

If local asymmetries must be selected and relationally combined into a new composite object, a separate `COMPOSE` occurrence may be required. If that composite is claimed to perform a bounded function in another context, `PROJECT_AS` is required.

Chapter 21 may reconstruct the internal structure of an already identified Asymmetry occurrence. It may not silently construct a new macro-object or target function.

### 9.4 Counterpressure

Finer evidence may show:

- opposing local gradients;
- role reversals across intervals;
- equalization in one dimension but not another;
- uncoordinated local differences;
- inaccessible cost data;
- or an overstated coarse Asymmetry claim.

The source typing must remain revisable.

## 10. Impulse-Typed Occurrence Preparation

A concrete Impulse occurrence may possess a temporal formation history. The chapter may reconstruct:

- activating differences;
- directing Frames;
- intensifying Non-Events;
- repeated triggers;
- threshold crossings;
- attenuation or redirection;
- transition into a stabilized pattern.

It must preserve:

```text
temporal formation of one Impulse occurrence
≠ composition of the Impulse operator
```

The chapter must also avoid psychological inference. An Impulse-typed occurrence is a structural activation claim, not a diagnosis of desire, motive, temperament, or inner state.

## 11. Binding-Typed Occurrence Preparation

A concrete Binding occurrence may be carried by:

- repeated commitments;
- institutional rules;
- expected continuity;
- shared records;
- role stabilization;
- coordinated reminders;
- costs of breach;
- temporal persistence;
- partial or contradictory commitments.

The finer reconstruction may show that a seemingly unified Binding occurrence is internally unequal, distributed, replaceable, unstable, or partially contradictory.

It must preserve:

```text
Binding-typed occurrence
≠ person property
≠ moral rank
≠ permanent identity
```

A person-level statement such as “this person is bound” or “unbound” exceeds the chapter’s structural authority unless independently grounded and appropriately scoped outside STRATA.

## 12. Stable Function, Variation, and Substitution

Chapter 21 must make three possibilities simultaneously available:

1. different internal configurations sustain the same coarse function;
2. one component may be replaceable without loss of the coarse function;
3. finer evidence may reveal that the coarse function was overstated or wrongly typed.

This produces no simple one-to-one relation between internal components and operator typing.

```text
same coarse function
≠ same internal configuration

changed component
≠ changed occurrence type automatically

stable typing
≠ every component essential
```

The chapter must retain Loss and uncertainty where the exact contribution of a component cannot be isolated.

## 14. Failure, Stop, and Non-Capture

### 14.1 Failed occurrence decomposition

Failure may occur when:

- the source occurrence is not independently identifiable;
- the operator type is treated as a material aggregate;
- the analysis only paraphrases the operator definition;
- components are selected to guarantee the source typing;
- relevant counterevidence is excluded;
- relations are missing;
- source support is insufficient;
- or operation boundaries are crossed.

### 14.2 Mandatory Stop

Mandatory Stop applies when continued family reconstruction would require:

- invented components or relations;
- unsupported person inference;
- operator redefinition;
- hidden `COMPOSE` or `PROJECT_AS`;
- source or Claim Ceiling breach;
- or automatic authority transfer.

### 14.3 Non-Capture

Non-Capture remains available where no stable supported occurrence-family model can be retained. It must preserve known source fragments, rival models, and uncertainty. It may not protect a stronger failed occurrence typing.

```text
non_capture of finer occurrence model
≠ original typing retained by default
```

## 15. Operation-Confusion Boundaries

### 15.1 Occurrence decomposition versus operator decomposition

```text
which practices maintain this Frame occurrence?
→ valid DECOMPOSE question

what parts constitute the Frame operator?
→ category error
```

### 15.2 Occurrence decomposition versus composite decomposition

Chapter 21 may discover that an occurrence is already composite. Detailed component hierarchy, redundancy, substitution architecture, internal conflict, and operator weighting then transfer to Chapter 22 rather than being fully absorbed here.

### 15.3 Dynamic Attractor versus RETYPE

A recurrent transition pattern remains an occurrence-level source object until a separate target context and bounded target function are declared.

### 15.4 Distributed Asymmetry versus new COMPOSE

Opening an already identified Asymmetry occurrence differs from selecting many local asymmetries to form a new composite macro-object.

### 15.5 Binding occurrence versus person typing

Structural commitments and breach costs do not license character, maturity, diagnosis, moral, or identity claims about persons.
