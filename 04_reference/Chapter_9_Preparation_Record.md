## 5. Temporal Position Architecture

### 5.1 Required declaration

A temporal-position claim must state which temporal relation matters for the tested claim. Available forms include:

- analytical instant;
- analytical period;
- relative before/after relation;
- open or closed interval;
- bounded but imprecise interval;
- disputed date or ordering;
- retrospective periodization;
- unresolved temporal edge.

```text
calendar timestamp
≠ temporal position automatically
```

A calendar date may support temporal position, but temporal position is claim-relative. The relevant burden may concern whether an obligation preceded a decision, whether a delay overlapped a changed frame, or whether a non-event persisted past a threshold—not merely what date appears in a source.

### 5.2 Uncertainty and retrospective periodization

Unclear or disputed dating must remain explicit. Retrospective periods are analytical constructions and require declared boundaries and formation reasons.

```text
retrospective period label
≠ historically self-given interval
```

Uncertainty may yield a bounded or provisional claim, Formal Diagnostic, failed transition, or Non-Capture depending on the claim and available sources. It may not be hidden by false precision.

## 6. Order Dependence Architecture

### 6.1 Core distinction

```text
order dependence
≠ mere succession
≠ path dependence
```

Order dependence is a bounded claim that reversing or materially changing the order of declared elements would alter the tested praxis relation. It may concern:

- frame legibility;
- expectation structure;
- costs or exposure;
- binding and commitment;
- available alternatives;
- asymmetry;
- the significance of a later event;
- the transition’s result.

### 6.2 Required counterfactual question

> Would a materially different order, with the relevant elements otherwise held as comparable as the sources permit, alter the warranted transition claim?

A positive answer supports order relevance. It does not establish actual causality, path dependence, inevitability, or a complete counterfactual history.

### 6.3 Narrative-order guard

Source presentation order, document order, interview order, and retrospective narrative order must remain distinct from the claimed historical order.

```text
narrative sequence
≠ temporal order
```

A persuasive narrative cannot repair an unsupported ordering relation.

## 7. Duration, Delay, and Persistence Architecture

### 7.1 Duration

Duration becomes praxeologically relevant where the interval changes the warranted reconstruction of expectation, exposure, cost, binding, accessibility, stabilization, residue, or available alternatives.

```text
metric time
≠ praxeologically relevant duration automatically
```

Equal clock durations may carry different praxis load under different frames. Unequal durations may remain analytically equivalent for a bounded claim where no relevant difference is supported.

### 7.2 Delay

Delay may enter two distinct claim forms:

```text
delay as transition structure
≠ delay as framed non-event
```

- **Delay as transition structure** concerns the temporal spacing or deferred realization connecting configurations and changing costs, expectations, bindings, or alternatives.
- **Delay as framed non-event** additionally requires an expected occurrence, a warranted expectation relation, a bounded realization condition, and a praxeologically relevant non-realization.

The same interval may support both claims only when they are separately declared and tested. Missing information, silence, or elapsed time alone supports neither automatically.

### 7.3 Persistence

```text
persistence
≠ stasis
≠ duration alone
≠ sedimentation automatically
```

Persistence is the continued structural relevance of a frame, residue, cost, binding, asymmetry, expectation, or constraint across changing events or configurations. The surrounding configuration may change while the relevant structure persists. Conversely, an unchanged snapshot does not prove persistence without a supported interval relation.

## 8. Bounded Irreversibility and Temporal Recontextualization

### 8.1 Bounded irreversibility

An irreversibility claim must declare:

- the reference object or relation;
- the frame and temporal scope;
- the restoration, neutralization, or cost-equivalent-return criterion;
- the source basis;
- the residual difference or additional cost;
- the claim ceiling.

```text
bounded irreversibility
≠ metaphysical impossibility
≠ total loss
≠ permanence under every future condition
```

Repetition is not necessarily return, exit is not necessarily reset, and formal reversal is not necessarily cost-equivalent restoration.

### 8.2 Temporal recontextualization

Later events or frames may alter how earlier structures are read and what claims they can support. The earlier occurrence and sequence remain part of the historical trace.

```text
temporal recontextualization
≠ retroactive erasure
```

Chapter 9 must also preserve three distinctions:

```text
temporal recontextualization as historical relation
≠ Φ operator type
≠ PROJECT_AS target function
```

Where a later frame is itself treated as a PMS `Φ` occurrence or an origin-typed object is assigned a new bounded function through `PROJECT_AS`, those are separate claims and records.

## 9. Transition Preconditions

A transition candidate requires at minimum:

1. at least two reconstructible configurations;
2. a relevant temporal relation;
3. a declared reference object and comparison basis;
4. a determinable change object or retained structure;
5. separately representable events and non-events where applicable;
6. a declared frame and temporal scope;
7. sufficient Constitutive Source Trace for the claim;
8. explicit changed or retained praxis conditions;
9. known gaps, intermediates, and uncertainty;
10. a bounded claim and claim ceiling.

The preconditions are conjunctive for the declared transition claim. Detail, chronology, utility, or record completion cannot compensate for a failed load-bearing precondition.

```text
precondition completeness
≠ transition truth automatically
```

A precondition may be present but insufficiently supported. The record must distinguish declaration from support.


### 9.1 Comparison basis and retained dimensions

A transition does not require every field of the source and target configurations to differ. It requires a declared comparison basis showing which dimensions changed, which remained materially stable, and why that pattern matters to the tested claim.

At minimum, drafting must distinguish:

```text
changed field
≠ constitutive transition field automatically

retained field
≠ irrelevant field automatically
```

A retained frame may make a change comparable. A retained binding may be the very reason that a later cost change matters. Conversely, a visibly changed field may remain incidental to the transition claim. The relation between endpoint descriptions must therefore be reconstructible rather than inferred from the amount of difference.

A responsible comparison statement should answer:

- Which reference object or relation is followed across the configurations?
- Which frame and scope make the comparison admissible?
- Which changed dimension is constitutive for the transition claim?
- Which retained dimension supplies continuity or comparison control?
- Which dimensions remain unknown or incomparable?
- Which claim would change if a proposed constitutive difference were removed?

### 9.2 Intermediate-structure burden

Chapter 9 does not require exhaustive reconstruction of every moment between configurations. It does require enough intermediate structure to prevent an endpoint story from carrying more load than the sources support.

```text
non-exhaustive transition trace
≠ endpoint-only transition claim
```

An intermediate event, non-event, delay, frame change, obligation, branch, or residue becomes mandatory where omitting it changes the warranted relation between the configurations. Where intermediates are known to exist but cannot be reconstructed, the record must state whether the transition remains boundedly supportable, requires Claim Reduction, stays formal-diagnostic, fails, or enters responsible Non-Capture.

The Source and Claim Ceiling govern this burden. A narrow transition claim may survive an unresolved minor interval. A strong claim about intention, causality, irreversibility, or unavailable alternatives may not.

### 9.3 Transition support and result-axis discipline

Chapter 9 must preserve the Chapter 7 axis separation:

```text
source support
≠ transition-test result
≠ claim disposition
≠ routing state
≠ canonical Output Class
```

For example, both endpoint configurations may be strongly supported while the relation between them remains underdetermined. A temporal order may be supported while the structural transition claim fails. A transition candidate may remain formal-diagnostic because an unmarked frame change blocks comparison. None of these states should be compressed into an undifferentiated “uncertain transition.”

A routed result requires a delimited claim. If the record mixes temporal order, transition, causal explanation, path formation, and target function, the claims must be segmented before routing. Formal Diagnostic remains available where operation identity, frame comparability, temporal order, or transition reference is not yet sufficiently declared.

### 9.4 Chapter 10 handoff discipline

The Chapter 9 handoff consists of warranted transition objects, failed transition records, rival transition candidates, and preserved weaker temporal claims. It does not contain a path result.

```text
warranted transition set
≠ path automatically
```

Chapter 10 must still test selection, connectedness, realized traversal, branch and omission treatment, and path-specific loss. Do not yet compose the transitions into a path.

## 11. Transition Failure, Stop, and Non-Capture

### 11.1 Failure conditions

A transition claim fails or requires reduction where:

- only two isolated snapshots are available;
- temporal order is unknown and indispensable;
- change is asserted without a determinable change object;
- relevant intermediates are omitted beyond the claim ceiling;
- the frame changes without declaration or comparability rationale;
- events and missing information are conflated;
- a delay is treated as a non-event without a supported expectation frame;
- persistence is inferred from one observation;
- irreversibility is asserted absolutely;
- later recontextualization is used to erase earlier structure;
- source presentation order substitutes for historical order.

### 11.2 Weaker claims preserved

A failed transition may preserve:

- two separately supported configurations;
- a bounded before/after relation;
- an unresolved chronology;
- a candidate change dimension;
- a Formal Diagnostic;
- rival transition reconstructions;
- a re-entry condition.

```text
failed transition claim
≠ erased source objects
≠ no temporal information
```

### 11.3 Stop

`mandatory_stop` is required when analysis continues to compose a path, causal chain, trajectory, or path-dependence claim from a known failed load-bearing transition without repair or segmentation.

Optional Stop remains available where further temporal subdivision or transition search would add no PraxisPurchase or exceed TraceableLoad.

### 11.4 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available when two or more responsible transition reconstructions remain materially rivalrous and available sources cannot select among them without false precision, category collapse, or source overreach.

```text
unknown order
≠ non_capture automatically
```

Unknown order may instead support a Formal Diagnostic, bounded claim, failed transition, or Claim Reduction. Non-Capture requires a claim-relative limit after responsible rival reconstruction.
