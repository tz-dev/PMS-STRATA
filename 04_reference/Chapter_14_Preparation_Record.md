## 5. Why `Λ` Is Central to PATH

Paths and Trajectories are not composed only of realized events. A source-supported non-realization can:

- leave a transition incomplete;
- preserve an earlier configuration;
- shift costs or exposure;
- narrow or hold open alternatives;
- accumulate residue;
- alter later meaning;
- change which continuations remain reachable;
- become sedimented historical load.

The chapter must nevertheless keep the stronger threshold:

```text
nothing happened
≠ path-forming Non-Event
```

The PATH claim must state what was expected, why that expectation was warranted, when realization was due, how non-realization is supported, and what difference the non-realization made.

## 6. Expectation Frame and Expected Window

### 6.1 Expectation relation

A warranted expectation may arise from:

- an explicit commitment;
- a rule or procedure;
- a schedule or deadline;
- a declared role responsibility;
- a recurrent institutional practice;
- an adopted plan;
- a triggered review, repair, release, or exit condition;
- another source-supported relation that makes realization due or conditionally due.

It may not be inferred from analyst preference, moral intuition, hindsight, statistical likelihood alone, or the mere desirability of an occurrence.

### 6.2 Expectation frame

The record must identify the frame in which the expectation operates. Institutional, procedural, relational, contractual, or other frames may support different expected occurrences and windows. A frame shift may terminate, replace, translate, or leave uncertain the earlier expectation; it does not silently preserve it.

### 6.3 Expected window

The expected window may be:

- a fixed interval;
- a deadline;
- a recurring decision window;
- a condition-triggered interval;
- a bounded sequence position;
- another source-supported realization condition.

An open-ended possibility is insufficient. A window still open at the analytical cut does not yet support completed non-realization.

```text
not yet realized
≠ failed to realize within the expected window
```

## 7. Source-Supported Non-Realization

The source basis must support non-realization within the declared bound. Evidence may include complete decision logs, calendars, disposition records, signed completion records, verified absence from an otherwise complete event register, or another source structure capable of establishing bounded non-occurrence.

The following remain insufficient by themselves:

- no document found;
- an incomplete archive;
- silence in one communication channel;
- later recollection without supporting chronology;
- endpoint difference;
- an analyst's inability to identify the event.

```text
no record found
≠ record of non-occurrence
```

Where the source bundle cannot distinguish non-realization from missing information, the result must reduce, stop, or remain `non_capture` if the unresolved distinction itself is materially structured.

## 8. Delay as Non-Event

Delay may qualify as `Λ` where an expected transition does not occur within a warranted window and the non-realization changes costs, alternatives, roles, bindings, residues, or later accessibility.

The preparation must distinguish:

```text
Delay as Non-Event
≠ observed sequence of postponement events
```

The same interval may contain positive events—rescheduling messages, reminders, partial approvals, interim work—while the governing expected transition remains unrealized.

Required delay declarations include:

- expected occurrence;
- original expected window;
- whether the window remained open, was extended, or closed;
- positive sub-events;
- source support for non-realization;
- accumulated load;
- changed alternatives or later reachability;
- uncertainty and Claim Ceiling.

Intentionality is not required and may not be inferred.

## 9. Repeated Non-Decision

Repeated Non-Decision requires recurring or renewed decision contexts in which a source-supported expected decision repeatedly fails to occur. It is not established by a long period with no visible decision record.

The record must distinguish:

- one prolonged unresolved window;
- several renewed decision windows;
- explicit refusal;
- deferral;
- blockage;
- missing records;
- repeated non-realization.

Repeated Non-Decision may increase coordination load, stabilize expectations of continued non-resolution, reinforce attractor effects, intensify asymmetry, or reduce later alternatives. Those effects require separate support.

```text
repeated Non-Decision
≠ repeated refusal
≠ repeated missing documentation
```

## 10. Blocked Responsibility

Blocked Responsibility concerns an expected action or decision that remains unrealized because responsibility is distributed, cyclically referred, procedurally blocked, mutually conditional, or otherwise structurally prevented from resolving.

The chapter reconstructs:

- the expected occurrence;
- the role or responsibility architecture;
- the blocking relation;
- the expected window;
- the supported non-realization;
- affected roles and alternatives;
- accumulated load;
- uncertainty.

It does not infer:

- individual motive;
- bad faith;
- moral fault;
- legal duty;
- diagnostic incapacity;
- legitimacy or sanction.

```text
structurally blocked responsibility
≠ person blame
```

An independently supported refusal remains a different claim.

## 11. Missing Repair

Missing Repair is a Non-Event candidate where an expected integration, correction, review, remediation, reconciliation, or restoration does not occur within a warranted frame and window, and the non-realization leaves material residue or changes later praxis.

The expectation must be independently grounded. A repair desired by the analyst, affected party, or later reviewer is not enough unless a rule, commitment, role, procedure, adopted plan, or other supported relation made repair due.

Possible effects include:

- preserved incompatibility;
- unresolved evidentiary or procedural residue;
- increased binding or switching costs;
- intensified asymmetry;
- altered interpretation of later events;
- closure of later repair windows.

```text
harm or defect observed
≠ expected repair established
```

---

## 12. Missing Exit

Missing Exit requires more than the formal availability of an exit. The analysis must identify an expected, triggered, announced, contractually due, procedurally required, or otherwise warranted exit occurrence or release process and a bounded realization condition.

The chapter may reconstruct how attractors, asymmetrical costs, bindings, authorization barriers, dependencies, or procedural failures contribute to the non-realization. It may not infer why a person did not leave, whether they truly wanted to leave, or whether remaining was voluntary, rational, legitimate, or coerced without independent support.

```text
formal exit exists
≠ exit expected

exit did not occur
≠ motive known
```

Where only practical difficulty is supported, the claim must remain at that level rather than be upgraded to `Λ`.

## 13. Non-Event Sedimentation

Non-Event Sedimentation occurs where one or more warranted `Λ` occurrences remain structurally active in later meaning, cost, role distribution, alternatives, bindings, residue, or action corridors.

The preparation must distinguish:

```text
repetition
≠ sedimentation automatically

long duration
≠ trajectory-forming load automatically
```

The chapter must declare:

- occurrence boundaries or renewed windows;
- what persists, accumulates, or changes;
- which later configurations or transitions carry the load;
- whether the structure is one prolonged Non-Event, repeated occurrences, or a mixed formation;
- possible `Α + Θ`, `Ω + Θ`, `Ψ + Θ`, or `Λ + Θ` interactions without fusion;
- competing constructions and uncertainty;
- Loss and Claim Ceiling.

A sedimented Non-Event may be trajectory-relevant without establishing strong Path Dependence.

## 15. False Non-Event

A claimed Non-Event fails where:

- no identifiable expected occurrence existed;
- no warranted expectation relation is supported;
- no bounded realization condition or window existed;
- the source basis cannot establish non-realization;
- the expectation was created retrospectively;
- the alleged absence creates no praxis-relevant difference;
- the occurrence remained merely possible rather than expected;
- an open window is treated as already failed;
- a branch status or missing record is relabelled as `Λ`.

Failure does not prove that the expected event occurred. It proves only that the stronger Non-Event claim is unwarranted under the present record.

```text
failed Non-Event claim
≠ positive-event proof
```

`false Non-Event` remains a local failure description mapped to canonical Output Classes, not an eleventh class.

## 17. Failure, Claim Reduction, Stop, and Non-Capture

### 17.1 Failure and reduction ladder

A strong Non-Event claim may reduce through:

```text
PATH-forming or sedimented Non-Event
→ bounded local Non-Event
→ expected occurrence with unresolved realization status
→ ordinary absence or delay event chain
→ source gap or no Non-Event claim
```

Reduction must preserve any warranted weaker finding, including:

- documented expectation without supported non-realization;
- supported delay events without `Λ` status;
- practical exit difficulty without expected exit;
- unresolved repair issue without warranted repair duty;
- repeated decision contexts without confirmed repeated non-realization;
- source incompleteness.

### 17.2 Mandatory Stop

`mandatory_stop` applies where a known unsupported `Λ` claim is reused to:

- infer hidden intention or refusal;
- assign blame, guilt, diagnosis, coercion, legitimacy, or sanction;
- establish causal necessity or prediction;
- convert missing sources into non-occurrence;
- erase source uncertainty through a graph, label, frame, granularity, or record completion;
- claim completed `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, or `PROJECT_AS`;
- assign a target function or application authority.

### 17.3 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where the material expectation/non-realization problem is positively supported but sources cannot responsibly distinguish, for example:

- non-realization from missing records;
- one prolonged Non-Event from repeated occurrences;
- Non-Decision from blockage;
- failed repair from undefined repair expectation;
- non-exit from unresolved exit expectation;
- later realization from a newly constituted occurrence;
- sedimentation from merely repeated but non-cumulative absence.

Non-Capture may not protect a weak claim from reduction where the record supports only ordinary absence or missing information.
