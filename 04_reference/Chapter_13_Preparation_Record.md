## 5. Why PATH Must Include Alternatives

A realized Path is not adequately reconstructed merely by listing what occurred. Selection becomes visible only relative to source-supported continuations that were available, prepared, contested, rejected, blocked, deferred, aborted, or later lost.

The chapter must prevent retrospective linearization:

```text
realized continuation
≠ necessary continuation
```

The alternative field remains selective. Chapter 13 does not require exhaustive preservation of every conceivable possibility. It requires preservation of alternatives material to the Path claim and supported by the historical source basis.

---

## 6. Branch Point and Historical Availability

A branch point requires more than hindsight. At the historical cut, at least two continuations must be source-supported as practically available, conditionally available, prepared, or actively contested.

Required declarations:

- branch-point configuration;
- frame and temporal window;
- candidate continuation;
- required resources or permissions;
- expected costs and exposure;
- binding consequences;
- source support;
- uncertainty;
- later reachability.

Availability must be evaluated at the earlier cut, not from later success or failure.

```text
possible in principle
≠ available in practice
```

---

## 7. Realized and Rejected Branches

### 7.1 Realized branch

The realized branch is the actually traversed continuation. Selection may be explicit, implicit, distributed, or produced by a Non-Event. Realization does not establish rationality, optimality, legitimacy, or inevitability.

### 7.2 Rejected branch

A rejected branch requires source-supported non-selection or refusal while the continuation remained materially available at the relevant cut. Rejection may leave expectations, costs, records, conflict, or later recontextualization pressure.

```text
not realized
≠ rejected automatically
```

---

## 8. Blocked Branch

A blocked branch was materially available or prepared but prevented by an identifiable blocking structure such as:

- frame exclusion;
- resource denial;
- authorization barrier;
- asymmetrical cost or exposure;
- binding constraint;
- deadline or temporal closure;
- coordinated obstruction;
- source-supported Non-Event.

The burden is to distinguish prevention from impossibility and rejection.

```text
blocked
≠ impossible
≠ rejected
```

---

## 9. Aborted Branch

An aborted branch must have begun. Evidence must show traversal, commitment, resource use, role change, expectation formation, or another source-supported initiation trace followed by interruption.

Abortion may leave:

- sunk cost;
- residual expectation;
- altered credibility;
- partial infrastructure;
- changed access;
- later path dependence.

```text
aborted branch
≠ branch never begun
```

---

## 10. Deferred Branch

A deferred branch remains potentially continuable but is shifted beyond its initial window. Delay is part of the Path and may change:

- frame;
- costs;
- available actors or resources;
- binding structure;
- exposure;
- meaning;
- later reachability.

A later continuation must not be represented as if uninterrupted.

```text
deferred continuation
≠ same continuation at a later timestamp automatically
```

---

## 11. Lost Alternative

A lost alternative was source-supported as reachable earlier and later became unavailable or materially unreachable. Loss may result through `Θ`, `Ω`, `Ψ`, `Λ`, frame change, resource change, or accumulated selection.

Required burdens:

- earlier availability;
- historical window;
- mechanism or structure of loss;
- later unavailability;
- source support for both cuts;
- residual effect;
- uncertainty.

```text
lost alternative
≠ option currently disliked, costly, or unlikely
```

---

## 12. Source-Bounded Counterfactual Path

A counterfactual Path is a bounded hypothetical continuation derived from a source-supported earlier alternative. It is used to inspect selection, Path Dependence, and Loss. It is not used to prove what would have happened.

Minimum restrictions:

- start from a documented branch point;
- vary only declared source-supported conditions;
- retain known constraints and uncertainties;
- stop where sources no longer support continuation;
- prohibit prediction, causal certainty, and narrative completion.

```text
counterfactual Path
≠ alternate-history fiction
≠ forecast
≠ causal proof
```

## 13. Non-Selection as Praxis Structure

Non-selection may shape a Path where a decision context, availability window, expectation structure, and consequence are source-supported. It may occur through silence, postponement, repeated deferral, failure to authorize, or failure to close.

The chapter must distinguish:

```text
non-selection under an active decision context
≠ no evidence that a decision context existed
```

## 16. Failure, Claim Reduction, Stop, and Non-Capture

### 16.1 Failure and reduction ladder

Where a claimed status is not supported, the claim may reduce as follows:

```text
lost alternative
→ earlier available but later reachability unresolved
→ candidate continuation with incomplete availability support
→ no warranted alternative claim
```

```text
blocked branch
→ non-realized continuation with unresolved cause
→ no warranted branch-status claim
```

```text
aborted branch
→ attempted or prepared continuation
→ candidate continuation
→ no warranted alternative claim
```

Each retained claim requires its own burden.

### 16.2 Mandatory Stop

`mandatory_stop` applies where:

- a known unsupported alternative is used as if historically available;
- a counterfactual is extended beyond source support;
- blocked is used to imply impossible, blame, legitimacy, or sanction;
- a lost-alternative claim is used for prediction or irreversible decision;
- a graph or Reader rendering hides uncertainty or presents one branch as factual;
- a failed branch claim is laundered through new frame, granularity, operation, or target label.

### 16.3 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where:

- sources support multiple branch statuses but cannot distinguish them;
- the decision window is unclear;
- availability depended on inaccessible private, institutional, or technical conditions;
- rejected and blocked remain equally source-responsible;
- later reachability cannot be established;
- every reconstruction destroys decisive heterogeneity.

Non-Capture does not protect a preferred story.
