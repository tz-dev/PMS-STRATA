## 6. Scientific Hardening Controls

### 6.1 Independent source selection and anti-retrospective motif construction

The trajectories and comparison dimensions must be warranted before the desired attractor result is selected. Later recurrence may test the candidate source; it may not retroactively decide which earlier trajectories, phases, or differences count as the recurrent form.

### 6.2 Comparability before recurrence

Multiple trajectories are not comparable merely because they share a label or visual sequence. Frame, granularity, roles, temporal scope, evidence quality, relevant transition structure, and operator weighting must be declared. Incomparability is a positive result, not missing data to be silently averaged away.

### 6.3 Constitutive recurrence versus surface resemblance

The Record must distinguish:

- constitutive transitions or relations;
- phases that may vary without breaking the form;
- background similarities;
- uncertain correspondences;
- incompatible or opposite sequences.

A recurrent form must remain discriminating enough to fail.

### 6.4 Mechanism and temporal direction

An attractor-function requires more than repeated appearance. The analysis must identify source-traceable path influence such as expectation stabilization, friction reduction, role reproduction, recurrent Non-Events, repeated repair/exit structure, or rising alternative cost. Earlier recurrence must be temporally available to structure later paths; simultaneous or independently regenerated similarity is insufficient.

### 6.5 Pattern Threshold without universal count

Thresholds remain relational to source quality, comparability, mechanism, target effect, and Claim Scope. A small source set may support only a provisional claim; a large set may remain inadmissible where selection, comparability, or mechanism fails. No recurrence count becomes a universal gate.

### 6.6 Dynamic versus static target function

```text
static attractor-function
→ stabilizes a configuration or state range

dynamic attractor-function
→ stabilizes a transition form or sequence
```

The claim must identify which function is tested. A recurring endpoint alone does not establish a dynamic attractor; a recurring sequence does not establish a static state attractor.

### 6.7 Anti-determinism and probability discipline

A bounded attractor-function may alter friction, expectation, accessibility, or cost without determining later paths. Probability claims require independent data, calibration, and claim authority not supplied by STRATA. The family method remains structural and source-bound.

### 6.8 Sampling, selection, and survivorship pressure

The analysis must retain absent, failed, truncated, divergent, and non-recurring trajectories where the source basis makes them relevant. Selecting only completed or salient repetitions can fabricate a recurrent form. Missing information remains distinct from a Non-Event and from evidence of non-recurrence.

## 7. Section Architecture

### 33.1 Basic Claim

Establish the bounded recurrent-form-to-attractor-function family claim while preserving the source as a derived recurrent form and the target as a contextual function.

### 33.2 Single Trajectory versus Recurrent Form

Require multiple independently warranted and sufficiently comparable trajectories. Reject the universalization of one path and distinguish source formation from later projection.

### 33.3 Dynamic Attractor

Specify how a transition form rather than a static configuration may be stabilized, without converting the recurrent form into Α or implying determinism.

### 33.4 Pattern Threshold

Require context-sensitive comparability, constitutive recurrence, mechanism, and later-path effect. Reject universal recurrence counts and purely formal thresholds.

### 33.5 Constitutive Repetition

Identify constitutive phases, variable phases, recurring roles, Non-Events, costs, frame conditions, and allowable deviations.

### 33.6 Attractor Load

Specify source-traceable target work: lower friction, stabilized expectation, role repetition, recurring exit/repair structure, or increased alternative cost.

### 33.7 Dynamic versus Static Attractor-Function

Keep state-stabilizing and transition-form-stabilizing functions separate in source trace, target difference, Validity Scope, and result.

### 33.8 Recurrent Form versus Retrospective Similarity

Reject label similarity, selective cases, visual alignment, narrative motifs, and after-the-fact phase selection as sufficient recurrence or mechanism evidence.

### 33.9 Counterfactual Attractor Test

Vary constitutive phases, frames, roles, costs, source selection, comparison coordinates, and target contexts. Test whether the candidate function weakens, changes, or disappears.

### 33.10 Failed Attractor Projection

Represent failure where source recurrence, comparability, mechanism, target effect, discrimination, sensitivity, or scope cannot be sustained without elasticity, determinism, or source erasure.
