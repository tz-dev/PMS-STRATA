## 1. Preparation Status and Scope

This record prepares Chapter 24 as the primary SUB site for opening PATH-produced analytical objects under finer resolution. Its source objects are not raw chronologies. They are already formed and bounded Path-side objects: a Path, subpath, Trajectory, phase, turning-point claim, branch cluster, or path-dependence claim whose coarser function was established or provisionally carried by earlier PATH work.

The governing question is:

> When is a finer temporal reconstruction a `DECOMPOSE` occurrence applied to the same Path or Trajectory, and when does the new selection, periodization, or relation set instead constitute a rival `COMPOSE` result?

The bounded Chapter-24 architecture is:

```text
independently warranted PATH-produced source object
+
explicit source reference, boundary, category, selection/formation rule, and coarser function
+
source-supported subpaths, transition clusters, turning-point relations, branches, frame changes, continuations, and historical loads
+
Same-Reference and operation-classification test
+
inherited and new Loss
→
Path/Trajectory DECOMPOSE candidate
```

Chapter 24 must resist two symmetrical errors:

```text
more chronology, new periodization, or broader selection
→ DECOMPOSE automatically

same source label retained
→ same Path or Trajectory automatically
```

The first conceals a new PATH construction inside SUB. The second protects a coarse label after its constitutive selection, temporal boundary, transition relations, or historical function has changed. A valid decomposition may reveal previously compressed subpaths and branches while preserving the same source object. It may also show that a purported finer map is actually a rival Path composition and therefore requires a separate `COMPOSE` occurrence.

This Preparation Gate produces no canonical §24 prose, standalone Case, Transformation Record, Smoke Record, appendix expansion, schema extension, new operation, new Output Class, new Rule, new audit stage, Path ontology, periodization enum, turning-point score, path-dependence score, person type, prediction engine, or authority mechanism.

## 3. Contract Lock

### 3.1 Required core claim

Chapter 24 must establish:

> A Path or Trajectory may be decomposed into source-supported subpaths, transition clusters, turning-point relations, branches, internal Frame changes, competing continuations, and historical dependence loads only while the source reference, coarser function, and original selection/formation commitments remain explicit and testable.

The source object is not immune to finer evidence. A purported Trajectory may reduce to a Path. A claimed turning point may disappear. A supposedly linear path may reveal parallel or competing subpaths. A Path-Dependence claim may weaken when present conditions prove sufficient. A new periodization may be useful but belong to a rival `COMPOSE` result rather than to the current `DECOMPOSE` occurrence.

### 3.2 Same-Path rule

A finer reconstruction remains a candidate decomposition of the same Path only where it preserves or explicitly revises:

- the historical reference;
- the source boundary and temporal scope;
- the Path's selection and formation rule;
- the constitutive transitions and relation structure;
- the role of relevant Non-Events and alternatives;
- the coarser Path or Trajectory function under test;
- the relation between finer components and the same source claim.

Same label, overlapping dates, shared endpoint, or common topic are insufficient.

### 3.3 Rival-PATH rule

A proposed finer reconstruction pressures classification as a new PATH construction where it:

- selects materially different source configurations or events;
- introduces a new beginning or endpoint that changes the object;
- changes the governing Frame rather than merely reconstructing an internal Frame transition;
- applies a materially different formation or relevance rule;
- turns excluded alternatives into constitutive segments;
- creates a new macro-object from formerly independent paths;
- produces a different historical referent or target claim.

Such a construction may be admissible, but it requires its own `COMPOSE` occurrence, Loss declaration, and claim.

### 3.4 Non-inverse rule

```text
SUB(PATH(X))
≠ X
```

Chapter 24 cannot recover a complete original history because the source Path was already selective and compressive. The original `COMPOSE` may have excluded local transitions, collapsed parallel processes, lost uncertainty, removed alternatives, or made some distinctions irrecoverable. Later evidence may add structure unavailable at formation time, but this does not make the decomposition an inverse operation.

## 6. Same-Reference and Formation-Lineage Test

### 6.1 Formation lineage

A Path source inherits an original `COMPOSE` lineage. Chapter 24 must retain:

- which source objects were selected;
- why they were selected;
- how they were ordered or partially ordered;
- which relations made the Path more than chronology;
- what was preserved, compressed, excluded, uncertain, and irrecoverable;
- which alternatives were included or left outside the object.

Finer evidence may challenge that lineage. It may not disappear from the Record.

### 6.2 Same-reference indicators

Evidence supporting the same source object may include:

- unchanged historical process reference;
- compatible temporal boundary;
- preserved central transition chain;
- preserved endpoint or open-continuation relation;
- finer components that explain rather than replace the coarse function;
- explicit accounting for newly visible branches and Loss.

No single indicator is sufficient. The test is relational and claim-bound.

### 6.3 Reference-break pressure

A reference break is pressured where the finer map:

- centers a different process;
- adopts a different temporal beginning that changes the formation story;
- replaces the original transition chain;
- changes the unit from one institutional path to a network of independent paths;
- removes the constitutive Non-Events or alternatives;
- retains only the label while changing the object.

Where reference breaks, continuation under the current `DECOMPOSE` claim must stop. A rival PATH construction may be opened separately.

## 7. Subpaths and Transition Clusters

### 7.1 Subpath rule

A subpath is not any temporally bounded fragment. It must retain:

- a relation to the same coarse Path;
- a bounded internal source and endpoint or open continuation;
- transitions carrying part of the coarse function;
- a declared relation to parallel, sequential, competing, interrupted, or differently paced subpaths;
- source support and Loss.

A fragment that cannot be related to the coarser object remains a detail, an unresolved remainder, or a candidate source for another operation.

### 7.2 Possible subpath relations

Subpaths may be:

- sequential;
- parallel;
- partially overlapping;
- competing;
- mutually constraining;
- interrupted and resumed;
- differently paced;
- locally reversible while the coarse Path remains irreversible;
- carried by different roles or institutions.

The analysis must not force these relations into one linear chain for readability.

### 7.3 Transition clusters

A coarse transition may be opened into:

```text
configuration A
→ intermediate configuration or transition cluster
→ configuration B
```

A transition cluster can include local events, Non-Events, role transfers, thresholds, partial closures, and parallel changes. It is admissible only where the intermediate structure changes or substantiates a warranted claim about the coarse transition.

### 7.4 Transition-cluster counterpressure

If the proposed cluster merely lists more events without clarifying how configuration A became configuration B, it falls below the Relevance Floor. If the cluster selects independent transitions into a new macro-transition, a separate `COMPOSE` occurrence may be required.

## 8. Turning Points

### 8.1 Turning-point burden

A turning point is a claim about a source-supported transition or transition cluster that materially changes later possibilities, costs, bindings, Frame legibility, or reachability. It cannot be established solely because later analysis finds the moment narratively salient.

The burden includes:

- component transitions;
- contemporaneous or structurally traceable effects;
- changed alternatives or action corridors;
- changed costs, asymmetries, or bindings;
- temporal boundary and rival candidates;
- counterfactual sensitivity under source-bounded alternatives;
- uncertainty and Loss.

### 8.2 Retrospective-label guard

```text
later importance
≠ turning point already established
```

A moment may become symbolically central only after later events. Chapter 24 must distinguish later recontextualization from a transition that was already structurally consequential at the tested time.

### 8.3 Turning-point revision

Finer reconstruction may:

- confirm one turning point;
- distribute the change across a cluster;
- relocate the relevant threshold;
- identify several dimension-specific turning points;
- reduce the claim to a bounded local shift;
- reject the turning-point claim.

These are local findings, not canonical Output Classes.

## 9. Branch Reconstruction and Competing Continuations

### 9.1 Branch-status preservation

Chapter 24 may reconstruct realized, rejected, blocked, aborted, deferred, and lost continuations. Their status must remain tied to the historical window and source support. A branch is not real merely because it is imaginable from the present.

### 9.2 Source-bounded counterfactuals

Unrealized alternatives require evidence that they were structurally available at the relevant time. The operation may use bounded counterfactual pressure to test turning points and Path Dependence, but it may not invent free alternative histories.

### 9.3 Competing continuations

An open Trajectory may contain several supported continuations with unequal:

- accessibility;
- cost;
- exposure;
- institutional support;
- role capacity;
- binding load;
- temporal window.

Making these continuations visible is not prediction, recommendation, or probability assignment. Chapter 24 reconstructs the available corridor structure at the analytical cut.

### 9.4 Non-linearity preservation

Finer reconstruction must preserve:

- temporary reversals;
- local repair;
- counter-trends;
- pauses and resumptions;
- branches that later converge;
- branches that share an endpoint but carry different residue;
- parallel developments with different pacing.

A clean direction may be a property of the coarse claim, not a license to erase internal non-linearity.

## 10. Internal Frame Changes

### 10.1 Internal change versus source replacement

A Path or Trajectory may cross several Frames while retaining historical reference. Chapter 24 must distinguish:

```text
Frame changes inside the same Path
≠ analysis changes to a different source object
```

The distinction depends on what persists across the change: reference, roles, transition lineage, unresolved residue, bindings, and the relation to the coarser function.

### 10.2 Required reconstruction

Internal Frame analysis should identify:

- prior and later Frames;
- transition or recontextualization relation;
- structures that persist;
- structures that become newly visible or irrelevant;
- changed operator-occurrence weightings without altering PMS operators;
- changed alternatives and role access;
- effect on source identity and Path function.

### 10.3 Operation boundary

A Frame change does not automatically produce `PROJECT_AS`. A claim that the completed Path performs a contextual function in a new target context belongs to RETYPE. A claim that several Frames and transitions form a new Path may require `COMPOSE`.

## 11. Irrecoverable Compression and Loss

### 11.1 Three Loss positions

Chapter 24 must distinguish:

1. **Inherited `COMPOSE` Loss** — information already compressed, excluded, uncertain, or made irrecoverable when the PATH object was formed.
2. **Recovered or newly sourced detail** — finer structure now available through additional evidence or changed analytical distinction.
3. **New `DECOMPOSE` Loss** — information compressed or excluded by the present finer map, including rival periodizations not pursued.

Recovered detail does not prove that the original history has been restored. New evidence can exceed the original Record while still leaving historical gaps.

### 11.2 Compression debt

Compression debt is the declared unresolved burden created when the coarse Path claim depends on distinctions that were compressed or are no longer reconstructible. It is not a financial score, automatic defect class, or new PMS primitive. It names the traceability work still required for the current claim.

### 11.3 Non-compensation

```text
many new documents
+ detailed subpath map
+ coherent visualization
≠ irrecoverable history recovered
```

Formal richness cannot compensate for excluded or irrecoverable source structure.

## 12. Decomposition of Path Dependence

### 12.1 Property, not substance

Path Dependence remains a property of a bounded Path or Trajectory claim. Chapter 24 may open its historical load into source-supported profiles such as:

- `Α + Θ` recurrence and stabilization;
- `Ω + Θ` accumulated cost, exposure, or access asymmetry;
- `Ψ + Θ` binding and commitment load;
- `Λ + Θ` unresolved residue, repeated non-decision, or lost windows;
- specific lost or increasingly costly alternatives.

These expressions retain the canonical PMS operators and dependencies. They are not fused operators or hidden substances inside the Path.

### 12.2 Dependence-load test

A valid finer map asks:

- which historical sequence remains necessary for the present claim;
- which current conditions could explain the result without detailed history;
- which alternatives changed because of prior transitions;
- which costs, bindings, and residues persisted;
- whether the dependence claim is strong, weak, dimension-specific, or unsupported.

### 12.3 Possible revision

Finer reconstruction may confirm a bounded Path-Dependence claim, reduce it to order dependence, localize it to one dimension, show current-condition sufficiency, or leave the claim underdetermined. Chapter 24 does not own the complete resolution taxonomy, which remains Chapter 25 work.

## 13. Decomposition versus Rival PATH Construction

### 13.1 Classification question

The mandatory classification question is:

> Does the proposed finer reconstruction open the same bounded source object under a more discriminating map, or does it select and form a different Path object?

### 13.2 Same-Path indicators

- source reference remains stable;
- boundary remains compatible;
- original selection rule remains visible;
- finer components explain the coarse transition structure;
- coarser function remains reconstructible or explicitly revised;
- inherited Loss is disclosed.

### 13.3 Rival-PATH indicators

- materially different periodization;
- different constitutive source selection;
- different beginning or endpoint;
- different governing Frame;
- different formation relation;
- different historical object or claim;
- new combination of formerly separate paths.

The indicators do not form an automatic decision score. Human and source-grounded judgment remains necessary.

### 13.4 Separate records

Where a rival PATH construction is pursued, it requires:

- a distinct `COMPOSE` occurrence;
- separate source and target declarations;
- separate selection and formation rules;
- separate Loss;
- separate admissibility result.

The failed or reduced current `DECOMPOSE` claim remains visible. A new transformation is a new testable claim, not a rescue mechanism.

## 16. Failure, Mandatory Stop, and Non-Capture

### 16.1 Failure pressure

A Chapter-24 transformation pressures `failed_transformation` where it:

- lacks an independently warranted PATH source;
- produces only more chronology;
- lists fragments without subpath relations;
- invents transition clusters or turning points;
- forces partial or competing processes into one linear direction;
- loses the source Path or Trajectory function;
- treats Path Dependence as a substance or operator fusion;
- claims complete historical recovery;
- hides a rival `COMPOSE` result;
- silently assigns a target function;
- converts structural findings into person, guilt, legitimacy, or authority claims.

### 16.2 Mandatory Stop

Mandatory Stop applies where continuation would require:

- inventing subpaths, branches, transitions, turning points, Frame changes, or alternatives;
- treating retrospective salience as contemporaneous effect;
- maintaining Same-Path identity after reference break;
- recovering irrecoverable history as fact;
- hiding a new periodization or macro-object inside `DECOMPOSE`;
- revising PMS operator identities or dependencies;
- inferring prediction, person type, moral rank, legitimacy, sanction, or authority;
- continuing below the Relevance Floor or above the Traceability Ceiling.

### 16.3 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where:

- rival periodizations are equally supported;
- the source Path is legitimate but no stable finer map preserves its reference;
- transition clusters are partly known but their relations remain underdetermined;
- turning-point candidates cannot be discriminated;
- branch availability cannot be reconstructed without speculation;
- inherited compression blocks a responsible dependence-load map.

```text
non_capture
≠ coarse Path or Trajectory claim confirmed
≠ rival periodizations merged into compromise history
≠ irrecoverable detail restored
```
