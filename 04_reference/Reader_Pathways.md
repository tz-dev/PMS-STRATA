## 1. Role, Status, and Authority
This scaffold controls how readers may enter, traverse, leave, and return to PMS-STRATA artifacts without turning convenience into theory, production sequence into rank, or a presentation layer into authority. It remains the pathway owner during Foundations production and distinguishes current Core/smoke inspection and the available Chapter-0 route from still-pending corpus routes. Reference Kernel population and post-model synchronization are not the final Reference Freeze.
```text
Reader Pathways
≠
Reader implementation
≠
Front Matter
≠
canonical corpus
```
The file routes already controlled material. It does not define operations, claims, output classes, evidence standards, or audit semantics. It may mark a designated future route while keeping the corresponding target explicitly unavailable.
It shall:
- distinguish current repository inspection from the later canonical reading sequence;
- preserve Foundations prerequisites for every operation-specific entry;
- present LIMITS as cross-cutting even though its systematic Part occurs later;
- route cases, appendices, Reference files, and the Formal Model back to governing prose;
- keep derivatives and the later Reader without back-propagating authority;
- mark unavailable targets as pending rather than simulate completed content;
- provide bounded routes for different reader tasks without changing dependency or authority relations.
It shall not:
- introduce a fourth STRATA operation or a fifth contract-bound main route family;
- summarize chapter content beyond navigational necessity;
- turn route order into a truth, depth, maturity, or authority hierarchy;
- permit a shortcut to waive a hard dependency;
- present the formal model, cases, appendices, derivatives, or Reader as independent sources of truth;
- describe empty placeholders as usable present routes;
- introduce machine fields, a route score, a reader type, or a canonical output value.
## 2. Reader-Pathway Semantics
A **reader pathway** is a local navigation route linking a reader task to available artifacts, prerequisites, optional branches, required back-references, and non-substitution limits. It is not an analytical object in PMS-STRATA.
```text
reader pathway
≠
analytical path
≠
STRATA PATH
```
The word `pathway` is retained as ordinary navigation language. It does not invoke COMPOSE, PATH temporality, trajectory, or path dependence.
| Navigation term | Meaning in this scaffold | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| route family | one of four contract-bound primary reading families | not a STRATA operation or Part |
| supplemental route | task-specific navigation subordinate to the four primary families | does not create a fifth primary family |
| entry point | first artifact opened for a task | not a definition site automatically |
| prerequisite | required prior material or dependency | not a higher-authority artifact |
| core sequence | recommended traversal for the declared task | not an ontology or truth scale |
| optional branch | supporting artifact that may be consulted | not a substitute for governing prose |
| back-reference | required return to canonical or controlling source | does not transfer authority to the route |
| cannot substitute for | explicit limit on route use | not a claim that artifacts are unrelated |
| unlock condition | production event required before a route becomes current | not an output class |
| canonical return | route from support or derivative material back to governing corpus | not a reverse authority inheritance |

## 4. Reading Order, Production Order, and Authority
Navigation must preserve three independent relations: what is produced first, what a reader may read first, and what controls what. None can be inferred from another.
```text
production order ≠ reading order
reading order ≠ authority hierarchy
recommended reading sequence ≠ hard dependency
entry point ≠ definition site
specialist shortcut ≠ dependency waiver
```
The production sequence places Formal Model v0 before Foundations drafting. Its Core Assembly and internal smoke-test gate are complete; Foundations production is active, Chapter 0 is provisionally locked, and the Chapter 1 preparation gate plus Chapter 1 local audits have passed. The preparation record guides production, while the canonical Chapter 1 Chapter 1 anchor now supplies the available core and extended object-architecture reading route. The eventual full reading sequence begins with Front Matter and Foundations. Neither sequence changes the repository authority order.
| Relation | Controlled by | Reader-pathway duty | Prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| authority order | Project Instructions and repository architecture | show artifact roles at route entry | earlier or more convenient means more authoritative |
| hard dependency | Chapter and Block Contracts | display before specialist entry | recommended shortcut cancels dependency |
| production order | project production sequence | mark current and future availability | later-produced material is conceptually inferior |
| reading order | FM-HOW-TO-READ and this scaffold | offer bounded task routes | first-read artifact becomes source of truth |
| definition ownership | Cross Reference Map and primary prose sites | return readers to owner | entry point becomes definition site |

## 7. Availability and Pending-Target Discipline
Current artifact state and future unlock stage are recorded separately. A route may combine current controls with pending branches, but no single status term may silently encode both dimensions.

### 7.1 Controlled current-state vocabulary

| Current state | Permitted representation | Not permitted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| current architecture control | populated Structure artifact carrying architecture | treating it as locked corpus prose |
| current binding control | populated minified control or contract | treating compact control as complete theory |
| current navigation/status control | populated README or status-navigation artifact | treating status text as theory |
| provisionally controlled pre-Block | populated substantive Reference artifact | treating pre-Block control as final Reference Freeze |

## 28. No-Shortcut and Non-Substitution Rules
1. A specialist shortcut may change the first file opened; it may not waive a hard dependency.
2. An operation-specific route may focus on one operation; it may not imply that the other operations do not constrain a chain.
3. A Reference lookup may answer where and how a term is controlled; it may not replace the primary definition site.
4. A minified control may expose a rule quickly; it may not replace the full argument or cases.
5. A formal-model route may expose syntax and consistency; it may not decide substantive adequacy.
6. A case-first route may illustrate a rule; it may not define the rule or prove universal validity.
7. An appendix route may supply a template or stress test; it may not host unresolved core theory.
8. A derivative entry may orient a public reader; it may not become the source of truth.
9. A Reader interface may filter or sequence content; it may not change authority, support, status, or output.
10. A route to Chapter 51 may explain Stop; it may not treat production blockage as `mandatory_stop`.
11. A route to Chapter 52 may explain Non-Capture; it may not turn missing information into `non_capture`.
12. A route to a finer DECOMPOSE may not be used as a truth-priority shortcut.
13. A route to PROJECT_AS may not be used to evade a failed origin-type claim.
14. A route to a new frame, level, granularity, composition, or function creates a new testable claim where analysis changes.
15. A return link from a derivative does not make the derivative authoritative; it restores the proper source relation.
16. A full-route reader is not more authoritative than a specialist reader; route coverage is not person rank.
17. A route marked current is not necessarily canonical prose; current control and canonical destination remain distinct.
18. A route marked pending is not defective; it is unavailable until a declared production condition is met.
19. Navigation completeness does not imply corpus, case, model, or empirical completeness.
20. Route selection does not select, perform, or validate a STRATA operation.
```text
specialist shortcut ≠ dependency waiver
route selection ≠ operation selection
```
