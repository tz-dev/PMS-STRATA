## 2. Reader-Pathway Semantics
A **reader pathway** is a local navigation route linking a reader task to available artifacts, prerequisites, optional branches, required back-references, and non-substitution limits. It is not an analytical object in PMS-STRATA.
```text
reader pathway
≠
analytical path
≠
STRATA PATH
```
The word `pathway` is retained as ordinary navigation language. It does not invoke COMPOSE, PATH temporality, trajectory, or path dependence.
| Navigation term | Meaning in this scaffold | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| route family | one of four contract-bound primary reading families | not a STRATA operation or Part |
| supplemental route | task-specific navigation subordinate to the four primary families | does not create a fifth primary family |
| entry point | first artifact opened for a task | not a definition site automatically |
| prerequisite | required prior material or dependency | not a higher-authority artifact |
| core sequence | recommended traversal for the declared task | not an ontology or truth scale |
| optional branch | supporting artifact that may be consulted | not a substitute for governing prose |
| back-reference | required return to canonical or controlling source | does not transfer authority to the route |
| cannot substitute for | explicit limit on route use | not a claim that artifacts are unrelated |
| unlock condition | production event required before a route becomes current | not an output class |
| canonical return | route from support or derivative material back to governing corpus | not a reverse authority inheritance |

## 28. No-Shortcut and Non-Substitution Rules
1. A specialist shortcut may change the first file opened; it may not waive a hard dependency.
2. An operation-specific route may focus on one operation; it may not imply that the other operations do not constrain a chain.
3. A Reference lookup may answer where and how a term is controlled; it may not replace the primary definition site.
4. A minified control may expose a rule quickly; it may not replace the full argument or cases.
5. A formal-model route may expose syntax and consistency; it may not decide substantive adequacy.
6. A case-first route may illustrate a rule; it may not define the rule or prove universal validity.
7. An appendix route may supply a template or stress test; it may not host unresolved core theory.
8. A derivative entry may orient a public reader; it may not become the source of truth.
9. A Reader interface may filter or sequence content; it may not change authority, support, status, or output.
10. A route to Chapter 51 may explain Stop; it may not treat production blockage as `mandatory_stop`.
11. A route to Chapter 52 may explain Non-Capture; it may not turn missing information into `non_capture`.
12. A route to a finer DECOMPOSE may not be used as a truth-priority shortcut.
13. A route to PROJECT_AS may not be used to evade a failed origin-type claim.
14. A route to a new frame, level, granularity, composition, or function creates a new testable claim where analysis changes.
15. A return link from a derivative does not make the derivative authoritative; it restores the proper source relation.
16. A full-route reader is not more authoritative than a specialist reader; route coverage is not person rank.
17. A route marked current is not necessarily canonical prose; current control and canonical destination remain distinct.
18. A route marked pending is not defective; it is unavailable until a declared production condition is met.
19. Navigation completeness does not imply corpus, case, model, or empirical completeness.
20. Route selection does not select, perform, or validate a STRATA operation.
```text
specialist shortcut ≠ dependency waiver
route selection ≠ operation selection
```
