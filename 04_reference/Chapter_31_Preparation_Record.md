This Record prepares the first RETYPE projection-family chapter. Chapter 31 does not redefine Trajectory, Frame, functional projection, or `PROJECT_AS`. It instantiates the already locked generic procedure for one bounded family claim:

```text
source object: reconstructed Trajectory X
origin type: Trajectory
```


## 6. Section Architecture

### 31.1 Basic Claim

Establish the bounded family claim: a Trajectory may perform a frame-function when source-traceable historical load materially structures a declared later configuration.

### 31.2 Source Object

Require a PATH-established Trajectory, retained source reference, origin type, temporal scope, prior disposition, uncertainty, and inherited Loss.

### 31.3 Target Context

Declare the later configuration, target Frame, granularity, relative level, roles, time window, praxis dimensions, present conditions, and Claim Ceiling.

### 31.4 Frame-Function

Specify what the Trajectory does in the target context: it may condition legibility, expectation, credible interpretation, role reading, action corridor, cost, binding, or alternative visibility. It does not become `□`.

### 31.5 Historical Load

Trace the proposed function to sedimented expectations, roles, costs, exclusions, bindings, residues, non-events, and changed alternatives.

### 31.6 Frame-Function without Historical Determinism

Require constraint or conditioning without total determination, prediction, inevitability, or causal monopoly.

### 31.7 Multiple Frame Sources

Represent current Frames, other historical trajectories, institutional conditions, role rules, events, and recontextualizations as potentially concurrent sources. State relative load without scoring.

### 31.8 Rhetorical History versus Frame-Function

Reject invocations of “history,” “legacy,” “culture,” or “the past” that lack an identifiable source trajectory, constitutive trace, bounded target difference, and counterfactual pressure.

### 31.9 Counterfactual Frame Test

Test whether relevant alteration or removal of historical-load carriers changes the frame-function claim in the declared target context. The test does not prove causality.

### 31.10 Competing Frame Projections

Retain rival bounded frame-functions, background relevance, present-frame accounts, recontextualization-only, and no-projection.

### 31.11 Failed Frame Projection

Represent failure where source trace, target difference, sensitivity, scope, type integrity, or responsible discrimination cannot be sustained. Failure does not invalidate the source Trajectory automatically.

1. **Retrospective source selection.** A Trajectory must not be reconstructed or selected only because it fits the later frame-function claim. The source object must already be independently warranted by PATH.

3. **Temporal evidence leakage.** Later retrospective descriptions may support a bounded reconstruction only when marked as later evidence; they may not silently substitute for source-period or target-period support.
