## 3. Contract Lock

### 3.1 Governing question

> What must every transformation disclose so that its source, operation, target, admissibility, loss, alternatives, limits, and status remain reviewable across all STRATA Parts?

### 3.2 Required claims

Chapter 7 must establish:

1. every `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, and `PROJECT_AS` occurrence uses one shared record envelope;
2. operation-specific extensions are permitted only when the common declaration duties remain intact;
3. a transformation and its record are different objects;
4. a complete record makes a claim inspectable but does not prove truth, causal correctness, semantic adequacy, normative validity, or admissibility;
5. the common conceptual field families are Source, Operation, Target, Admissibility, Loss, Alternatives, Governance, and separated Status/Result axes;
6. record identity, metadata, claim, relation, chain, extension, and formal-diagnostic structures are serialization companions whose role must be prose-governed rather than model-invented;
7. every operation occurrence has exactly one operation kind;
8. every operation in a chain receives its own occurrence record or explicit occurrence segment, with local result and loss preserved;
9. a chain-level claim is distinct from every occurrence-level claim;
10. required declaration families may contain explicit uncertainty, empty categories, or source-limited values where warranted, but may not be silently omitted or filled by invention;
11. loss is declared through the closed five-part structure `preserved`, `compressed`, `excluded`, `uncertain`, and `irrecoverable`;
12. rival transformations, no-transformation candidates, non-translation, and unresolved alternatives remain distinguishable;
13. support status, resolution-test result, claim disposition, capture statement, routing state, and canonical Output Class remain separate axes;
14. a routed record selects exactly one canonical Output Class per delimited tested claim;
15. a `formal_diagnostic` record carries no canonical Output Class;
16. local extensions are namespaced, sourced, bounded, and non-authoritative;
17. authority inheritance remains prohibited;
18. formal schemas may check presence, type, allowed values, relation consistency, and routing structure, but not substantive adequacy.

### 3.3 Required counterpressure

Chapter 7 must withstand at least the following pressures:

- a record can be syntactically complete while its Constitutive Source Trace is substantively insufficient;
- a record can be declaration-complete while epistemically incomplete;
- a record can remain formal-diagnostic because routing prerequisites are unresolved;
- a capture limit can be present without the result being `non_capture`;
- provisional support does not by itself imply `admissible_but_provisional`;
- failed claim disposition does not by itself imply `failed_transformation`;
- a chain can contain one admissible occurrence followed by one failed occurrence;
- two rival transformations may need separate records rather than one blended account;
- a local extension can be formally well-typed yet impermissible because it bypasses a shared duty;
- an explicitly empty loss or alternatives category is a substantive declaration, not an invisible default;
- source gaps may require underdetermination, Stop, or Non-Capture instead of invented values.

### 3.4 Prohibited claims

Chapter 7 must not claim that:

- a schema-valid record is true or admissible;
- more fields create more authority;
- machine-required precision justifies source-unsupported precision;
- one record may declare `COMPOSE + DECOMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE + PROJECT_AS`, or any other compound kind;
- chain-level success rescues a failed occurrence;
- chain-level failure erases an admissible occurrence;
- every record must be routed even when formal diagnosis is the only justified state;
- every capture limit is Non-Capture;
- every unsupported claim is `failed_transformation`;
- every failed claim disposition is operation failure;
- operation-specific templates may omit loss, alternatives, governance, Stop, or Non-Capture duties;
- the record owns operation semantics, admissibility criteria, or Output Class definitions;
- record metadata, versioning, or workflow state affects substantive claim rank;
- technical validation grants application authority.

## 5. Shared Record Architecture

### 5.1 Canonical conceptual envelope

The shared conceptual envelope contains eight duty families:

```yaml
source:
operation:
target:
admissibility:
loss:
alternatives:
governance:
status_and_result:
```

The first seven are the common transformation declaration families identified by the chapter contract and Structure blueprint. The eighth names the required separation of record status, claim disposition, capture, routing, and canonical result.

The conceptual envelope is operation-neutral in format but operation-specific in populated content.

```text
shared envelope
≠ flattened operation semantics
```

### 5.2 Serialization companion families

The current formal schema also uses companion structures for:

- schema and record identity;
- record metadata and provenance;
- the delimited claim;
- operation-chain declarations;
- result payload and routing rationale;
- relations to prior, rival, successor, reduced, or split claims and records;
- namespaced extensions;
- formal diagnostics.

These companion families do not create new STRATA theory objects. They serialize identities, relations, provenance, and process states required to inspect the shared record responsibly.

```text
serialization companion
≠ new transformation primitive
≠ new claim authority
```

### 5.3 Family completeness versus field precision

Every applicable common family must be present. This does not mean that every possible field value is known.

```text
declaration completeness
≠ epistemic completeness
```

A declaration-complete record can state:

- known source gaps;
- provisional typing;
- underdetermined Counterfactual Sensitivity;
- explicitly empty rival categories;
- unresolved alternatives;
- capture limits;
- a Stop condition;
- a formal-diagnostic routing state.

The record fails its disclosure duty when it silently omits an applicable family, hides a known gap, converts uncertainty into precision, or lets a local extension substitute for a common duty.

The current formal schema may distribute these duties across `audit_stages`, `rule_assessments`, `stop_assessment`, `non_capture_assessment`, and routing fields. Chapter 7 should define the disclosure relationship, not create duplicate conceptual slots.


### 7.2 Loss Declaration

The Loss family is closed at five categories:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

All five categories remain visible. An empty category means that no item is currently asserted under that category within the declared scope. It does not mean that loss has been proven absent universally.

```text
empty declared category
≠ omitted category
```

```text

≠ irrecoverable loss
```

```text
compressed
≠ excluded
```

Operation-specific templates may elaborate loss entries but may not remove or merge the five categories.

### 7.3 Alternatives Declaration

The Alternatives family preserves rival analytical routes and the possibility that no transformation is warranted:

```yaml
alternatives:
  rival_compositions:
  rival_decompositions:
  rival_projections:
  no_transformation:
  non_translation:
  unresolved:
```

The preparation must keep separate:

- a rival transformation of the same or related source material;
- a no-transformation alternative;
- non-translation, where the proposed movement is not responsibly representable as a STRATA operation under the tested claim;
- unresolved alternatives that available sources cannot discriminate.

Two rival transformations may require sibling records. They are not forced into one composite record merely for convenience.

### 7.4 Governance Declaration

Governance records limits; it does not create authority.

The conceptual duties include:

The current schema distributes some of these duties across `/claim/claim_ceiling`, `/admissibility/stop_assessment`, `/admissibility/non_capture_assessment`, and `/governance/*`. Chapter 7 must map the conceptual duty to the existing carrier instead of duplicating fields.

## 10. Completeness, Unknowns, and Non-Capture

### 10.1 Declaration completeness

A record is declaration-complete when every applicable common family is present, every required relation is explicit, known gaps are disclosed, and the routing state is justified.

### 10.2 Epistemic completeness

Epistemic completeness concerns whether the sources support all relevant distinctions needed for the tested claim. Chapter 7 does not require epistemic completeness as a universal condition. It requires honest declaration of its absence.

```text
declaration-complete
+ epistemically incomplete
→ possible and reviewable
```

### 10.3 Analyst omission versus justified incompleteness

Justified incompleteness may arise from:

- unavailable sources;
- underdetermined target context;
- unresolved rival transformations;
- non-reconstructible historical relation;
- a legitimate Stop boundary;
- claim-relative Non-Capture.

Analyst omission arises when an applicable duty is simply not recorded, a known gap is hidden, or a convenient extension substitutes for the common field.

```text
justified incompleteness
≠ analyst omission
```

### 10.4 Non-Capture record duty

A Non-Capture record must preserve:

- the delimited tested claim;
- the available source material;
- rival candidate transformations or functions;
- the limiting condition;
- the capture boundary;
- rejected overclaims;
- re-entry conditions.

Non-Capture does not permit an empty record. It requires a precise record of why stronger capture is not currently warranted.

## 11. Local Extension Rule

Operation- and Part-specific extensions are permitted only under the following constraints:

1. the extension is explicitly named and scoped;
2. its control source is identified;
3. its rationale states why the common envelope is insufficient for the local procedure;
4. it does not redefine a common field;
5. it does not omit, merge, or bypass a common family;
6. it does not add a fourth operation, new Output Class, new object primitive, or authority-bearing status;
7. it remains compatible with the record’s claim ceiling and governance limits;
8. it remains removable without changing the canonical meaning of the common envelope;
9. it is auditable as an extension rather than silently presented as core vocabulary.

```text
local extension
= bounded additional declaration
≠ local override
```

A formally valid extension that bypasses shared loss, alternatives, governance, status, or Stop duties is not admissible merely because it conforms to a local template.

### 13.1 Positive minimal record duty
