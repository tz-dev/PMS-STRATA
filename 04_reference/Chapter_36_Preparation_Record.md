## 1. Preparation Status and Scope

This Record prepares the comparative RETYPE chapter. Chapter 36 does not redefine `PROJECT_AS`, target functions, family-specific projection requirements, Output Classes, the Admissibility Band, or the Shared Transformation Record. It applies Chapters 29–35 to the problem of **several candidate projections from the same source object**, distinguishing compatibility, competition, context-dependence, indeterminacy, non-comparability, non-translation, and no-projection.

The governing question is:

> How may several plausible target functions be compared without forcing one integrated label, confusing comparative preference with truth, converting a target function into the source object's type, or turning comparison into tribunal judgment?

```text
multiple plausible projections
≠ one projection must be true

comparative preference
≠ source identity
≠ absolute theory superiority

indeterminacy
≠ hidden failure

non-translation
≠ contradiction
```

Chapter 36 may add comparative discipline. It may not create a universal projection ranking, empirical truth criterion, person judgment, political or legal legitimacy decision, recommendation, sanction, governance, or authority inheritance.

## 6. Scientific Pressure and Counterfactual Architecture

The family method must remain vulnerable to at least the following pressures:

1. **Coordinate pressure:** apparent competition may dissolve when target context, relative level, Claim Scope, temporal scope, or validity scope is made explicit.
2. **Shared-source pressure:** candidates may rely on different source subsets or periods, defeating a nominal same-source comparison.
3. **Family-burden pressure:** one candidate may satisfy its own family requirements while another does not.
4. **PraxisPurchase pressure:** one candidate may add no discrimination beyond source-only description.
5. **Source-Trace pressure:** a candidate may use a compelling label but remain insensitive to material source changes.
6. **Counterexample pressure:** one candidate may absorb cases that should defeat or narrow it.
7. **Loss-profile pressure:** candidates may preserve different dimensions and incur non-comparable losses.
8. **Parsimony pressure:** a weaker or narrower candidate may discriminate better with fewer assumptions.
9. **No-projection pressure:** source-only or current-target explanations may outperform all projected functions.
10. **Indeterminacy pressure:** available evidence may support co-validity, partial preference, or unresolved competition without Failure.
11. **Non-translation pressure:** candidates may not be losslessly convertible into each other even where both are admissible.
12. **Tribunal pressure:** comparative preference must not become person judgment, political legitimacy, or absolute theory rank.

The integrated comparison test must vary:

- target context and target level;
- Claim Scope and validity scope;
- family-specific source subset;
- material source features;
- present target conditions;
- countercases and excluded evidence;
- Loss profile;
- alternative and no-projection accounts;
- comparison criteria and their applicability.

Counterfactual comparison remains qualitative. It does not produce causal percentages, prediction, or a universal ranking score.

## 9. Loss, Stop, Non-Capture, and Re-entry

Canonical Loss remains exactly:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

Comparison must not collapse candidate Loss profiles into one aggregate score. One projection may preserve historical framing while another preserves transition segmentation; these differences may be relevant rather than defects.

Mandatory Stop applies where continuation would require:

- inventing common target coordinates or shared source features;
- changing one candidate's source or target to manufacture comparability;
- forcing compatible projections into one integrated label;
- treating a preferred target function as source identity;
- ranking by breadth, elegance, popularity, formal completeness, or authority;
- suppressing no-projection, indeterminacy, non-comparability, or Non-Capture;
- converting comparison into person, moral, political, legal, institutional, or theory tribunal judgment;
- recommendation, sanction, governance, or authority inheritance.

Non-Capture remains available where:

- candidates use non-equivalent source subsets without a justified common basis;
- target contexts, levels, scopes, or Loss profiles are not responsibly comparable;
- evidence supports several non-equivalent candidates equally;
- present target conditions cannot be separated from historical projection;
- no bounded preference can be selected without overclaim.

A material change in source object, source subset, target context, target level, target object, Claim Scope, validity scope, target function, evidence, Loss, or comparison criterion creates a new candidate and/or comparison claim.
