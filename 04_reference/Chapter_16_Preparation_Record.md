## 5. PATH Admissibility-Band Localization

Chapter 16 does not create a second Band. It applies the common Band to temporal objects and `COMPOSE` claims.

The lower boundary asks:

> Does the added temporal differentiation change the warranted reconstruction of praxis?

The upper boundary asks:

> Does the result remain reconstructibly dependent on its declared temporal and structural source load?

```text
lower-bound success
≠ upper-bound success automatically

upper-bound traceability
≠ lower-bound purchase automatically
```

The boundaries are non-compensatory. Source volume, formal completeness, visual elegance, historical span, causal rhetoric, or detail on one axis cannot repair failure on the other.

---

## 6. Lower PATH Boundary

A candidate falls below the lower PATH boundary when temporal differentiation adds no material praxis finding for the tested claim.

Typical signals:

- dates are added but roles, costs, alternatives, bindings, residuals, or Action Corridors do not change;
- order is narrated but its reversal or removal would not alter the reconstruction;
- transitions are enumerated without a source-supported relation;
- finer temporal detail reproduces the same claim without improved discrimination;
- duration is treated as historical load without sedimentation or continuation effects.

The local boundary result uses the existing controlled vocabulary:

```text
relevance_floor_boundary_result:
  gain
  neutral
  below_floor
  underdetermined
```

These values are not Output Classes.

---

## 7. Chronology without Trajectory Gain

A chronology may be accurate, useful, and well sourced while still remaining below the PATH threshold or below a stronger Trajectory claim.

The preparation requires separate questions:

1. Does order matter?
2. Does the order form a Path rather than a list?
3. Does earlier structure remain active in later structure?
4. Does the added temporal account change the licensed claim?

```text
many dates
+ correct ordering
≠ Trajectory gain
```

A chronology below the Floor may map to `resolution_neutral`, `claim_reduction_required`, or another canonical class depending on the delimited claim and full audit. It is not automatically a failed transformation.

---

## 8. Upper PATH Boundary

A candidate exceeds the upper PATH boundary when the result no longer carries reconstructible load from the temporal structures declared as constitutive.

Typical signals:

- a macro-label replaces the path;
- periods are homogenized into one phase;
- local reversals and competing subpaths disappear;
- `Λ`, `Ω`, `Ψ`, or residual load is erased;
- the result survives removal, reorder, or substitution of allegedly constitutive sources;
- source citations remain but no source-to-result dependency is reconstructible.

The existing local vocabulary remains controlling:

```text
traceability_ceiling_boundary_result:
  within_ceiling
  claim_reduction
  above_ceiling
  underdetermined
```

Again, these are boundary findings, not Output Classes.

---

## 9. Trajectory without Path Trace

A Trajectory claim fails the upper boundary when historical direction or sedimentation is asserted but the path load cannot be reconstructed.

Required trace includes, where material:

- starting and intermediate configurations;
- transitions and relevant order;
- turning points and phase boundaries;
- Non-Events and delays;
- asymmetries and binding relations;
- branches, alternatives, reversals, and repair;
- present-bearing historical load;
- selection, compression, and Loss.

```text
Trajectory label
+ historical rhetoric
≠ traceable Trajectory
```

A weaker Path, Sequence, chronology, or source collection may remain.

---

## 10. Excessive Temporal Compression and Punctualization

Temporal compression is permitted when the reduced representation preserves claim-relevant internal temporality and external recoverability.

Punctualization occurs when an extended, internally differentiated process is treated as a single point, event, or homogeneous phase such that constitutive order, duration, reversal, branch, `Λ`, `Ω`, binding, or residual load disappears.

```text
compression
≠ punctualization
```

The test must state:

- compressed interval and rule;
- retained internal distinctions;
- lost or externally recoverable load;
- effect on order, alternatives, asymmetries, Non-Events, and claim strength;
- whether decompression remains possible only as a later `DECOMPOSE` operation.

---

## 11. Artificial Directionality

Directionality is artificial when retrospective arrangement is presented as an intrinsic developmental vector without preserving contingency, reversals, counter-movements, and alternative continuations.

Pressure questions:

- Would a different supported periodization alter the apparent direction?
- Are local reversals or parallel subpaths suppressed?
- Is later stabilization back-projected into earlier ambiguity?
- Does the direction depend on excluding inconvenient transitions?

```text
ordered retrospectively
≠ directed originally
```

Artificial directionality may require claim reduction even where the underlying Path remains admissible.

---

## 12. Hidden Teleology

Teleology is present when the later outcome governs selection, periodization, or interpretation of earlier material such that the result appears necessary, progressive, declining, maturing, or convergent by construction.

A teleology test must examine:

- endpoint-conditioned selection;
- removal of non-fitting transitions;
- treatment of blocked or lost alternatives;
- conversion of contingency into necessity;
- conflation of directionality with purpose or destination;
- predictive or normative inflation.

```text
later outcome
≠ earlier purpose
≠ historical necessity
```

Teleology and excessive compression are separately testable. Either may occur without the other.

---

## 13. Omitted Non-Events

A PATH claim crosses boundary pressure when constitutive `Λ` occurrences are translated into positive continuity or disappear as harmless gaps.

The audit asks whether the candidate preserves:

- expected occurrence;
- expectation frame and window;
- supported non-realization;
- positive sub-events;
- accumulated delay, cost, binding, residue, or branch effect;
- uncertainty and source limitation.

```text
positive-event path only
≠ complete path where Λ is constitutive
```

Omission may cause claim reduction, failed transformation, mandatory stop, or Non-Capture depending on materiality and continuation.

---

## 14. Omitted Asymmetries

A PATH claim loses traceable load when equal-looking events are treated as equally costly, accessible, reversible, or binding despite source-supported `Ω` differences.

The audit asks whether the reconstruction preserves:

- unequal entry and exit costs;
- role-specific exposure;
- differential access or authorization;
- uneven reversibility;
- distribution of repair, evidence, and coordination burdens;
- historical persistence or redistribution of those loads.

```text
same event label
≠ same practical load
```

The chapter remains structural and does not infer person type, blame, legitimacy, or sanction.

---

## 15. PATH versus RETYPE

PATH asks how a temporal structure formed, persisted, branched, sedimented, or failed.

RETYPE later asks what bounded contextual function an origin-typed object performs in another context through `PROJECT_AS`.

```text
Trajectory object
≠ Frame function
≠ Macro-Event function
≠ Attractor function
```

A failed PATH claim cannot be rescued by assigning a target function. A valid later `PROJECT_AS` is a new testable claim and does not repair the origin-object analysis retroactively.

---

## 16. PATH versus SUB

PATH composes or reconstructs temporal relations among source structures. SUB opens an already compressed occurrence or composite at finer resolution through `DECOMPOSE`.

```text
more temporal detail
≠ DECOMPOSE automatically

finer chronology
≠ stronger PATH automatically
```

A move to finer granularity may reveal a different claim, a resolution-neutral result, drift, or escape. It cannot erase a failed PATH claim. The original claim and result remain recorded.

---

## 17. Praxeological Purchase Test

The PATH-specific Purchase Test asks whether temporal differentiation materially changes the warranted reconstruction of:

- action corridors and reachable continuations;
- roles, access, and exposure;
- costs, asymmetries, and reversibility;
- bindings, release conditions, and residuals;
- branch status, lost alternatives, and repair;
- present-bearing historical load;
- claim scope or output route.

A positive test requires a reasoned source-to-difference account. Narrative interest, historical richness, extra dates, visual density, or reader engagement do not suffice.

---

## 18. Traceable Path Test

The PATH-specific Trace Test asks whether the target claim remains reconstructibly dependent on:

- identifiable source configurations and transitions;
- warrantable order and boundaries;
- constitutive Non-Events, asymmetries, bindings, alternatives, and residuals;
- Selection Rule, Formation Rule, and complete Loss where `COMPOSE` is used;
- Counterfactual Sensitivity to removal, reorder, substitution, recompression, and reframing;
- declared uncertainty and source ceilings.

A source-insensitive label exceeds the Ceiling even if it is rhetorically useful.

## 19. Claim Reduction and Boundary Outputs

Boundary findings remain separate from canonical Output Classes. The chapter prepares the following controlled reduction ladder:

```text
Trajectory
→ bounded Path
→ Sequence
→ chronology or partially ordered source field
→ source collection
→ no PATH claim
```

Possible canonical mappings include:

- `resolution_neutral` where added temporal detail creates no material gain for the tested resolution claim;
- `admissible_with_bounded_claim` where a narrower time, frame, object, or relation survives;
- `admissible_but_provisional` where order or source support remains materially open but a bounded claim survives;
- `claim_reduction_required` where the stronger claim must be withdrawn and retested;
- `failed_transformation` where no warranted PATH result remains from the attempted operation;
- `mandatory_stop` or `non_capture` under their distinct conditions.

No local boundary value mechanically selects an Output Class.

---

## 20. PATH Stop Conditions

Stop is positive boundary governance, not analytical defeat.

Optional Stop is appropriate when further temporal detail or recomposition adds no justified purchase and the current bounded result is adequate.

`mandatory_stop` pressure arises when continued analysis would:

- exceed the source basis;
- force arbitrary periodization or total order;
- preserve a known teleological or source-indifferent label;
- conceal constitutive `Λ`, `Ω`, alternatives, reversals, or Loss;
- reuse a failed PATH claim for prediction, sanction, person judgment, legitimacy, irreversible classification, target-function assignment, or authority inheritance;
- relabel the same failure through SUB, RETYPE, a new frame, graph, or level.

```text
optional Stop
≠ mandatory_stop
```

---

## 21. PATH Non-Capture

Non-Capture remains available where adequate bounded reconstruction cannot be achieved without distortion or false closure, for example when:

- no stable order or periodization is supportable;
- competing Paths remain materially irreducible;
- relevant alternatives or transitions are unavailable;
- every composition destroys decisive heterogeneity;
- the same materials support incompatible but non-reducible source traces;
- lower- and upper-bound pressures cannot be resolved into one adequate claim.

```text
uncertainty
≠ non_capture automatically

missing data
≠ non_capture mechanically
```

Non-Capture differs from Stop: Stop governs inadmissible continuation; Non-Capture states that adequate capture remains unavailable after bounded tests.
