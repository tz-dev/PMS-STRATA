## 3. Contract Lock

### 3.1 Governing question

> When does a Path become a Trajectory rather than remain a traversed sequence of configurations and transitions?

### 3.2 Required chapter claim

```text
A trajectory is a temporally ordered path
whose repeated and irreversible structure
historically co-determines the present configuration
and bounded continuation possibilities.
```

The phrase **historically co-determines** is bounded. It means that earlier Path structure carries traceable present load. It does not mean exclusive causation, deterministic production, or the strong Path-Dependence property owned by Chapter 12.

### 3.3 Conjunctive trajectory threshold

The Preparation Gate fixes the following threshold architecture:

```text
warranted path
+ sedimented or cumulative relation
+ persistent residue
+ changed present praxis conditions
+ historical load
+ non-teleological directionality
+ bounded boundary and compression
→ trajectory candidate
```

A **warranted Trajectory** additionally requires applicable Continuity, Admissibility, Loss, Governance, Counterfactual, Source, Claim, Stop, and Non-Capture tests.

```text
trajectory candidate
≠ warranted trajectory
```

No single element may compensate for a failed conjunctive burden. A long Path with no persistent load remains a Path. Repetition without changed action corridors remains repetition. A coherent periodization without Source–Result Dependency remains narrative construction.

The Chapter Contract identifies four recurrent operator-temporality relations:


These are not formulas that mechanically generate Trajectory. They identify possible source burdens:

A Trajectory need not instantiate all four profiles. It must declare which profiles are constitutive, which are contextual, which are absent, and which remain uncertain. No profile can be inferred solely from the operator symbol.

## 5. From Path to Trajectory

### 5.1 Necessary Path substrate

A Trajectory claim requires a warranted Path or an explicitly bounded Path segment whose traversal, selection, frame, components, alternatives, evidence, and loss are already declared.

```text
failed path substrate
≠ valid trajectory source automatically
```

A Trajectory may be reconstructed from a revised Path claim only where the revision is explicit, newly tested, and preserves lineage to the earlier result.

### 5.2 Additional Trajectory load

The Path becomes a Trajectory candidate only when its earlier structure remains operative at the present analytical cut. The record must show more than accumulated events. It must show how earlier transitions, non-events, asymmetries, bindings, attractor relations, frame changes, or exclusions carry forward.

At minimum, Chapter 11 must be able to declare:

- what accumulated or sedimented;
- through which configurations or transitions;
- what persisted or remained as residue;
- which present action corridors, costs, expectations, bindings, asymmetries, or alternatives differ because of that history;
- how the source trace supports the relation;
- which counterfactual variation would weaken or revise the claim;
- what remains open, reversible, contested, or underdetermined.

### 5.3 Historical co-determination

Historical co-determination is a bounded relation between earlier Path structure and present praxis conditions.

```text
historical co-determination
≠ complete causal mechanism
≠ exclusive determination
≠ deterministic closure
```

The present may also be shaped by current rules, resources, actors, technologies, environments, or exogenous events. Chapter 11 must not erase current-condition explanations merely because the Path is reconstructible.

### 5.4 Stronger-than-Path, weaker-than-Dependence boundary

```text
path reconstruction burden
< trajectory burden
< path-dependence burden
```

This is an ordering of claim load, not of truth or authority. Chapter 11 establishes sedimented historical load. Chapter 12 later tests the degree to which that load constrains, channels, or determines current and future alternatives.

## 6. Historical Sedimentation Architecture

### 6.1 Sedimentation

Sedimentation is the traceable retention and cumulative transformation of earlier Path structure such that it remains materially relevant to the present configuration or continuation possibilities.

```text
elapsed duration
≠ sedimentation

repeated occurrence
≠ sedimentation

persistent label
≠ sedimentation
```

A sedimentation declaration must identify:

- the sedimented relation or structure;
- its Path and temporal scope;
- the carrier or continuity relation;
- repeated, cumulative, or irreversible contributions;
- persistent residue;
- present praxis effect;
- source trace and gaps;
- relevant counterfactual pressure;
- loss and compression;
- claim ceiling.

### 6.2 Cumulative change

Cumulative change need not be linear, monotonic, or additive. It may include:

- repeated reinforcement;
- threshold crossing;
- partial erosion with remaining residue;
- repair that leaves altered cost or expectation;
- alternation that gradually changes accessible options;
- distributed effects across several carriers.

```text
cumulative change
≠ monotonic increase
≠ numerical addition automatically
```

Chapter 11 must avoid universal accumulation scores. A qualitative or mixed record may be admissible where the source relation remains traceable.

### 6.3 Persistent residue

Persistent residue is unresolved or retained historical load that survives beyond the event or transition that produced it. It may include:

- unresolved commitments;
- sunk or reopening costs;
- institutional memory embedded in procedures or documents;
- reduced visibility of alternatives;
- altered expectations;
- non-restored asymmetries;
- accumulated Non-Events;
- durable interpretive or material traces.

```text
persistent residue
≠ permanence
≠ metaphysical irreversibility
```

Residue may weaken, be repaired, be redistributed, or become conditionally dormant. Its later absence must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

### 6.4 Changed action corridors

A Trajectory must show bounded change in accessible continuation possibilities. Relevant changes may include:

- new or institutionalized routes;
- increased or reduced exit costs;
- options becoming less visible or less accessible;
- recurrent continuation becoming locally easier;
- repair requiring additional resources or authorization;
- alternatives remaining formally open but effectively narrowed.

```text
changed action corridor
≠ eliminated agency
≠ inevitable continuation
```

### 6.5 Sedimentation matrix

| Dimension | Required declaration | Prohibited shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| repeated/cumulative structure | what recurs, accumulates, or changes through the Path | repetition count proves sedimentation |
| continuity/carrier | how the historical load persists or is translated | same label proves continuity |
| residue | what remains at the present cut | every trace is permanent |
| present praxis effect | which corridor, cost, expectation, binding, asymmetry, or alternative differs | historical interest equals current effect |
| counterpressure | repair, reversal, current-condition explanation, rival construction | coherent story immunizes claim |
| source/claim ceiling | strongest warranted relation | detail volume increases authority |

The dimensions are non-compensatory for the tested claim.

### 6.6 Sufficiency, failure, and re-entry

Sedimentation sufficiency is claim-relative but not discretionary. The record must show that removing or materially altering the alleged historical carrier would change the warranted reconstruction of the present configuration or continuation possibilities. Where that counterfactual relation is unsupported, the analyst may preserve duration, recurrence, persistence, local accumulation, or residue without retaining the full Trajectory claim. Re-entry requires new source material, a revised Path lineage, a narrower claim, or an explicitly changed analytical cut. Merely adding detail, lengthening the period, changing the label, or selecting a more coherent endpoint does not repair the missing historical-load relation.

```text
more temporal detail
≠ stronger sedimentation warrant automatically

re-entry after failed trajectory
= new testable claim with preserved lineage
```

## 7. Directionality without Teleology

### 7.1 Directionality

A Trajectory may exhibit a reconstructible orientation where successive sedimented relations repeatedly alter continuation conditions in a bounded direction—for example toward higher exit cost, narrower reconsideration routes, stronger binding, or stabilized institutional continuation.

```text
directionality
≠ teleology
```

Directionality is claim-relative and dimension-specific. A Path may show directional increase in one burden and directional erosion in another.

### 7.2 Anti-teleology requirements

The record must preserve:

- unrealized alternatives;
- interruptions, reversals, repairs, and contingencies;
- uncertainty about intention or foresight;
- the difference between later coherence and earlier plan;
- the possibility that the current endpoint was not anticipated;
- the distinction between probability, accessibility, and necessity.

```text
retrospective coherence
≠ original plan

repeated continuation
≠ destiny

directional change
≠ progress or decline automatically
```

### 7.3 Direction reversal and internal variation

Internal variation does not automatically invalidate a Trajectory. The relevant question is whether the bounded historical load survives or is transformed across the variation.

```text
internal variation
≠ trajectory failure automatically
```

A reversal may:

- dissolve the Trajectory claim;
- create a new segment;
- preserve residue despite changed direction;
- produce competing periodizations;
- require claim reduction.

The disposition must be source- and claim-specific.

## 8. Operator-Combination Pressure

### 8.1 Attractor Sedimentation — `Α + Θ`

Attractor Sedimentation may be supported where repeated configurations, lowered local friction, stabilized expectations, recurring coordination, or increased default accessibility make particular continuations easier or more likely.

```text
repetition
≠ attractor sedimentation automatically
```

Required controls include:

- bounded Attractor occurrence typing;
- temporal trace across configurations;
- changed continuation accessibility or local friction;
- competing explanations;
- no conversion of likelihood into necessity;
- no conversion of a Trajectory into an attractor-function.

### 8.2 Asymmetry Accumulation — `Ω + Θ`

Asymmetry Accumulation may be supported where differential cost, information, access, exposure, decision capacity, or exit burden is repeatedly retained or increased through the Path.

```text
unequal outcome
≠ accumulated asymmetry automatically
```

The record must identify distribution, carrier, temporal relation, present effect, repair or countertrend, and source ceiling.

### 8.3 Binding Accumulation — `Ψ + Θ`

Binding Accumulation may be supported through repeated commitments, obligations, implicit expectations, sunk coordination, rule stabilization, or increasing breach and reopening costs.

```text
multiple commitments
≠ cumulative binding automatically
```

Binding may strengthen, weaken, transfer, conflict, or become dormant. Chapter 11 must not infer moral obligation, legitimacy, or personal commitment from structural binding alone.

### 8.4 Residual Accumulation — `Λ + Θ`

Residual Accumulation may be supported where framed Non-Events, unresolved decisions, non-realized review, repeated omissions, or excluded alternatives leave persistent expectations, costs, or narrowing effects.

```text
multiple absences
≠ accumulated Non-Event residue automatically
```

Each `Λ` occurrence remains expectation- and frame-bound. Missing information cannot be converted into accumulated residue.

### 8.5 Cross-profile relations

The four profiles may interact, but their identities remain distinct:

```text
attractor sedimentation
≠ asymmetry accumulation
≠ binding accumulation
≠ residual accumulation
```

A single event or transition may contribute to several profiles, but each claim requires its own constitutive relation. No synthetic “trajectory strength” score may flatten them.

## 9. Trajectory Identity, Boundary, and Periodization

### 9.1 Trajectory identity

Trajectory identity depends on:

- the warranted Path reference;
- stable or explicitly translated Reference and Temporal Continuity;
- constitutive sedimented structures;
- present historical load;
- boundary and periodization;
- compression and exclusions;
- competing constructions;
- claim scope.

```text
same trajectory label
≠ same trajectory identity
```

A revised boundary, Path substrate, operator profile, or present analytical cut generates a new testable claim and must preserve lineage.

### 9.2 Boundary

The Trajectory boundary must answer:

- where the relevant development begins;
- which prehistory is constitutive rather than contextual;
- where the current reconstruction ends;
- whether the continuation is open, interrupted, repaired, or source-limited;
- which alternative periodizations remain plausible;
- how the boundary affects the historical-load claim.

```text
trajectory boundary
≠ objective universal boundary
```

### 9.3 Periodization

Different periodizations may produce different bounded Trajectory constructions from overlapping material. This is not arbitrary equivalence. Each periodization must carry its own Selection, Source, Continuity, Loss, Counterpressure, and Claim Ceiling.

```text
competing periodizations
≠ both equally warranted automatically
```

### 9.4 Segment lineage

A Trajectory may contain phases of reinforcement, interruption, erosion, repair, and renewed accumulation.

```text
trajectory segment
≠ complete trajectory
```

A warranted early segment does not establish later continuity. A failed later segment does not erase an earlier warranted segment. A later resumed pattern does not become uninterrupted by retroactive description.

## 11. Competing Trajectory Constructions

Competing constructions may arise from:

- different Path boundaries;
- different periodizations;
- different selections of load-bearing transitions;
- different treatment of Non-Events;
- different operator profiles;
- different present analytical cuts;
- different but defensible claims about which residue remains constitutive.

A comparison must declare shared materials, divergent selections, frame differences, evidentiary asymmetries, and claim-specific consequences.

Possible local dispositions include:

- one construction better supported;
- both admissible with bounded claims for different questions;
- partial overlap with unresolved constitutive difference;
- incomparability;
- `non_capture` where responsible adjudication is unavailable.

```text
single coherent trajectory
≠ uniquely true trajectory construction
```

A rival construction is not automatically a counterexample to every local finding. Nor may the analyst merge rival constructions merely to avoid a decision.

## 12. False Trajectory Architecture

A False Trajectory pressure arises where:

- only chronology or duration is present;
- a Path is long but carries weak present historical load;
- repetition lacks cumulative or sedimented effect;
- direction is asserted only from the endpoint backward;
- alternatives and reversals are removed;
- internal heterogeneity is compressed into one macro-label;
- operator symbols replace occurrence-level evidence;
- a frame-function, macro-event, or attractor-function is assigned without `PROJECT_AS`;
- current-condition explanations are ignored;
- one periodization is treated as uniquely true without comparison;
- historical load is equated with strong Path Dependence;
- a failed Path substrate is hidden by a stronger Trajectory label.

```text
false trajectory
≠ no history
```

The correct disposition may preserve chronology, Path segments, repeated occurrences, duration, persistence, local residue, operator profiles, or rival constructions while reducing or failing the Trajectory claim.

## 14. Failure, Stop, and Non-Capture Architecture

A Trajectory claim is under pressure where:

- the Path substrate is absent, failed, or silently revised;
- sedimentation is inferred from duration or repetition;
- persistent residue or changed action corridors are not shown;
- current historical load is replaced by narrative coherence;
- directionality is teleological, normative, or endpoint-driven;
- operator combinations are treated as deterministic formulas;
- boundary or compression removes counterpressure;
- a macro-label replaces traceable transitions and Non-Events;
- a Trajectory is used as Path Dependence, target function, causality, legitimacy, or authority proof.

Possible local dispositions remain separated:

```text
formal_diagnostic
≠ claim_reduction_required
≠ failed_transformation
≠ mandatory_stop
≠ non_capture
```

- **Formal Diagnostic:** a required relation—often Path lineage, sedimentation carrier, present historical load, directionality basis, boundary, or competing construction—remains unresolved and responsible re-entry is possible.
- **Claim Reduction Required:** a Path, repeated pattern, persistent relation, local accumulation, or bounded historical influence remains supportable while the full Trajectory claim is too strong.
- **Failed Transformation:** the delimited Trajectory claim cannot be sustained under the declared source basis, Path substrate, frame, and claim ceiling.
- **Mandatory Stop:** a known failed Trajectory is used to infer Path Dependence, causal necessity, target function, legitimacy, normative ranking, or authority.
- **Non-Capture:** materially different, source-responsible Trajectory constructions remain non-decidable under available sources.

```text
failed trajectory claim
≠ erased path
≠ erased local sedimentation finding
≠ erased rival construction
```

A later new Trajectory or Path-Dependence claim requires explicit lineage and a new test.
