## 1. Preparation Status and Scope

This record prepares Chapter 25 as the primary SUB definition site for classifying what finer resolution has actually produced. Chapters 18–24 establish when a source object may enter SUB, how granularity changes, how `DECOMPOSE` proceeds, and how occurrences, composites, temporal objects, Paths, and Trajectories may be opened. Chapter 25 does not repeat those procedures. It evaluates the result of the finer reconstruction and determines whether further decomposition remains justified.

The governing question is:

> What changed in the warranted praxis reconstruction because of finer resolution, and what follows when the answer is gain, neutrality, drift, escape, unsupported refinement, or Non-Capture?

The bounded Chapter-25 architecture is:

```text
completed or attempted finer reconstruction
+
explicit coarse claim and finer claim
+
source-supported comparison of changed and unchanged praxis dimensions
+
coarser-function, source, calibration, anti-immunization, and Stop tests
→
resolution classification and bounded continuation decision
```

Chapter 25 must resist four recurrent substitutions:

```text

→ Resolution Gain automatically

→ failure automatically

→ deeper reconstruction automatically

→ original claim repaired automatically
```

All four are prohibited. A correct finer description may be resolution-neutral. A neutral result may confirm that the coarse reconstruction was sufficient for the tested claim. Complexity may become drift rather than gain. A change of granularity may be a warranted revision only where the counterpressure is answered and the original claim disposition is retained; otherwise it is Resolution Escape.

## 3. Contract Lock

### 3.1 Required core claim

Chapter 25 must establish:

> Finer resolution counts as gain only when it changes a warranted praxis reconstruction. Correct additional detail may remain resolution-neutral, while unsupported complexity, calibration loss, source overreach, and anti-immunization failure require drift, escape, reduction, Stop, failure, or Non-Capture rather than automatic promotion.

The comparison must declare:

### 3.2 Changed-reconstruction rule

Resolution Gain requires a changed warranted reconstruction. The change may consist in:

- correcting a source category;
- narrowing or expanding a bounded claim;
- revealing a constitutive relation;
- changing an Event, Path, branch, or turning-point boundary;
- redistributing costs, exposure, access, responsibility, or binding load;
- changing an expectation or Non-Event claim;
- changing alternatives or action corridors;
- exposing previously hidden Loss;
- establishing a Stop or Non-Capture condition that the coarse account concealed.

Gain does not require practical recommendation or immediate actionability. It requires that at least one warranted statement about the reconstructed praxis must now be expressed differently.

### 3.3 Neutrality rule

Resolution Neutrality requires a valid, source-supported comparison. The added detail may be true, accurate, and appropriately bounded, yet leave the tested reconstruction unchanged. Neutrality is therefore not equivalent to:

- failure;
- unsupported refinement;
- absence of evidence;
- claim reduction;
- Non-Capture;
- mandatory Stop automatically.

A neutral result may support Optional Stop because the current claim needs no further opening. Mandatory Stop arises only where continuation would cross a binding boundary, repeat the same no-gain pattern, exceed the source basis, or become drift or escape.

### 3.4 Drift rule

Resolution Drift occurs where complexity, fragmentation, or inferential burden grows faster than relational or discriminative performance. Signals include:

- more components without clearer relations;
- more timestamps without a changed temporal claim;
- more alternative maps without comparable thresholds;
- increasing source uncertainty hidden by semantic precision;
- declining ability to reconstruct the source object or coarser function;
- analysis stabilized only by adding further analysis;
- increasingly elastic explanations that absorb every counterexample.

Drift is not the same as honest source limitation. A source limitation may be bounded and openly declared. Drift arises when the attempted finer reconstruction continues or is promoted despite insufficient purchase, support, coherence, or calibration.

### 3.5 Escape rule

Resolution Escape occurs when a challenged coarse claim is moved to a finer granularity without answering the challenge. The characteristic sequence is:

```text
counterexample or failed condition burdens coarse claim
→ analysis changes granularity or relative level
→ new local distinctions are presented
→ original burden is not answered
→ local detail is treated as confirmation of the original claim
```

A warranted revision differs sharply. It records the original claim disposition, states what the counterpressure defeated, formulates a new bounded claim, and subjects the new claim to independent testing. Escape preserves the failed claim by changing the analytical target while pretending continuity.

## 6. Resolution Gain

Resolution Gain requires both source support and claim consequence. The finer reconstruction must show a difference that matters to the tested praxis structure.

A gain may be positive even when it weakens the original claim. For example:

- a single turning point becomes a transition cluster;
- a universal Path-Dependence claim reduces to one dimension;
- one Non-Event separates into several bounded windows;
- a composite source function proves distributed rather than centralized;
- a coarse Event category becomes underdetermined;
- an inherited Loss becomes visible and forces claim reduction.

The gain lies in the improved warrant, not in strengthening the earlier assertion.

The changed-reconstruction test asks:

1. What was the warranted coarse statement?
2. What source-supported finer distinction has been added?
3. Which praxis-relevant dimension changes?
4. What must now be said differently?
5. Which part of the earlier claim is maintained, reduced, or rejected?
6. Does the finer account retain the same source reference and coarser function?

If no answer can be given to the fourth question, gain is not established.

## 7. Resolution Neutrality

Resolution Neutrality is a completed negative finding about gain, not a failure to perform the test. The finer reconstruction must be:

- source-supported;
- relationally coherent;
- correctly typed;
- comparable to the coarse reconstruction;
- bounded to the same claim;
- free of hidden Source Overreach;
- sufficiently calibrated to establish that the relevant claim remains unchanged.

Neutrality may be valuable because it:

- confirms the adequacy of the coarse claim;
- identifies non-load-bearing detail;
- prevents unnecessary complexity;
- preserves a simpler communicable representation;
- supplies a justified Optional Stop;
- records that a proposed component distinction is not constitutive.

```text
correct additional detail
+
valid comparison
+
no changed warranted reconstruction
→
Resolution Neutrality candidate
```

The result does not mean that all future finer analysis is useless. A new claim, source, Frame, or counterexample may justify re-entry.

## 8. Resolution Drift

Resolution Drift is a relation between growing analytical complexity and declining or stagnant discriminatory performance. It may occur below the Relevance Floor, above the Traceability Ceiling, or through loss of calibration between rival reconstructions.

Typical drift patterns include:

- component proliferation without source-function reconstruction;
- edge proliferation in a graph without relation warrants;
- ever finer temporal partitions with no changed claim;
- several internally coherent maps that cannot be compared because each uses a moving threshold;
- source gaps filled by increasingly elaborate internal models;
- a reconstruction that no longer preserves the source object;
- detail introduced only to stabilize another unsupported detail.

Drift may begin after an initially valid gain. The fact that earlier decomposition was admissible does not authorize indefinite continuation.

```text
earlier gain
≠ perpetual license to continue
```

## 9. Resolution Escape and Anti-Immunization

Resolution Escape is the specifically anti-immunization form of granularity change. It protects a burdened claim from failure by moving the site at which it is tested.

The required audit sequence is:

- identify the original claim;
- identify the counterexample or failed condition;
- record the original claim disposition before changing granularity;
- identify the new finer claim;
- test whether the new claim actually answers the original burden;
- prohibit transfer of local support back to the failed coarse claim without a new warranted inference.

A warranted revision is possible. It must state:

```text
original claim failed or reduced
+
new finer claim separately formulated
+
new source and comparison burden
+
no retroactive rescue
```

Changing Frame, granularity, level, composition, or target function does not erase the earlier failure.

## 10. Detail without Purchase

Detail lies below the Chapter-25 lower bound when it changes none of the tested praxis dimensions. Relevant dimensions include:

- action corridors;
- costs and exposure;
- roles and access;
- asymmetries;
- commitments and bindings;
- expectations and Non-Events;
- temporal order;
- Path structure;
- source function;
- Loss;
- Stop or Non-Capture conditions.

A detail may be interesting, accurate, or useful for another claim while remaining without purchase for the current claim. Chapter 25 therefore requires claim-relative rather than universal relevance.

## 11. Components without Coarser Function

A decomposition approaches the Traceability Ceiling when it identifies parts but can no longer reconstruct how those parts constitute, reproduce, destabilize, or revise the source object.

Warning signs include:

- every component treated as analytically independent;
- no relation between components and the coarse function;
- the original reference disappearing from the account;
- component labels derived from the model rather than the source;
- a graph that remains visually coherent even when source relations change materially;
- a finer description that cannot state which coarse claim it tests.

```text
components found
≠ source object reconstructed
```

The proper result may be drift, unsupported refinement, Failure, Mandatory Stop, or Non-Capture depending on source and claim conditions.

## 12. Source Overreach

Source Overreach occurs when the semantic or structural precision of the reconstruction exceeds the precision carried by the sources. It includes:

- filling gaps with model defaults;
- converting uncertainty into precise phases or relations;
- inferring internal mechanisms from a coarse type alone;
- assigning exact order where only coexistence is supported;
- treating missing records as Non-Events;
- deriving motive, intention, or person properties from structural delay;
- presenting one plausible internal model as established.

Source limitation is not itself overreach. Overreach occurs when the limitation is concealed, exceeded, or converted into stronger structure than the evidence supports.

## 13. Calibration Loss

Calibration Loss occurs when the analysis loses stable, comparable conditions for distinguishing gain, neutrality, drift, or failure. Signals include:

- thresholds change after each counterexample;
- every deviation receives a new local exception;
- rival decompositions use incomparable units or Frames without translation;
- no observation could reduce confidence in the preferred map;
- increasingly precise language rests on increasingly open sources;
- uncertainty cannot be represented without converting it into structure.

An open threshold is not automatically Calibration Loss. A threshold may remain explicitly provisional when:

- the current sources do not support greater precision;
- rival values remain visible;
- revision conditions are declared;
- the tested claim remains bounded;
- the model remains sensitive to counterevidence.

Calibration Loss begins where openness becomes elasticity against comparison or falsification.

## 14. Decomposition Fatigue

Decomposition Fatigue is an optional methodological marker for a source object that could still be opened further in principle, while the current analysis carries no additional structural load from doing so.

It is not:

- psychological tiredness;
- a property of the analyst;
- proof that the source is intrinsically simple;
- a permanent ban on future decomposition;
- a canonical Output Class.

It may support Optional Stop when further differentiation is admissible but unnecessary. It contributes to Mandatory Stop where continuation repeats a completed neutral result, crosses source support, loses coarser function, or becomes drift or escape.

## 20. Failure, Mandatory Stop, Non-Capture, and Re-entry

### 20.1 Failed Transformation

`failed_transformation` pressure arises where the attempted finer reconstruction:

- lacks source-supported components or relations;
- cannot be compared to the coarse claim;
- loses source identity or coarser function;
- treats more detail as gain without changed reconstruction;
- conceals source gaps as precise structure;
- becomes uncalibrated or counterfactually insensitive;
- uses a new granularity to preserve a failed claim;
- collapses local resolution result into canonical Output Class;
- infers persons, motives, legitimacy, or authority.

### 20.2 Mandatory Stop

Mandatory Stop applies before continuation where proceeding would require:

- adding distinctions below the Relevance Floor after a completed no-gain finding;
- constructing relations beyond source support;
- preserving components after the source object or coarser function is lost;
- filling source gaps with model assumptions;
- changing thresholds to absorb counterevidence;
- moving to a finer level without recording the earlier claim failure;
- recursively stabilizing one unsupported detail with another;
- converting Decomposition Fatigue into indefinite analysis;
- crossing the Source or Traceability Ceiling;
- producing person, normative, predictive, sanctioning, or authority claims.

Stop is a positive methodological result. It preserves supported findings, including a valid neutral result, and prevents recursion from becoming immunity.

### 20.3 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where the resolution question is legitimate but available sources or comparison conditions cannot responsibly distinguish among gain, neutrality, drift, unsupported refinement, or rival finer reconstructions without false precision.

```text
non_capture
≠ coarse claim confirmed
≠ finer claim confirmed
≠ compromise classification invented
≠ source limitation converted into neutrality
```

### 20.4 Re-entry

Re-entry after Optional or Mandatory Stop requires a materially new basis, such as:

- newly available sources;
- a revised bounded claim;
- a different declared Frame;
- a new counterexample;
- a new source object;
- a different comparison basis;
- a repaired relation map;
- a previously unavailable calibration threshold.

Re-entry is a new testable claim. It does not erase the earlier Stop or failure.
