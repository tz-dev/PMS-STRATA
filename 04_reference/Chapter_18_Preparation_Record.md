## 5. Part-II Entry Condition

A Chapter-18 candidate requires:

- one identifiable source reference;
- one declared origin/source type;
- one source Frame;
- one source granularity;
- one relative level;
- one bounded temporal scope where material;
- one current/coarser function or claim under test;
- known internal structure;
- unresolved internal structure;
- a source-supported reason to decompose or a reason not to decompose;

- an explicit authority boundary.

```text
more available detail

identifiable source object
+ unresolved claim-relevant internal structure
+ expected praxeological difference
+ sufficient source support
→ Chapter-18 decomposition candidate
```

Chapter 18 declares candidacy only. Chapter 20 decides whether an actual `DECOMPOSE` occurrence is admissible.

## 6. Provisional Elementarity

Provisional elementarity is relative to an analytical coordinate set. An object is treated as undivided because the current reconstruction does not need or cannot responsibly support finer differentiation.

The status must state at least:

- the Frame in which the object is treated as one;
- the granularity at which distinctions stop;
- the claim for which the object is sufficient;
- the source access that bounds what can be opened;
- the uncertainty that remains;
- the condition under which the status may be reopened.

```text
elementary-for-this-claim
≠ elementarily constituted in reality
```

Provisional elementarity can be retained after a counterexample when the counterexample attacks another claim, another Frame, or an unsupported hypothetical microstructure. It can be withdrawn when a traceable finer difference changes the current reconstruction.

## 7. The Compressed Source Object

A compressed source object is an occurrence or composite treated as an analytical unit while some internal structure remains unrepresented. Compression can be deliberate, necessary, source-forced, claim-relative, or provisional.

Eligible source categories include:

- operator-typed occurrences;
- events and structured Non-Events;
- transitions and transition clusters;
- institutional arrangements;
- Path and Trajectory objects from the provisionally locked PATH corpus;
- compositions produced by a prior `COMPOSE` occurrence;
- concrete occurrences or composites carrying a contextual function from a prior `PROJECT_AS` record, provided the next record states which retained object is opened.

Eligibility does not establish success. A candidate may remain too weakly sourced, too heterogeneous, wrongly framed, already sufficient at coarse resolution, or better treated as a competing object rather than the same reference object.

### 7.1 Compression content

Compression may leave unrepresented:

- constituent occurrences;
- internal relations;
- internal temporal ordering;
- distributed asymmetries;
- repeated reproductive practices;
- local conflicts or branch structures;
- internal carriers of a coarse function;
- source uncertainty and irrecoverable gaps.

A preparation claim may identify these as unresolved categories. It may not populate them with invented content.

## 9. Why Compression Is Necessary

Compression is a constitutive analytical operation even where no explicit `COMPOSE` record is being executed. It permits bounded comparison, temporal orientation, relation preservation, claim control, and manageable reconstruction.

Necessary compression may:

- preserve a relevant macro relation;
- prevent irrelevant atomization;
- keep uncertainty visible rather than filling gaps;
- support comparison across cases;
- preserve a source function whose carriers are not yet required;
- keep analysis within available source precision;
- allow Optional Stop where further detail has no purchase.

```text
compression
≠ defect automatically

no compression
→ possible fragmentation, calibration loss, or infinite regress
```

Chapter 18 must therefore begin from a neutral question: not “what parts are hidden?” but “is the current unit adequate for the current claim, and what traceable difference would opening it make?”

## 10. When Compression Becomes Insufficient

A coarse reconstruction becomes a legitimate pressure point when a source-supported internal difference may change the current claim, object identity, relation, burden allocation, temporal reconstruction, source-function status, or available alternative.

Typical insufficiency signals include:

- a coarse function appears without identifiable carriers;
- incompatible findings are collapsed into one unit;
- constitutive internal temporal order is omitted;
- distributed `Ω` is homogenized;
- repeated practices disappear behind a stable macro-label;
- a transition cannot be reconstructed at the current granularity;
- a coarse source function fails a counterfactual component test;
- competing internal Paths remain materially possible;
- a prior claim fails for a specifically traceable internal reason.

These are decomposition reasons only where source support and expected praxeological purchase are stated. A label such as “complex,” “emergent,” “multi-layered,” or “underexplained” is insufficient.

## 11. Reasons to Decompose

A Chapter-18 decomposition reason must state:

1. which coarse claim or function is under pressure;
2. which unresolved internal distinction is expected to matter;
3. which sources could support that distinction;
4. what change in reconstruction would count as praxeological gain;
5. what outcome would leave the coarse reconstruction unchanged;
6. what source or relevance limit would stop the attempt.

Legitimate reason families include:

- unclear formation of a stable function;
- contradictory source-supported findings inside the object;
- hidden or distributed asymmetry;
- claim-relevant internal time structure;
- insufficient explanation of a transition;
- competing internal Paths;
- source-supported failure of a coarse claim;
- need to test whether the source function is confirmed, refined, differentiated, partially preserved, rejected, or underdetermined.

```text
reason to decompose
= claim-relative expected difference
+ source route
+ stop condition

reason to decompose
≠ promise that finer structure exists as hypothesized
```

---

## 12. Reasons Not to Decompose

Not decomposing can be the correct analytical result. Chapter 18 must preserve at least five legitimate no-decomposition routes:

1. **coarse sufficiency** — the existing reconstruction already carries the current claim;
2. **no praxeological purchase** — added distinctions would not alter reconstruction or action corridor;
3. **source insufficiency** — internal relations cannot be supported responsibly;
4. **claim irrelevance** — internal structure is outside the bounded question;
5. **calibration loss** — additional detail would reduce comparability, stability, or source precision.

Additional no-decomposition grounds include:

- the proposed “parts” are fragments without relation to the source function;
- the supposed finer object is actually a competing Path or new `COMPOSE` target;
- the move is a Frame change or `PROJECT_AS`, not `DECOMPOSE`;
- the request repeats a completed neutral refinement without changed grounds;
- the attempt would cross the Relevance Floor or source/Traceability Ceiling.

```text
no decomposition warranted
≠ object proved simple
≠ object proved indivisible
≠ future reopening prohibited
```

## 13. Preservation Requirement

SUB may not replace the source object with an unstructured list of parts. The finer reconstruction must remain answerable to the same reference object and explain relations among candidate components.

The preservation burden has four parts:

- **reference continuity:** why the same source object remains under reconstruction;
- **relation continuity:** how finer structures relate to one another and to the source boundary;
- **function traceability:** how they carry, refine, differentiate, destabilize, reject, or fail to resolve the current/coarser function;
- **loss continuity:** what is preserved, compressed, excluded, uncertain, and irrecoverable across the move.

```text
component list
≠ decomposition

component relations
+ retained source reference
+ source-function test
→ DECOMPOSE candidate
```

The preservation requirement does not force confirmation. A valid later `DECOMPOSE` occurrence may reject the prior source-function claim while retaining the source object as the reconstruction target.

## 14. Source Preservation versus Source Immunization

Source preservation prevents the coarse object from disappearing silently. Source immunization protects a prior claim from the very finer evidence that the operation was opened to test.

The preparation must therefore distinguish:

- the empirical or documentary reference object;
- its origin/source type;
- the current/coarser analytical function;
- the claim status of that function;
- the possible finer carriers or counterpressure;
- the conditions under which the function may be revised or rejected.

```text
same reference object retained
≠ same source-function claim retained automatically
```

A Chapter-18 source declaration is invalid if its preservation rule makes every possible finer result compatible with the prior claim.

## 16. Source Support, Uncertainty, and Claim Ceiling

Chapter 18 must keep three kinds of uncertainty separate:

- **source uncertainty:** whether internal evidence exists or is accessible;
- **object uncertainty:** whether the same reference object can be retained under finer reconstruction;
- **function uncertainty:** whether the current/coarser function will be confirmed, revised, rejected, or remain underdetermined.

The Claim Ceiling cannot exceed the source declaration. A preparation claim may state that a decomposition test is warranted. It may not claim the internal result in advance.

```text
warrant to open
≠ warrant for a particular internal reconstruction
```

Where multiple incompatible finer reconstructions are equally source-supported, Chapter 18 preserves competing candidates for later adjudication or `non_capture`; it does not choose by narrative coherence.

## 17. Admissibility-Band Entry Test

Chapter 18 localizes the existing Admissibility Band to the decision whether an object should enter SUB.

### Relevance Floor

The proposed opening must promise a specific praxeological difference for the current claim. Curiosity, visual detail, data abundance, interpretive appetite, or desire for “deeper truth” do not pass.

### Traceability / source ceiling

The proposed internal distinctions must have a plausible source route. The coarse type, macro-label, or operator profile cannot generate hidden components by inference alone.

### Non-compensation

```text
high source volume
cannot compensate for no praxeological difference

strong expected difference
cannot compensate for unsupported internal structure
```

The Preparation Gate does not select a canonical Output Class. It only fixes the entry burdens that Chapter 18 prose must operationalize and Chapter 20 records must later test.

## 18. Stop and Non-Capture

### Optional Stop

Optional Stop is appropriate where:

- the coarse reconstruction already carries the claim;
- a prior finer test was `resolution_neutral` and no grounds changed;
- additional detail repeats the same relations;
- calibration would worsen without new purchase.

### Mandatory Stop

Mandatory Stop becomes relevant where continuation would:

- decompose an operator type;
- invent unsupported internal structure;
- use finer resolution to evade a failed claim;
- erase the source object or substitute a competing object silently;
- assign a target function without `PROJECT_AS`;
- exceed source precision while claiming semantic precision;
- create person, legitimacy, sanction, intervention, or authority conclusions.

### Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where no responsible bounded source object or single finer route can be maintained. It may not protect a weak source declaration when the correct result is simply not to decompose, reduce the claim, or stop.

```text
not enough warrant to decompose
≠ non_capture automatically
```
