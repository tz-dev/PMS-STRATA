## 7. Source Objects and Typing

Eligible sources include:

- configurations;
- transitions;
- events;
- Non-Events;
- subpaths;
- branch structures;
- partial trajectories;
- operator-typed occurrences;
- previously formed composite structures.

A source list is not sufficient. Each source needs identity, type, frame, granularity, level, source support, and its relation to the claimed target.

### 7.1 Type Integrity

`COMPOSE` does not retroactively rewrite components:

```text
source occurrence type before composition
=
source occurrence type after composition
```

A Non-Event remains `Λ` where preserved. An asymmetry-bearing occurrence remains `Ω`-typed where preserved. A component does not become a macro-event, frame, or attractor merely because it participates in a composite.

### 7.2 Source-set boundary

Source-set closure is claim-bound. The analyst must state:

- what source domain was searched;
- what temporal range was considered;
- which source families were eligible;
- which relevant sources remain unavailable or contested;
- whether later materials are used only for periodization or are improperly back-projected.

## 8. Selection Rule

Selection is an analytical decision constrained by sources and claim relevance, not passive discovery.

The record must disclose:

- why each included element is included;
- why each omitted element is omitted;
- which elements are load-bearing;
- which are illustrative or redundant;
- which omissions are contested;
- which alternatives were tested;
- what loss follows from selection.

```text
selected source
≠ only true source

omitted source
≠ irrelevant automatically
```

Selection must not be driven by the desired endpoint, macro-label, normative conclusion, or later target function.

### 8.1 Selection contestability

Where two source selections remain materially supportable, the chapter must preserve competing compositions or return `non_capture`; it may not hide contestability inside a single authoritative path.

## 9. Ordering Rule

Permissible ordering may be:

- linear;
- partially ordered;
- overlapping;
- parallel;
- cyclically constrained without circular causal proof;
- uncertain;
- explicitly retrospectively periodized.

Ordering must be distinguished from storytelling order.

```text
narrative sequence
≠ warranted temporal or structural order
```

A retrospective periodization is permitted only when the retrospective cut, source basis, uncertainty, and changed alternatives are declared. Later knowledge may clarify ordering; it may not rewrite the historical availability of alternatives.

## 11. Formation Rule and Target-Object Threshold

The formation rule explains why the target is more than a source collection.

It must state:

- what relation unifies the sources;
- what new analytical discrimination the target supports;
- which transitions, Non-Events, asymmetries, bindings, alternatives, or residues are constitutive;
- which source changes would alter the target;
- why the target class is Sequence, Path, Trajectory, branch structure, phase, or another declared composite;
- which stronger target class is not yet warranted.

```text
collection
+ label
≠ formation
```

### 11.1 Claim-specific target thresholds

- Sequence requires warranted ordering beyond a list.
- Path requires actual traversal, selective reconstruction, relations, and Loss.
- Trajectory requires sedimentation and historical load beyond Path.
- Path Dependence remains a separately tested property of historical indispensability.
- Branch closure requires Chapter-13 support for historical alternatives and closure status.

No stronger claim inherits from a weaker target class automatically.

## 12. Preservation Duty

Where constitutive to the claim, `COMPOSE` preserves:

- source lineage and typing;
- relevant temporal or structural order;
- load-bearing transitions;
- central events and Non-Events;
- asymmetries and exposure distributions;
- bindings and release conditions;
- branch points, blocked or lost alternatives, and counterfactual horizon;
- relevant heterogeneity;
- uncertainty;
- the relation between source structures and target claim.

Preservation means reconstructible dependence, not total retention.

```text
preserved
≠ copied without change
≠ lossless
```

A target that cannot be traced back to its claimed source load exceeds the Praxeological Traceability Ceiling.

## 13. Compression, Exclusion, Uncertainty, and Irrecoverable Loss

Every occurrence uses exactly the canonical Loss structure:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

### 13.1 Compressed

Compressed material remains represented at reduced resolution. The record states the compression rule and what remains recoverable from external lineage.

Typical examples:

- repeated similar events;
- fine temporal resolution;
- local variation;
- parallel minor paths;
- non-constitutive detail.

### 13.2 Excluded

Excluded material is deliberately outside the target construction. Exclusion requires a reason and does not establish falsity.

### 13.3 Uncertain

Uncertain material cannot be responsibly assigned, ordered, or interpreted at the claimed level. It remains visible rather than being converted into exclusion or irrecoverability.

### 13.4 Irrecoverable

Irrecoverable loss identifies information that cannot be reconstructed from the composite and available lineage, including:

- lost intermediate states;
- non-separable local costs;
- vanished heterogeneity;
- alternatives no longer recoverable;
- source relations destroyed by compression.

```text
compressed
≠ irrecoverable automatically

uncertain
≠ excluded
```

No composition is presumed lossless. Formal elegance cannot compensate for concealed Loss.

## 16. Counterfactual Sensitivity and Overelasticity

The central test asks:

> Would removing or materially changing a claimed load-bearing source element alter the target object or the warranted composition claim?

Possible local findings remain descriptive, not scored:

```text
strongly sensitive
partially sensitive
weakly sensitive
insensitive
underdetermined
not testable with available sources
```

### 16.1 Constitutive-element test

For each claimed constitutive element:

1. remove, replace, reorder, or reframe it in a source-bounded variation;
2. hold unrelated source conditions as stable as the record permits;
3. test whether target identity, class, boundary, or claim changes;
4. record uncertainty and changed Loss;
5. compare with at least one illustrative or exchangeable element.

### 16.2 Overelasticity

A composite is overelastic where its label and claim remain unchanged under too many material source alterations.

```text
same target label after constitutive removal
→ formation rule under pressure
```

Overelasticity may require claim reduction, competing compositions, `failed_transformation`, `mandatory_stop`, or `non_capture`.

Counterfactual sensitivity is not causal proof, prediction, or unrestricted counterfactual history.

## 17. Failure, Claim Reduction, Stop, and Non-Capture

### 17.1 Failure conditions

A Chapter-15 candidate fails or requires reduction when:

- source objects are untyped or unrelated;
- order is unsupported or narratively imposed;
- selection is endpoint-driven, teleological, or source-insensitive;
- formation is only aggregation or relabeling;
- relevant `Λ`, `Ω`, `Ψ`, branch, or alternative load disappears;
- compression destroys constitutive heterogeneity;
- the source path is no longer reconstructible;
- Loss is incomplete or falsely declared recoverable;
- counterfactual variation exposes overelasticity;
- the target creates no additional praxeological discrimination;
- a target function or authority is asserted without `PROJECT_AS`.

### 17.2 Canonical output pressure

Local findings map only to canonical classes, including:

- `admissible`;
- `admissible_with_bounded_claim`;
- `admissible_but_provisional`;
- `partially_admissible`;
- `claim_reduction_required`;
- `mandatory_stop`;
- `failed_transformation`;
- `non_capture`.

`resolution_neutral` and `analogy_only` remain available only where their canonical meanings are actually met; they are not generic uncertainty labels.

### 17.3 Mandatory Stop

`mandatory_stop` applies where continuation would:

- conceal source or Loss gaps;
- convert chronology into Path by label;
- erase Non-Events, asymmetry, binding, or alternatives;
- manufacture target function or authority;
- use graph density or record completeness as proof;
- reuse a known failed composition claim in sanctions, diagnosis, irreversible classification, or automatic action.

### 17.4 Non-Capture

`non_capture` remains available where multiple materially different compositions remain source-supported and cannot responsibly be adjudicated, or where the source field cannot support a single formation without destructive reduction.

Non-Capture does not protect a weak or convenient claim.
