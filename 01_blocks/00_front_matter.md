# PMS-STRATA

## Granularity, Trajectory, Composition, and Cross-Level Functional Projection in PMS

> **Front Matter status:** canonical orientation layer aligned to the current corpus. It records bounded Part status and declared residual obligations without altering theory, Part lock status, or the authority of PMS Base.

---

## Abstract

PMS-STRATA specifies a bounded discipline for transforming PMS reconstructions across temporal structures, granularities, composites, relative levels, and contextual functions. It addresses a methodological problem that can remain implicit even in otherwise careful analysis: a reconstruction may move from local occurrences to a composite path, from a compressed object to a finer relational account, or from an origin-typed source object to a function attributed within another context without explicitly declaring that the analytical position and therefore the claim have changed.

STRATA makes such movement inspectable through exactly three core operations:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

`COMPOSE` forms a new composite analytical object from multiple or sequential source structures. `DECOMPOSE` opens a provisionally compressed occurrence or composite under finer granularity while retaining the burden of reconstructing the same reference object. `PROJECT_AS` attributes a bounded contextual target function to an origin-typed source object without replacing its origin type. These operations may be repeated and linked in declared chains, but every occurrence remains a separate testable claim with its own source, target, coordinates, admissibility burden, Loss profile, result, and Stop conditions.

The operations do not create a new PMS grammar. PMS Base remains the sole source of the Δ–Ψ operator inventory, canonical functions, dependency order, and application guardrails. STRATA transforms reconstructions made with that grammar; it does not decompose, revise, rename, reorder, or supersede the operator types themselves. Its governing reduction is therefore constitutive rather than rhetorical:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

A larger composite is not automatically more valid than its sources. A finer reconstruction is not automatically truer. A higher relative level is not an ontological layer. A successful contextual projection does not create a new origin type. A formally valid record does not establish empirical truth, actual causality, semantic adequacy, normative validity, or application permission.

Every transformation is assessed within the STRATA Admissibility Band. Below the Praxeological Relevance Floor, additional distinction lacks warranted praxis-relevant purchase. Above the Praxeological Traceability Ceiling, abstraction no longer carries reconstructible load from its sources. Between these bounds, admissibility remains non-compensatory: formal elegance, detail, or model consistency cannot replace source support, type integrity, reference and functional continuity, contextual boundedness, counterfactual sensitivity, disclosed Loss, claim discipline, and an available Stop.

STRATA therefore preserves unsuccessful and bounded results as genuine outcomes. Its ten canonical Output Classes include admissible forms, bounded or provisional forms, resolution neutrality, analogy-only findings, partial admissibility, claim reduction, mandatory stop, failed transformation, and non-capture. A change of frame, granularity, relative level, composite, or target function does not erase a previously failed claim. A later transformation is a new claim and must be tested as such.

The corpus combines canonical prose, chapter and block contracts, minified control artifacts, a formal model, transformation records, cases and countercases, reference indexes, and appendices. These layers do not carry equal authority. The prose and controls specify the method; records and cases instantiate and test it; the formal model checks declared structure and consistency; reference files support lookup and navigation; derivatives and the Reader remain downstream representations without backward authority.

PMS-STRATA does not exhaust second- or third-order analysis of the PMS ecosystem. It does not decide how every PMS project, lens, corpus, implementation, or workflow relates to every other one. It supplies the transformation discipline under which such later studies can declare their objects, movements, losses, limits, and claims more explicitly.

```text
STRATA does not close the PMS ecosystem.
It makes later second-order work less arbitrary
and more inspectable.
```

Within the present STRATA grammar, every admitted transformation must be typed as `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, `PROJECT_AS`, or a finite declared chain of their occurrences. This is a closed methodological vocabulary, not a proved theorem that every conceivable PMS-relevant transformation is representable by those three operations. Non-capture remains possible, and any proposal for a fourth core operation would require explicit corpus-level revision rather than informal local extension.

## Preface

### The problem from which STRATA emerged

PMS-STRATA emerged from repeated analytical situations in which a PMS reconstruction remained locally intelligible but its movement to another analytical position did not. A set of occurrences could be narrated as a path without an explicit selection rule. A trajectory could be treated as a larger event without showing which relations carried that function. A compressed structure could be opened into finer components without demonstrating that the same object was still being reconstructed. A source object could be described as functioning as a frame, attractor, asymmetry, or modulating profile in another context without preserving the distinction between its established origin type and the attributed target function.

None of these movements is inherently illegitimate. Many are indispensable. The methodological problem begins when the movement remains unmarked.

An unmarked transformation can conceal several changes at once:

- the reference object may have changed;
- the frame may have shifted;
- the temporal scope may have widened;
- the granularity may have become finer or coarser;
- a part may have become a composite;
- an origin-typed object may have acquired a contextual function;
- exclusions and uncertainty may have disappeared from view;
- the resulting claim may have inherited more authority than its source permits.

The target statement can then appear to follow naturally from its source even where the actual analytical movement would require a new claim, new evidence, and a new limit.

STRATA was developed to make that movement visible.

```text
implicit analytical movement
→ hidden change of claim

explicit transformation occurrence
→ new testable claim with declared burden
```

The project therefore begins neither from dissatisfaction with the PMS operator grammar nor from a desire to add a higher-order theory above it. It begins from a need for disciplined transformations within reconstructions already governed by PMS Base.

### Specification rather than extension of the base grammar

PMS Base supplies the operator grammar. STRATA does not supply another one.

The relationship is deliberately asymmetric:

```text
PMS Base
→ governing Δ–Ψ grammar, dependencies, and guardrails

PMS-STRATA
→ bounded transformation discipline for PMS reconstructions
```

This means that the Δ–Ψ operators retain their names, order, canonical functions, and dependencies. STRATA does not decompose an operator type as though it were an empirical composite. It does not promote a path, trajectory, macro-event, frame-function, attractor-function, profile, or higher-level function into a new PMS primitive. It does not convert an attributed target function into a replacement origin type.

The project expands the explicit method available for handling analytical movement. It does not expand the ontology, operator inventory, evidential warrant, or application authority of PMS.

That distinction is especially important because increased formal structure can be mistaken for increased theoretical authority. STRATA has a large corpus, explicit contracts, machine-readable schemas, decision routes, operation records, case packages, Output Classes, Loss fields, and audit procedures. These artifacts improve inspectability. Their existence does not prove that a reconstruction is true, complete, causally adequate, normatively valid, or entitled to guide action.

```text
more explicit
≠ more true

more formal
≠ more authoritative

more navigable
≠ more complete
```

### Ambition and restraint

The ambition of STRATA is substantial but bounded. It seeks to make the movement of a PMS claim itself an object of scientific and methodological scrutiny.

A transformation is not treated as a neutral bridge between two finished claims. It carries selections, assumptions, preserved relations, compressions, exclusions, uncertainties, and possible irrecoverable losses. It can change what counts as the object, which relations become constitutive, and what claim can be supported. For this reason, the transformation must be inspectable in its own right.

The project therefore requires the transformation route itself to remain inspectable:

```text
source object
→ declared analytical coordinates
→ operation occurrence
→ selection and constitutive relations
→ Loss
→ target object or function
→ admissibility result
→ bounded claim
```

This provenance is not a proof of the result. It is a condition of being able to inspect, compare, criticize, reproduce, revise, stop, or reject the movement that produced it.

The corresponding restraint is equally strong. STRATA does not claim to capture every possible praxis object, resolve every ambiguity, eliminate every competing reconstruction, or produce a universal scale of admissibility. It preserves the possibility that a transformation is unnecessary, neutral, only analogical, partially admissible, claim-reducing, stopped, failed, or non-capturing.

A method that could only return success would violate the very discipline it seeks to establish.

### The three operations and their chains

STRATA recognizes exactly three core operations. Their full definitions and signatures belong to Chapter 4 and the operation-specific Parts, not to this Preface.

At orientation level:

```text
COMPOSE
many or sequential source structures
→ new composite analytical object
```

```text
DECOMPOSE
compressed occurrence or composite
→ finer reconstruction of the same reference object
```

```text
PROJECT_AS
origin-typed source object
→ bounded contextual target function
```

The operations can recur and form chains. A composed object may be decomposed. A decomposition may supply components for a new composition. A source object may be projected and later reopened through its retained origin structure. A three-stage chain may compose, project, and then test whether the source structure can be reconstructed at a finer resolution.

No chain becomes a fourth operation. Every edge remains one operation occurrence. Every handoff must preserve its own type, reference, Loss, claim, and result. Earlier failure, Stop, or non-capture remains part of the chain history and cannot be averaged away by later success.

The current case corpus instantiates all six required minimum chain families:

```text
COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS
COMPOSE → DECOMPOSE
DECOMPOSE → COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE → PROJECT_AS
PROJECT_AS → DECOMPOSE
COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS → DECOMPOSE
```

These cases test the architecture. They do not prove a general mathematical completeness theorem.

### STRATA and later PMS ecosystem research

PMS exists within a broader ecosystem of theoretical, methodological, implementation, corpus, workflow, and lens projects. STRATA does not attempt to perform every comparative analysis among them.

Questions such as the relation between particular add-ons, lenses, corpora, implementations, or downstream projects can be legitimate and important. They are not automatically part of the foundational STRATA task. STRATA contributes something prior and narrower: it makes the transformation used in such comparisons more explicit.

A later ecosystem study can therefore ask:

- What are the source objects being compared?
- Are they composed into a new comparative object?
- Is one object decomposed under another granularity?
- Is an origin-typed object projected into a contextual function?
- Which losses are introduced by the comparison?
- Which result is structural, analogical, provisional, failed, or non-capturing?
- Which authority does the comparison not inherit?

STRATA does not answer every one of those future studies. It provides the discipline under which their movements can be inspected.

```text
Ebenendisziplin
≠ totale Ebenenwissenschaft
```

Or in the language used throughout the corpus:

```text
transformation discipline
≠ exhaustive ecosystem metatheory
```

### Status of the present corpus

The present corpus contains canonical prose for Chapters 0–57, three final bounded artifact-complete locks for RETYPE, LIMITS, and Conclusion, and a corpus-wide integration of those lock surfaces. Foundations, PATH, and SUB retain bounded provisional locks. The case repository contains 59 case operation Records, all ten canonical Output Classes, and all six required minimum operation-chain families.

Two case-coverage obligations are explicitly partial rather than missing: a same-source PATH boundary package for Chapter 16 and a same-source four-result resolution package for Chapter 25. Their partial status is visible and remains a bounded residual obligation within the PATH and SUB audit surfaces.

This Front Matter orients to the canonical prose and bounded lock statuses of the Parts. It does not certify independent completion of Appendices, Reference, Model, derivative, Reader, corpus-audit, or release layers.

The distinction matters:

```text
canonical Front Matter orientation
≠ independent completion of support or release layers
```

The Front Matter may orient the reader to what exists. It may not certify completion of independently governed artifacts.

## Status and Scope Note

### Governing authority order

PMS-STRATA must be read through its explicit authority order. More detailed, technical, or later artifacts do not automatically outrank earlier governing sources.

```text

→ terminology, indexes, navigation, and audit support

06_derivative_publications/*
→ downstream publications without backward authority

08_PMS-STRATA Reader/*

```

`_workfiles/**` is outside this authority order. It is a non-authoritative production-provenance archive and is excluded by default from active interpretation, evidence routing, Reference Freeze, Corpus Audit, Model Finalization, derivatives, and Reader ingestion. A historical pointer into the archive records provenance only; it does not create an active dependency.

This order is functional rather than a hierarchy of truth. It determines which artifact may define, constrain, instantiate, summarize, or display which content.

A model may detect a missing required field. It cannot rewrite the concept that gives the field meaning. A case may pressure a rule and expose a gap. It cannot silently become a new governing rule. A Reader may visualize dependencies or operation paths. It cannot generate new dependencies or transform a visual pattern into a theoretical finding.

### Positive scope

PMS-STRATA specifies controlled transformation among declared analytical positions. Its positive scope includes:

- temporal and structural composition of source occurrences into declared composites;
- finer reconstruction of compressed occurrences and composites;
- bounded contextual functional projection while preserving origin type;
- operation chains in which each occurrence remains separately typed and audited;
- disclosure of preserved, compressed, excluded, uncertain, and irrecoverable material;
- tests for praxis purchase and source-traceable load;
- reference, type, functional, temporal, Loss, and authority continuity;
- source, calibration, claim, and authority ceilings;
- alternative transformations and counterfactual sensitivity;
- Claim Reduction, Stop, Failure, and Non-Capture;
- machine-readable records and schemas for declared consistency and conformance.

This scope is methodological. STRATA can establish that a transformation was adequately declared, that its type boundaries were preserved, that its Loss was disclosed, that its source trace was sufficient for the bounded claim, or that the transformation must stop or fail. It cannot establish empirical truth merely by structural success.

### Negative scope

PMS-STRATA is not:

- a new PMS Base;
- a superior meta-PMS;
- a fourth operator layer;
- an ontology of micro, meso, macro, or other strata;
- a universal theory of scale;
- a mechanism for decomposing the Δ–Ψ operator types;
- a proof that all PMS-relevant transformation is representable by three operations;
- a lossless or automatically invertible transformation system;
- an unlimited decomposition method;
- an automatic generator of macro-objects or higher functions;
- a mechanism for rescuing failed claims through reframing;
- a person-typing, diagnostic, moral-ranking, forensic, legal, political, or governance engine;
- a source of application authority;
- an empirical validation of PMS Base;
- a completed theory of every relation within the PMS ecosystem.

The negative scope is not a list of secondary cautions. It defines the boundary within which the positive method is intelligible.

### No ontology of layers

STRATA uses frame, granularity, relative level, part, composite, source, and target as analytical coordinates and relations. They do not describe objectively discrete layers of reality.

An object may be a component relative to one composite and a composite relative to another. The relation is declared locally. Finer granularity may expose more distinctions without revealing more fundamental reality. A larger composition may be useful without becoming a more real whole.

Micro, meso, and macro may be used only as local relational shorthand. They cannot function as universal classes or sources of rank.

```text
relative level
≠ ontological layer

granularity
≠ depth of reality

composition
≠ discovery of an ultimate whole
```

### No authority inheritance

The governance field of every Shared Transformation Record preserves:

```yaml
authority_inheritance: prohibited
```

This prohibition applies even to a successful transformation. The source claim may carry empirical, interpretive, or other bounded warrant established elsewhere. STRATA does not increase that warrant by composing, decomposing, projecting, formalizing, validating, or visualizing the claim.

The distinction between Claim Ceiling and Authority Ceiling is central. A Claim Ceiling limits what the transformation itself may assert. The Authority Ceiling blocks kinds of authority that the method cannot license even when the structural claim succeeds.

```text
successful transformation
≠ permission to intervene

higher contextual function
≠ higher authority

finer reconstruction
≠ stronger judgment
```

### Person, psychology, and judgment boundary

STRATA reconstructs praxis structures. It does not determine what a person essentially is, diagnose inner states, or convert configuration-level findings into person-level identities.

```text
configuration performs asymmetry-function
≠ person is asymmetry
```

The corpus therefore prohibits:

- person-level essence claims derived from structural functions;
- clinical or psychological diagnosis;
- irreversible labels;
- automated moral judgment;
- ranking human beings;
- legal or political legitimacy decisions;
- sanctions or automatic action recommendations;
- authority inheritance from formal or structural success.

Any later application remains subject to the independent evidence, ethics, governance, competence, reversibility, and accountability requirements of its actual domain.

### Add-ons, lenses, and neighboring projects

Add-on lenses and neighboring PMS projects may be used as optional future test vectors. They do not define STRATA rules and are not architectural dependencies unless explicitly versioned into the governing corpus at a later stage.

This separation protects both STRATA and the ecosystem. STRATA does not absorb every later comparative question. Later projects can use STRATA to declare their transformations without becoming sources of retroactive authority over the STRATA grammar.

```text
optional test vector
≠ theoretical foundation

later ecosystem study
≠ hidden STRATA chapter
```

### Closed operation vocabulary and open research boundary

The present grammar recognizes three and only three core operations. Any admitted transformation must be expressed as one of them or as a finite declared chain of their occurrences.

This is an architectural closure rule:

```text
admitted STRATA transformation
→ COMPOSE | DECOMPOSE | PROJECT_AS | declared finite chain
```

It is not a proof of representational completeness:

```text
closed operation vocabulary
≠ theorem that every conceivable transformation is representable
```

A future formal research program may investigate whether a representation theorem can be stated and proved. Until then, the stronger claim remains outside the corpus boundary. A candidate movement that cannot be typed within the existing grammar is not licensed by analogy. It would require explicit revision, new contracts, new cases, new boundary tests, and a new version of the corpus.

### Formal model and software boundary

The formal model can test declarations such as:

- whether Loss fields use the canonical five-field structure;
- whether authority inheritance is prohibited;
- whether a record follows a declared route;
- whether schema and registry references are internally consistent.

It cannot automatically decide:

- whether the sources are true;
- whether a causal relation actually exists;
- whether a semantic interpretation is correct;
- whether a normative judgment is valid;
- whether a person should be classified;
- whether an intervention is justified;
- whether the object has been exhaustively captured.

The same boundary applies to the Reader and future graph or 3D-path visualizations.

```text
visualization
→ increased comparability and traceability

visualization
≠ evidence
≠ automatic finding
≠ stronger claim
```

A visualized branch is not automatically a historically available branch. A rendered path is not automatically an admissible path. A graph edge must be supported by a declared operation occurrence, source relation, or navigation dependency before it can be displayed as such.

## Terminology and Notation Note

### Language convention

The canonical corpus uses English operation names and most technical terms because they also appear as machine-readable values in records, registries, schemas, and decision routes. Explanatory prose may use ordinary language around them, but canonical names and enum values should not be casually translated or replaced.

The three operation names are always:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

The four Parts are:

```text
PATH
SUB
RETYPE
LIMITS
```

`LIMITS` is a Part and an integrated admissibility discipline. It is not a fourth operation and not a meta-level above the other Parts.

### Project name

`PMS-STRATA` is the project name. Where an informal mnemonic expansion is useful in orientation or derivative contexts, it may be read as:

```text
Systematic Transformational Reconstruction
and Traceability Analysis
```

This expansion is not a new canonical definition, an official replacement title, or an additional claim. The governing meaning of STRATA is supplied by the corpus itself.

### Δ–Ψ notation

The Δ–Ψ operator signs, names, functions, order, and dependencies are inherited unchanged from PMS Base. The current repository-reference `PMS.yaml` is the governing source.

A sign may refer to an operator type in the formal grammar. A concrete reconstruction may contain an operator-typed occurrence. Several occurrences and their relations may form a composite. These are not interchangeable.

```text
operator sign
≠ operator name
≠ operator type
≠ operator occurrence
≠ composite
```

The distinction is indispensable because STRATA may transform occurrences and composites, but it does not decompose or rewrite the base operator types.

### Source, target, and transformation context

In a transformation record, *source* identifies the declared input object and its coordinates. *Target* identifies the object or contextual function produced or tested by the operation. *Transformation context* identifies the conditions under which the movement is being claimed.

Source and target are not value rankings. Target does not mean more advanced, larger, truer, or more authoritative. A target may fail, require claim reduction, or remain uncaptured.

```text
source → target
≠ lower → higher truth
```

### Frame, granularity, and relative level

These terms retain the definitions supplied by Chapter 2 and must remain distinct.

- **Frame** identifies the contextual boundary within which the reconstruction is made.
- **Granularity** identifies the resolution of distinctions used in the reconstruction.
- **Relative level** identifies an object's position within a declared compositional or functional relation.

A change in one does not automatically imply a change in the others.

```text
frame
≠ granularity
≠ relative level
```

The terms *micro*, *meso*, and *macro* are permitted only as local relational shorthand where the comparison is declared. They do not name universal classes.

### Temporal vocabulary

The corpus distinguishes configuration, event, Non-Event, transition, sequence, path, trajectory, and path dependence. Their canonical definitions belong to Chapter 3 and PATH.

At orientation level, the reader should preserve the following non-equivalences:

```text
missing information
≠ Non-Event

sequence
≠ path

path
≠ trajectory

trajectory
≠ path dependence
```

A visual or narrative ordering of occurrences does not by itself establish a path. A path does not automatically establish a trajectory. A trajectory does not automatically establish that earlier states constrained later possibilities strongly enough for path dependence.

### Operation occurrence and operation chain

An operation type is one of the three canonical signatures. An operation occurrence is one specific, source-bound and context-bound use of that operation. A chain is an ordered series of declared occurrences.

```text
operation type
≠ operation occurrence
≠ operation chain
```

Each occurrence in a chain receives its own record and local result. The chain does not average its components into one undifferentiated success or failure.

### Origin type and target function

`PROJECT_AS` requires the distinction:

```text
origin type
≠ target function
```

The target function is bounded by the declared target context. It does not replace the source object's origin type and does not become a new PMS primitive.

```text
functions as Α in context C
≠ is Α as a new origin type
```

### Formulas and formal expressions

Equations and symbolic forms in the corpus are specifications, guards, compact relations, or schematic statements. They are not empirical laws unless a separate domain-specific study establishes such a status.

For example:

```text
Admissible(T, X, C)
iff
PraxisPurchase
and TraceableLoad
and TypeIntegrity
and ContextualBoundedness
```

This expression states the minimal non-compensatory structure of admissibility. It does not provide a universal numeric scoring function, automated truth condition, or domain-independent calibration scale.

Likewise:

```text
DECOMPOSE(COMPOSE(X)) ≠ X
```

states the absence of presumed lossless inversion. It does not claim that no information can ever be recovered or that every chain must fail.

### Loss notation

The canonical Loss structure contains exactly five fields:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

These fields disclose the epistemic and structural burden of the transformation. They are not a numerical utility function and do not require every field to contain loss in every case. `preserved` belongs to the same disclosure structure because continuity must be explicit rather than presumed.

### Output Classes

STRATA uses exactly ten canonical Output Classes:

```text
admissible
admissible_with_bounded_claim
admissible_but_provisional
resolution_neutral
analogy_only
partially_admissible
claim_reduction_required
mandatory_stop
failed_transformation
non_capture
```

They are result classes, not rankings of objects, persons, theories, or moral worth. Local findings must be mapped to one of these classes. Optional Stop is a legitimate procedural result after sufficiency but is not an eleventh Output Class.

No class should be inferred from a label alone. The record must preserve the route, rule findings, source basis, Loss, alternatives, and reason for the selected class.

### Stop, Failure, and Non-Capture

These terms must remain distinct.

- **Mandatory Stop** means that continuing the transformation under the current claim would be inadmissible.
- **Failed transformation** means that the declared operation does not carry its transformation claim.
- **Non-Capture** means that multiple adequate bounded attempts fail to produce an adequate retained form for the capture claim, while their partial captures and unresolved remainder remain explicit.

```text
mandatory_stop
≠ failed_transformation
≠ non_capture
```

Non-Capture is not a shield for a weak claim. Failure is not repaired by changing the analytical position. Stop is a positive methodological result where continuation would be illegitimate.

### Capitalization and code formatting

Canonical enum values and operation names are shown in code formatting where useful. Ordinary conceptual terms may be capitalized where the corpus treats them as named controls, such as Non-Capture, Claim Ceiling, Authority Ceiling, Praxeological Relevance Floor, and Praxeological Traceability Ceiling.

Readers should consult:

- `04_reference/Glossary.md` for term lookup;
- `04_reference/Operator_Index.md` for PMS operator references;
- `04_reference/Transformation_Operation_Index.md` for operation ownership and routes;
- `04_reference/Non_Equivalence_Index.md` for protected distinctions;
- `04_reference/Output_Class_Index.md` for result classes;
- `02_appendices/Appendix_B_Formal_Notation.md` for expanded notation after Appendix finalization.

The Reference and Appendix layers assist navigation and technical use. They do not replace the canonical definitions in the main corpus.

```text
PMS Base remains the governing operator grammar.
```

```text

≠ more authority
```

These rules determine how every later section should be interpreted. STRATA is not a ladder from local uncertainty to higher certainty. It is a discipline for declaring and testing analytical movement.

PATH studies temporal and structural composition. It develops configurations, events, Non-Events, transitions, ordering, branch status, historical alternatives, paths, trajectories, path dependence, periodization, omission, and the operation `COMPOSE`.

Read PATH when the analytical question concerns how multiple or sequential source structures form a new composite object.

Do not enter PATH with the assumption that chronology is already a path, that a path is already a trajectory, or that a trajectory is already path-dependent. The Part is organized precisely to prevent those automatic escalations.

The PATH local audit preserves the possibility that a temporal composition is admissible, bounded, provisional, reduced, stopped, failed, or uncaptured.

#### SUB — Chapters 18–28

SUB studies decomposition under finer granularity. It develops the conditions under which a provisionally compressed occurrence or composite may be opened while retaining the same reference object.

Read SUB when the analytical question concerns internal structure, finer relations, hidden temporal organization, or a narrower reconstruction of a composite.

Do not enter SUB with the assumption that finer resolution is more truthful or more relevant. A decomposition may produce resolution gain, resolution neutrality, resolution drift, resolution escape, or mandatory stop.

SUB applies to occurrences and composites, not to the abstract PMS operator types.

#### RETYPE — Chapters 29–40

RETYPE studies bounded contextual functional projection through `PROJECT_AS`.

Read RETYPE when an origin-typed object is claimed to perform a function in another declared context or relative-level relation.

The central discipline is:

```text
origin type
≠ target function
```

RETYPE includes trajectories functioning as macro-events or frames, recurrent forms functioning as attractors, composite asymmetry functions, operator-weighting profiles, competing projections, analogy boundaries, invalid type jumps, person-level type-jump failures, and the local audit.

The Part is not full retyping. Its name describes a method region in the project architecture, while the operation itself preserves origin type.

#### LIMITS — Chapters 41–53

LIMITS systematizes the constraints that already apply locally throughout Foundations, PATH, SUB, and RETYPE. It is not an external tribunal and not a fourth operation.

Read LIMITS when assessing:

- why transformation must be bounded;
- ontology and scale errors;
- the Relevance Floor and Traceability Ceiling;
- counterfactual sensitivity;
- continuity;
- Loss;
- Source and Calibration Ceilings;
- anti-immunization;
- Stop and Re-entry;
- Non-Capture;
- the integrated twelve-stage audit.

Readers who approach only one operation should still return to LIMITS because no local operation procedure exhausts the cross-cutting boundary conditions.

#### Conclusion — Chapters 54–57

Conclusion integrates the four Parts, states what STRATA provides, states what it does not provide, and closes the final claim boundary.

Read Conclusion after the substantive Parts. It is not a substitute for them and introduces no new theory. Its function is to restate the integrated architecture, preserve bounded capability, and prevent the corpus from acquiring claims it has not earned.

### Chain-reading route

Operation chains must be read as linked records, not as one compressed transformation.

For each edge, inspect:

1. the source object and declared coordinates;
2. the operation occurrence;
3. the target object or function;

The minimum chain corpus provides six route families. These are especially useful for understanding non-invertibility and claim preservation.

```text
later edge succeeds
```


### Final orientation

PMS-STRATA asks the reader to inspect not only what a reconstruction claims, but how it moved from one analytical position to another.

The recurring questions are:

- What is the source object?
- Which coordinates are declared?
- Which operation occurrence is being attempted?
- What becomes the target?
- Which relations are constitutive?
- What is preserved, compressed, excluded, uncertain, or irrecoverable?
- Which alternatives were considered?
- Does the result remain within the Relevance Floor and Traceability Ceiling?
- Which claim is actually supported?
- Where must the transformation stop?
- What remains failed or uncaptured?
- Which authority is explicitly not inherited?

A reader who keeps these questions visible will encounter STRATA as intended: not as a machine for producing higher claims, but as a discipline for making analytical movement more accountable.
