## Abstract

Analytical reconstructions often gain detail, scale, internal differentiation, or contextual reach as inquiry proceeds. Such gains are methodologically useful, but they are frequently accompanied by an illicit transfer of authority: a larger composite is treated as a more complete history, a finer reconstruction as a truer account, or a higher-level function as a new and more fundamental type. The resulting error can be summarized as the inference from **more structure** to **more truth, causality, ontology, or authority**.

PMS-STRATA addresses this problem as a bounded transformation discipline within the unchanged Praxeological Meta-Structure (PMS). It distinguishes exactly three operations. `COMPOSE` forms a new composite analytical object from multiple or sequential sources. `DECOMPOSE` reconstructs a compressed occurrence or composite under finer granularity while retaining the same reference burden. `PROJECT_AS` assigns an origin-typed source object a bounded contextual target function without replacing its origin type. These operations are non-collapsible because they move in different directions and carry different duties of disclosure, continuity, Loss accounting, and failure.

Their admissibility is constrained by a band between a Praxeological Relevance Floor and a Praxeological Traceability Ceiling. A transformation below the Floor adds distinction without praxeological purchase; one above the Ceiling produces abstraction, fragmentation, or projection without reconstructible source load. Formal elegance and additional detail cannot compensate for missing relevance, traceability, type integrity, reference continuity, or contextual boundedness. Every operation occurrence therefore constitutes a separately testable claim and remains subject to claim reduction, Stop, failure preservation, rival reconstruction, and Non-Capture.

Selected cases show three non-interchangeable boundary outcomes: an untraceable macro-composition fails, an overfine decomposition reaches mandatory Stop, and a label-substitution projection fails. Chain cases further show that later admissibility does not repair prior failure and that Non-Capture can be a bounded positive result.

The paper argues that temporal and structural reconstructions can be composed, decomposed, and contextually projected without converting analytical mobility into epistemic or practical privilege. The contribution is methodological rather than ontological: STRATA increases the explicitness, auditability, and criticizability of transformation while preserving the governing limitation that **more structure is not more authority**.

**Keywords:** Praxeological Meta-Structure; PMS-STRATA; composition; decomposition; functional projection; analytical granularity; traceability; praxeological relevance; claim discipline; non-capture

# 1. Introduction: The Authority-Inflation Problem

## 1.1 Analytical mobility and hidden claim transfer

Praxis reconstructions rarely remain at one analytical position. Separate occurrences may be ordered into a sequence and then treated as a path or trajectory. An apparently unitary event, non-event, or composite may be opened into finer internal relations. An object established in one frame may be assigned a function in another context. These moves are often necessary: without them, analysis would be unable to reconstruct temporal formation, expose concealed internal structure, or compare how the same source object functions under changed conditions.

The methodological problem begins when the movement is performed without an explicit account of what changed. A description that starts with several source structures may end with a single macro-label whose constitutive relations are no longer reconstructible. A finer analysis may multiply distinctions while silently replacing the original reference object. A contextual function may be narrated as though it revealed what the source object “really is.” In each case, the target claim appears to inherit support from the source even though the transformation has changed the object, resolution, relation, or function under analysis.

This hidden transfer creates **authority inflation**. The structure becomes larger, finer, more formal, or more encompassing, and the resulting claim is treated as stronger merely because of that structural expansion. The recurrent pattern is:

```text
more structure
→ falsely treated as
more truth / causality / ontology / authority
```

The error is not confined to one direction of analysis. Composition can inflate comprehensiveness; decomposition can inflate epistemic depth; contextual projection can inflate type or functional necessity. Formalization can add a fourth pressure by making a claim appear validated simply because its fields are complete or its schema is satisfied. The common defect is not “too much complexity” as such. It is the failure to distinguish increased analytical legibility from increased warrant.

PMS-STRATA begins from the opposite principle:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

This principle does not prohibit wider composites, finer reconstructions, or higher-order contextual functions. It denies that such transformations are self-justifying. Each movement must expose its source dependence, analytical coordinates, preservation duties, transformation Loss, claim scope, and reasons for continuing. A transformation that is available is not thereby admissible, and an admissible transformation does not thereby acquire empirical, causal, normative, diagnostic, legal, political, or application authority.

## 1.2 Why transformation requires its own method

PMS Base supplies the unchanged Δ–Ψ operator grammar through which praxis structures are reconstructed. STRATA does not add to that inventory, reorder its dependencies, or stand above it as a validating meta-system. Its object is narrower: the controlled movement **between reconstructions made with PMS**.

That distinction is necessary because continuity of vocabulary does not guarantee continuity of claim. An analysis may use the same operator terms before and after a shift while changing from a local occurrence to a composite, from a compressed object to a finer reconstruction, or from an origin-typed source to a contextual function. Without a transformation discipline, the use of stable terminology can conceal a substantive change in analytical burden.

STRATA therefore treats every transformation occurrence as a new, explicit, and criticizable claim. It asks at least four questions:

1. **What source object is being transformed?**  
   The source must be typed and bounded rather than inferred retrospectively from the desired result.

2. **Which operation is occurring?**  
   Composition, decomposition, and contextual projection cannot be collapsed into a generic “change of level,” because they preserve and alter different relations.

3. **What must remain continuous, and what is lost?**  
   Reference, origin type, source function, historical load, or constitutive relations may be preserved, compressed, excluded, uncertain, or irrecoverable in different ways.

4. **What claim is actually licensed?**  
   The target statement may require reduction, Stop, failure, or Non-Capture even when the transformation is formally describable.

The method thus shifts attention from the attractiveness of a transformed result to the burden carried by the transformation itself. The target is not accepted because it is more elegant, more detailed, or more useful. It is accepted only to the extent that its relation to the source remains praxeologically relevant, traceable, type-integral, contextually bounded, and claim-specific.

## 1.3 Contribution and scope

The paper develops one focused thesis:

> Temporal and structural reconstructions can be composed, decomposed, and contextually projected without treating increased structure, finer resolution, or higher-level function as increased truth, ontology, or authority.

The contribution has three parts. First, it separates three non-collapsible transformation operations: `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, and `PROJECT_AS`. Second, it places them inside an Admissibility Band in which praxeological relevance and source traceability are jointly necessary and non-compensatory. Third, it preserves bounded negative and terminal outcomes—claim reduction, Stop, failed transformation, and Non-Capture—so that analytical mobility cannot become a mechanism for escaping objection or laundering prior failure.

The argument remains structurally bounded. It does not establish a universal scale of analysis, a hierarchy of real strata, final constituents, an ultimate whole, causal proof, person-level typing, or automatic validity across domains. It does not claim that every source admits a successful transformation. It also does not infer application authority from method, corpus size, formal consistency, or implementation detail.

The next section introduces only the object and coordinate distinctions necessary to state the problem precisely. Later sections compare the three operation burdens, develop the Admissibility Band, examine three failure families, and show how continuity, Loss, chains, Stop, Non-Capture, and formalization preserve the governing boundary.

# 2. Controlled Transformation as a Methodological Problem

## 2.1 Transformations operate on declared analytical objects

A method of structural transformation must first identify what can be transformed. STRATA therefore distinguishes the abstract PMS operator grammar from the concrete and derived objects reconstructed through it.

An **operator type** is one of the unchanged Δ–Ψ functions defined by PMS Base. An **operator occurrence** is a source-bound and frame-bound typing of a concrete structure—for example, a particular warranted `Λ` occurrence or `Ω` relation in a reconstruction. A **composite** is a newly formed analytical object whose constitutive sources and relations must be declared. Operator types are not empirical objects with decomposable parts. Occurrences and composites may participate in transformations; the base operator types do not.

This boundary is compactly expressed as:

```text
operator type
≠
operator occurrence
≠
composite
```

Other source objects include configurations, events, non-events, transitions, sequences, paths, and trajectories. These categories are not interchangeable. A missing record is not automatically a non-event; endpoint difference is not automatically a reconstructed transition; chronology is not automatically a path; and a path is not automatically a trajectory or a path-dependence claim. The purpose of these distinctions is not to multiply ontology. It is to prevent a transformation from silently presupposing the very object it is meant to establish.

## 2.2 Coordinates are relations, not levels of reality

The same source material can be reconstructed under different analytical coordinates. STRATA separates at least three that are often conflated:

| Coordinate | Methodological question | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Frame** | Which scene, boundary, context, or relevance field organizes the reconstruction? | A final or naturally given context |
| **Granularity** | At what degree of internal differentiation is the reference object reconstructed? | A truer or more fundamental layer |
| **Relative level** | Relative to which declared relation is an object treated as part, whole, local, distributed, lower, or higher? | An ontological hierarchy of reality |

The governing distinction is:

```text
frame
≠
granularity
≠
relative level
```

Changing one coordinate does not entail changing the others. A reconstruction may retain its frame while increasing internal differentiation. It may change frame while retaining a similar granularity. It may declare a higher relative level while remaining within the same temporal and source scope. Because these possibilities diverge, terms such as “micro,” “meso,” “macro,” “up,” and “down” are insufficient operation descriptions. They name local relations at best; they do not identify whether the analysis has composed sources, decomposed a reference object, or projected a contextual function.

This relational treatment blocks two forms of inflation. First, finer granularity receives no automatic truth priority. Second, a higher relative level receives no automatic explanatory or ontological priority. Multiple granularities may be locally adequate, and competing reconstructions may remain unresolved without being ranked by scale alone.

## 2.3 Origin type and target function must remain distinct

A third boundary concerns what an object is typed as in its source reconstruction and what function it may perform in a declared target context. STRATA distinguishes:

```text
origin type
≠
target function
```

An origin-typed trajectory may function as a frame, attractor, macro-event, or modulation profile in a bounded target context. Such a projection can be analytically useful, but it does not replace the source identity or create a new PMS primitive. The target function must remain dependent on the source object, target context, validity scope, and counterfactual sensitivity of the projection.

This is also why projection cannot be reduced to analogy or label substitution. Similar vocabulary, shape, or usefulness does not establish that a source object performs a constitutive target function. Conversely, a valid target function does not imply that the source always or essentially has that function.

## 2.4 Continuity is claim-relative and operation-specific

A transformation succeeds only if it preserves the continuity relevant to the claim being made. There is no single universal continuity criterion. Depending on the operation, the burden may concern:

- **reference continuity** — whether the target still concerns the claimed source object or declared source set;
- **origin-type continuity** — whether a contextual function leaves the source type intact;
- **functional continuity** — whether a finer reconstruction or projection remains connected to the source function it claims to explain or preserve;
- **temporal continuity** — whether ordering, transitions, non-events, and historical load remain reconstructible;
- **constitutive continuity** — whether the relations that form a composite remain visible rather than being replaced by a summary label.

Continuity does not require losslessness. Every transformation may preserve some structure while compressing, excluding, rendering uncertain, or irrecoverably losing other structure. The methodological requirement is that this Loss be made explicit and that the claim be calibrated to what remains supportable.

The source and target therefore cannot be connected by a generic assertion that the analysis has moved to another “level.” A minimally explicit transformation occurrence requires:

```yaml
transformation_occurrence:
  source_object:
  source_coordinates:
  operation:
  target_object_or_function:
  target_coordinates:
  continuity_burden:
  expected_praxeological_difference:
  loss:
  claim_scope:
  claim_ceiling:
  stop_condition:
```

This representation is schematic rather than a replacement for the canonical Shared Transformation Record. Its purpose is to show why controlled transformation is a methodological problem in its own right. The result must be reconstructed through the declared operation; it cannot be treated as already latent in the source simply because the source and target share vocabulary.

## 2.5 Three directions, not a generic change of level

The preceding distinctions make the three operation directions visible without yet supplying their full specifications:

```text
many or sequential source structures
→ COMPOSE
→ new composite analytical object

compressed occurrence or composite
→ DECOMPOSE
→ finer reconstruction of the same reference object

origin-typed source object
→ PROJECT_AS
→ bounded contextual target function
```

The wider method is organized in exactly four Parts. `PATH` governs temporal and structural composition, `SUB` finer same-reference reconstruction, `RETYPE` bounded contextual projection, and `LIMITS` the constraints that apply across all three. `LIMITS` is not a fourth operation or a superior meta-level.

These directions carry different continuity and Loss burdens. `COMPOSE` must justify selection, ordering, formation, and constitutive relation. `DECOMPOSE` must preserve the same reference object while showing that additional resolution makes a praxeological difference. `PROJECT_AS` must preserve origin type while justifying a context-bound function. Collapsing them into “moving up,” “moving down,” or “changing level” would remove exactly the distinctions needed to test them.

Controlled transformation is therefore not a theory of vertical reality. It is a discipline for making analytical movement explicit, source-bound, and criticizable. The next sections examine the three operations and the common admissibility conditions under which their results may be retained, reduced, stopped, rejected, or left non-captured.

# 3. Three Operations, Three Distinct Burdens

The three STRATA operations are not alternative names for moving between analytical levels. Each changes a different relation between source and target, and each therefore carries a distinct burden of disclosure. `COMPOSE` establishes a composite object that did not previously exist as a single analytical object. `DECOMPOSE` opens an already identified occurrence or composite while retaining it as the reference object under examination. `PROJECT_AS` leaves an origin-typed source object intact while assigning it a bounded function in a declared target context.

| Operation | Direction | Primary result | Principal disclosure burden | Canonical failure pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `COMPOSE` | multiple or sequential sources → composite | sequence, path, trajectory, or another declared composite | selection, ordering, formation, constitutive relations, and compression | chronology or aggregation presented as formation |
| `DECOMPOSE` | compressed occurrence or composite → finer reconstruction | components, relations, internal temporality, revised source-function status | same-reference preservation, component relations, source support, and resolution gain | fragmentation, invented internals, or detail without praxeological purchase |
| `PROJECT_AS` | origin-typed source object → contextual target function | bounded functional relation | origin-type preservation, target context, constitutive source trace, sensitivity, and validity scope | analogy, label substitution, or origin-type replacement |

The operations may occur in sequence, but they cannot be merged. A composition does not automatically produce a contextual function; a decomposition does not automatically discover a lower-level cause; and a projection does not reconstruct or repair its source object. Whenever an analysis crosses from one operation to another, a new transformation occurrence and a new claim are required.

## 3.1 `COMPOSE`: formation without comprehensiveness inflation

`COMPOSE` forms a declared composite analytical object from several temporally or structurally related sources. Its burden is not satisfied by placing items in chronological order or collecting them under a common label. The analyst must disclose why the selected elements belong together, how they are ordered, which relations are constitutive, and why the result is a sequence, path, trajectory, phase, or other stated composite rather than an archive or aggregate.

The operation therefore requires three decisions that are often hidden in narrative synthesis. A **selection rule** identifies load-bearing, supporting, contested, and excluded source elements. An **ordering rule** states whether relations are linear, partial, overlapping, parallel, uncertain, or retrospectively periodized. A **formation rule** explains which relations make the target object more than a list. These decisions are claim-bearing: changing them may change the composite, its boundary, or its admissibility.

The synthetic case `C17-LINEAR-01` illustrates a bounded success. Four typed service-activation structures—validated intake, verification, authorization, and activation—are connected through three continuation-changing relations. The result counts as a Path because verification opens authorization and authorization opens activation within one declared episode. Reordering or removing either transition defeats or reduces the Path claim. The case supports neither a broader Trajectory nor a contextual target function. Its strength lies precisely in that limitation: the composite remains reconstructible from its sources and sensitive to changes in their order.

The central failure mode is comprehensiveness inflation. A macro-label may appear to summarize a history while concealing source selection, branch structure, non-events, reversals, or internal heterogeneity. In such cases the composite can become rhetorically broader while becoming analytically less traceable. `COMPOSE` is admissible only when the new object preserves enough source load to constrain what may be claimed about it.

## 3.2 `DECOMPOSE`: finer reconstruction without depth privilege

`DECOMPOSE` reconstructs an identifiable, provisionally compressed occurrence or composite at a finer granularity. The source object remains the object being opened. This same-reference burden distinguishes decomposition from constructing a rival Path, changing the frame without disclosure, or assigning a new target function.

A valid decomposition must identify both components and relations. A parts list alone is fragmentation. The reconstruction must show how components interact, recur, interrupt, bind, or condition one another, and it must return to the coarser function under examination. That function may be confirmed, refined, internally differentiated, partially preserved, rejected, or left underdetermined. Preserving the source object does not protect the original source claim from revision; it only keeps the target of the test stable.

`C28-TRAJECTORY-01` opens a previously warranted review-governance Trajectory into an intake subpath, a review cluster, a closure event, a residual obligation, and a reopening subpath. The decisive gain does not come from the number of components. It comes from the source-supported relation between formal closure, continuing obligation, and later reopening. The finer reconstruction explains why closure is not equivalent to finality while preserving the same Trajectory as reference. The coarser function is refined rather than merely repeated, and no new Path or target function is smuggled into the operation.

Finer resolution has no intrinsic epistemic priority. Decomposition fails when source precision is exceeded, when the source disappears into fragments, when operator types are treated as material composites, or when added detail changes no warranted praxis claim. A microscopic description can be accurate yet resolution-neutral; it can also cross below the Relevance Floor and require Stop. The methodological question is therefore not how far an object can be divided, but which additional distinction changes a defensible reconstruction of its praxis structure.

## 3.3 `PROJECT_AS`: contextual function without type replacement

`PROJECT_AS` assigns an independently reconstructed, origin-typed source object a bounded function in a declared target context. The source reference and origin type remain intact. The operation therefore does not say that a trajectory becomes a Frame, that a recurrent structure becomes an Attractor as a new primitive, or that a contextual function reveals the essential type of its source.

The projection burden is conjunctive. The target context and function must be named; load-bearing source features must be mapped to the function; material source variation must be capable of weakening or defeating it; and the validity scope must specify the relevant frame, interval, roles, relations, and transfer limits. A projection supported only by resemblance or terminology remains analogy or label substitution.

In `C40-P1`, a previously reconstructed workload-review Trajectory is projected as a bounded historical frame-function for one overloaded-role relation in a later coordination context. Missed review, persistent workaround, residual workload binding, and partial repair affect which reopening options appear credible and costly. A comparison context with a role reset and released binding does not require the same projection. The source remains a Trajectory; the target function is limited to the declared role, context, and interval. The result is therefore `admissible_with_bounded_claim`, not an unrestricted functional identity.

Projection is especially vulnerable to authority inflation because a useful contextual function can be mistaken for a deeper type, a universal explanation, or a transferable property of persons or groups. STRATA blocks that move. A successful projection establishes only that the declared source structure performs the stated function within the tested context and claim scope.

## 3.4 Why no generic “change of level” operation is available

Terms such as “upward,” “downward,” “micro,” and “macro” describe relative analytical positions at most. They do not specify what relation is transformed. The same shift in relative level may involve composition, decomposition, projection, or no admissible transformation at all. Conversely, the same operation can occur without a change of relative level—for example, when several local structures are composed within one frame or when an occurrence is decomposed under finer granularity but remains at the same declared relational level.

The three operations are therefore exhaustive only as the canonical STRATA transformation types, not as an ontology of every analytical movement. When none fits without destroying source identity, type integrity, or constitutive load, the correct result is not a fourth operation. It is reduction, Stop, failure, or Non-Capture.

# 4. The Admissibility Band

The availability of a transformation does not establish its admissibility. STRATA places every operation occurrence within a band bounded by two opposed failure pressures:

```text
Praxeological Traceability Ceiling
─────────────────────────────
admissible transformation
─────────────────────────────
Praxeological Relevance Floor
```

The diagram is topological and procedural rather than metric. The Traceability Ceiling is shown above the admissible range and the Relevance Floor below it; this orientation names the upper and lower bounds without implying a numerical scale.

Below the Floor, additional structure produces **distinction without praxeological purchase**. Above the Ceiling, the result produces **abstraction without traceable load**. An admissible transformation must therefore matter to the reconstruction of praxis and remain reconstructibly dependent on the source structures that are said to support it.

The band is not a quantitative scale. It has no universal score, weighted average, or fixed numerical threshold. Its boundaries are claim-relative and operation-specific. A transformation may pass the lower-bound test and still fail traceability or type integrity; it may be richly sourced yet add no relevant distinction; or it may remain formally incomplete without having substantively failed. These possibilities must remain separate.

## 4.1 The Praxeological Relevance Floor

The lower-bound question is:

> Which warranted reconstruction must be formulated differently because this distinction or transformation was introduced?

A transformation has **PraxisPurchase** when it changes a defensible claim about such structures as action corridors, cost or exposure distribution, roles and asymmetries, expectations and non-events, commitment and binding, temporal order, path or trajectory formation, source-function status, or a bounded target function. The changed result may also be a narrower claim, a newly visible countercase, or a justified Stop condition. Purchase does not require a stronger substantive claim; sometimes the relevant gain is that the previous claim must be reduced.

The lower-bound test distinguishes at least three situations. A genuine gain changes the warranted reconstruction. A resolution-neutral result adds supported detail but leaves the relevant claim unchanged. A below-floor continuation adds terminology or microdetail without further discrimination and must stop when continued refinement serves no new praxeological purpose.

`C28-OVERFINE-01` provides the canonical pressure case. Second-level timestamps, interface actions, and token segmentation are supportable descriptions inside an already adequate review-occurrence reconstruction. They do not change any warranted claim about access, threshold, closure, repair, cost, binding, alternatives, or source function. The detail is not declared false; it is methodologically non-gaining for the tested continuation. The prior adequate reconstruction remains preserved, while further decomposition reaches `mandatory_stop`.

## 4.2 The Praxeological Traceability Ceiling

The upper-bound question is:

> Does the target result remain reconstructibly dependent on its declared source load?

**TraceableLoad** requires more than citation or a long source list. The analysis must identify load-bearing source features, map them to the target claim, explain the relevant structural or functional dependency, preserve constitutive temporal and relational load, disclose compression and exclusion, and state how material source variation would affect the result. Exhaustive reproduction is not required. What must remain visible is the trace that constrains the claim.

`C17-MACRO-01` pressures this boundary. A unitary “progressive national modernization Trajectory” remains unchanged despite decisive alterations to regional branches, non-events, reversals, and repair structures. The macro-label therefore lacks a formation rule capable of showing how heterogeneous sources constitute the asserted target. Citation volume cannot repair this source-indifference. The attempted `COMPOSE` occurrence maps to `failed_transformation`, while narrower regional Paths or other bounded compositions remain available as new claims.

The Ceiling thus blocks abstraction that has become too elastic to be defeated by its own sources. A macro-label, decomposition, or projection is not traceable merely because it can be linked to source material. The source must constrain the target.

## 4.3 Non-compensation and mandatory companion tests

Relevance and traceability are necessary but not independently sufficient. Admissibility also requires **TypeIntegrity**, reference and functional continuity where applicable, contextual boundedness, counterfactual sensitivity, a complete Loss account, source and calibration discipline, a claim ceiling, and accessible Stop and Non-Capture routes.

These checks are non-compensatory:

```text
formal elegance
+ additional detail
+ many citations
≠ missing PraxisPurchase
≠ missing TraceableLoad
≠ TypeIntegrity repair
```

A highly relevant result cannot survive a decisive type jump. A perfectly typed operation cannot survive the disappearance of its source reference. Strong traceability cannot globalize a local target function. Likewise, completing every record field does not convert formal conformance into substantive validity. The full pattern must support the current claim; no majority vote among tests is available.

Counterfactual sensitivity functions as a supplementary load test rather than as causal proof. The analyst asks whether a controlled, source-supported modification would alter, narrow, weaken, or defeat the target. Insensitivity creates pressure when the claim presents the modified feature as constitutive, but stable response can still be warranted where an invariant or alternative source-load explanation is explicit. Untestable and underdetermined results must remain distinct from robustness or failure.

## 4.4 Admissibility is a bounded result, not an authority grant

Passing the band establishes that one transformation occurrence is sufficiently relevant, source-dependent, type-integral, and bounded for its declared analytical claim. It does not establish empirical truth, causal necessity, moral or legal validity, diagnostic status, governance legitimacy, or application authority. Nor does it make a later transformation inherit the success of an earlier one.

The band therefore performs two reductions at once. It prevents empty analytical proliferation below and source-detached abstraction above. At the same time, it prevents admissibility itself from becoming a higher-order warrant. A transformation may be retained as `admissible`, `admissible_with_bounded_claim`, `resolution_neutral`, or another canonical result only after the complete claim pattern has been assessed. Where the burdens cannot be met, explicit failure, Stop, claim reduction, or Non-Capture are positive methodological outcomes rather than defects to be hidden.

# 5. Three Failure Families

The Admissibility Band does not produce one undifferentiated category of failure. Because the three operations transform different relations, their characteristic defects are also different. A failed composition may lose the source-to-composite formation trace even while its topic remains important. A failed or stopped decomposition may add accurate detail that changes no warranted reconstruction. A failed projection may preserve the source object and its origin type while never establishing a source-dependent target function. These are not variants of a single mistake called “wrong level.” They are failures of different transformation burdens.

| Failure family | Operation under pressure | Decisive defect | What remains preserved | Canonical route in the selected case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| traceability failure | `COMPOSE` | the composite no longer depends reconstructibly on its constitutive source load | heterogeneous source field and narrower alternatives | `failed_transformation` |
| relevance failure | `DECOMPOSE` | finer supported detail changes no warranted praxis reconstruction | prior adequate same-reference reconstruction | `mandatory_stop` |
| type/function failure | `PROJECT_AS` | a label or category is substituted for a contextual function | source identity, origin type, and prior source result | `failed_transformation` |

The cases below are synthetic pressure tests. They show how STRATA discriminates among failure mechanisms; they do not establish empirical sufficiency across domains.

## 5.1 Traceability failure in composition

`C17-MACRO-01` tests a proposed national modernization Trajectory assembled from twelve years of heterogeneous regional adoption, divergence, delayed review, reversal, fallback, and partial repair. The intended composite has clear potential relevance: if the source field supported one directionally formed national Trajectory, that result would alter the historical reconstruction. The failure is therefore not lack of possible praxeological purchase. It is absence of a source-sensitive formation relation.

The decisive test changes features that the macro claim presents as load-bearing. Regional divergence may be reversed, the delayed review replaced by an on-time review, a reversal removed, or partial repair replaced by closure. The target label—“progressive national modernization Trajectory”—remains unchanged. This invariance does not demonstrate robustness. It shows that the macro-label is insufficiently constrained by the branches, non-events, reversals, and open residues from which it is purportedly composed.

The case therefore exceeds the Praxeological Traceability Ceiling. It has many sources, a temporal span, and an intelligible narrative, but lacks the constitutive dependency required for the declared composite. Source volume cannot substitute for a formation rule. Chronological coverage cannot substitute for Path or Trajectory structure. The correct result is `failed_transformation`, while the regional source structures, a national chronology, separate regional Paths, and No-Composition remain available as distinct alternatives.

This failure family is specific to composition. The target is not rejected because it is “too macro” in an ontological sense. It is rejected because the asserted composite does not change when its claimed source load changes. A differently bounded `COMPOSE` occurrence may still succeed, but it must open a new claim rather than inherit the failed macro result.

## 5.2 Relevance failure in decomposition

`C28-OVERFINE-01` begins from an already adequate reconstruction of a bounded review occurrence. Access, threshold application, closure, repair, and their relevant relations are source-supported. The proposed continuation adds second-level timestamps, cursor and display actions, and token-level subdivisions of wording. These distinctions may be accurate. The case does not declare them false or fabricated.

The problem is that their removal changes no warranted claim about access, roles, thresholds, closure, repair, cost, binding, alternatives, or source function. The finer packet therefore supplies detail without additional PraxisPurchase. The source reference remains stable, and some of the detail may be retained as descriptive information, but continued decomposition falls below the Praxeological Relevance Floor for the claim being tested.

The canonical route is `mandatory_stop`, not `failed_transformation`. The distinction matters. A failure result would suggest that the executed operation does not satisfy its defining or admissibility conditions as a transformation. Here the method has enough information to determine that further continuation is unnecessary and would become objection-displacing. The prior reconstruction remains valid; the stop applies to continued refinement on unchanged grounds.

Nor is the result `resolution_neutral` for the continuation as a whole. Resolution neutrality can describe a completed, supported finer reconstruction that adds valid resolution without changing the warranted praxis claim. The present case asks whether decomposition should continue beyond an already adequate point. Once further subdivision becomes repetitive and non-discriminating, the positive methodological result is Stop. Re-entry requires materially changed grounds: a new source-supported relation, a narrower claim whose answer depends on the finer distinction, or a new calibration basis.

This failure family shows why finer granularity has no truth priority. More detail can be both supported and methodologically unnecessary. The relevant question is not whether a smaller unit can be named, but whether opening it changes a warranted reconstruction of the same reference object.

## 5.3 Type/function failure in projection

`C40-N3` starts from the independently established service-activation Path of `C17-LINEAR-01`. A synthetic renewal-review registry assigns the label “eligibility-gate function” to every Path containing an authorization token. The analyst then presents the label assignment as a successful `PROJECT_AS` occurrence.

The source Path remains visible, and its origin type is not overwritten. Yet origin-type preservation alone is insufficient. A contextual target function must depend on load-bearing source features. In this case, the source order, continuation relations, and prior Loss may be altered while the authorization token is retained; the registry label remains unchanged. The only operative relation is therefore:

```text
token present
→ label assigned
```

The required relation would be:

```text
source Path structure
→ target-side functional difference
```

The target fails both the lower and upper pressures. It adds no target-side practical distinction beyond catalog membership and does not remain functionally continuous with the Path as reconstructed. Machine-readable vocabulary and context naming make the assignment technically repeatable, but technical translation success does not establish a projection.

The result is `failed_transformation`. It is not `analogy_only`, because no bounded correspondence with explicit breaking points is retained. It is not `claim_reduction_required`, because reducing the result to “the registry assigns this string” creates a separate metadata claim rather than a narrower target-function claim. It is not `non_capture`, because the failure of one source-indifferent projection leaves other source-sensitive projections, analogy, and No-Projection available.

This failure family is specific to `PROJECT_AS`: the source object may remain intact while the target function never becomes constitutively source-dependent. A label can be useful without being a function, just as an analogy can be informative without retyping its source. Keeping those results separate blocks the conversion of vocabulary compatibility into functional or ontological authority.

## 5.4 Why the failure families matter

The three cases expose different non-compensatory limits:

```text
relevant macro topic
≠ traceable composite

supported finer detail
≠ praxeological gain

well-formed target label
≠ contextual function
```

A generic level-change model would obscure these differences. It could describe all three cases as moving upward, downward, or across contexts, but it would not identify the burden that failed. STRATA's operation typing therefore has diagnostic value at the level of method—not diagnosis of persons or domains, but discrimination among transformation errors. The output is stronger precisely because it says less: which operation was attempted, which burden failed, what remains preserved, and which new claims remain available.

# 6. Continuity, Loss, and Claim Discipline

A transformation is not adequately described by naming its source, operation, and target. It must also state what remains continuous, what is selected away, and how the resulting claim is limited by those changes. STRATA treats continuity and Loss as operation-specific control dimensions rather than as assumptions of perfect preservation.

## 6.1 Continuity is multidimensional

At least four continuity questions recur across the three operations:

- **Reference continuity:** Does the target still concern the declared source object or source set where sameness is claimed?
- **Type integrity:** Does the origin type remain visible rather than being overwritten by a component set, composite label, or target function?
- **Functional continuity:** Does the source or target function remain dependent on concrete, load-bearing source features?
- **Temporal continuity:** Are enough order, duration, transition, branch, sedimentation, and historical-load relations preserved for the temporal claim?

Contextual boundedness adds a fifth control: the result must remain limited to its frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, validity scope, and claim ceiling. These dimensions need not all have the same status. A transformation may preserve reference and origin type while only partially preserving function; it may preserve a composite object while compressing its microhistory; or it may remain temporally adequate for a Path claim but not for a Trajectory claim.

Continuity is therefore neither binary nor merely nominal. Keeping the same name does not preserve the same reference, and preserving the reference does not establish the same function. The relevant matrix must be read against the current claim.

## 6.2 Five-part Loss

Every transformation selects. `COMPOSE` selects source elements, ordering, periodization, branch relevance, and compression. `DECOMPOSE` selects added distinctions, components, relations, and internal scales. `PROJECT_AS` selects a target context, a target function, foregrounded source features, and a validity scope. The resulting Loss must be recorded in five fields:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

**Preserved** identifies the structures that remain available and load-bearing. **Compressed** identifies structure retained only in reduced form. **Excluded** identifies material deliberately left outside the transformation and its claim. **Uncertain** records unresolved status that must not be converted into absence, failure, or confidence. **Irrecoverable** identifies structure that cannot be reconstructed from the current transformed result or available source packet.

This vocabulary prevents two symmetrical errors. The first is the fiction of lossless transformation. The second is the assumption that any Loss automatically invalidates the operation. A legitimate transformation may be lossy when the Loss is explicit, constitutive structure remains traceable, and the claim is reduced accordingly. Hidden Loss is more serious: it presents selection as completeness and may invalidate the claim because the reader cannot reconstruct which source relations were suppressed.

The three failure cases illustrate different Loss effects. `C17-MACRO-01` preserves the heterogeneous regional field but cannot retain it as one source-sensitive national Trajectory. `C28-OVERFINE-01` preserves the prior adequate occurrence while excluding further unsupported relevance claims. `C40-N3` preserves the source Path and its origin type, while the registry compresses the Path to token presence and irrecoverably lacks the target-function dependency it never encoded.

## 6.3 Claim reduction is not transformation repair

Loss and continuity determine what a result may still claim. When the tested assertion exceeds the surviving source, type, or functional burden, a weaker claim may remain supportable. STRATA represents this as `claim_reduction_required` where the reduced claim remains materially continuous with the tested claim.

Typical local reductions include:

```text
trajectory → path
path → sequence
strong path dependence → weak order dependence
strong target function → limited contextual function
functional projection → structural analogy
```

These are not points on one universal strength scale. Each reduction is local to the claim family and must preserve the original claim, the reason it was too strong, the remaining supported form, and the revised claim ceiling. Support downgrade is separate: a claim may remain the same type while becoming provisional or underdetermined. Resolution neutrality is separate again: the finer result may be valid without changing the relevant claim.

Claim reduction also cannot repair a failed operation by retrospective wording. If the reduced statement changes the transformation object—such as replacing a failed function claim with the factual statement that a registry assigned a label—it is a new claim, not a reduced version of the same one. The distinction prevents rhetorical retreat from being presented as methodological success.

## 6.4 Local result and canonical Output Class

STRATA separates the operation-specific result from the canonical Output Class. The local result states what happened in the language of the operation: an admissible Path, a resolution-neutral decomposition, a source-function refinement, a bounded target function, a useful analogy, or a source-indifferent label substitution. The canonical class maps that result into the closed ten-class governance vocabulary:

```text
admissible
admissible_with_bounded_claim
admissible_but_provisional
resolution_neutral
analogy_only
partially_admissible
claim_reduction_required
mandatory_stop
failed_transformation
non_capture
```

The mapping is not a truth score. A global class must not erase the local meaning, and no default precedence exists among boundedness, provisionality, partiality, reduction, Stop, failure, and Non-Capture. For example, an admissible `DECOMPOSE` occurrence may reject or refine the prior source-function claim without itself becoming a failed transformation. The operation result and the effect on the prior claim must be recorded separately.

The separation also preserves collision discipline. `mandatory_stop` concerns inadmissible continuation. `failed_transformation` concerns failure of the proposed operation or its defining conditions. `non_capture` concerns a bounded inability to adequately capture the declared object, relation, or function after the relevant routes have been examined. These outcomes may occur near one another, but they are not interchangeable labels for uncertainty or difficulty.

## 6.5 No authority inheritance

A transformation result receives no authority merely because it follows a prior admissible step, uses a complete record, or passes formal validation. The governance rule is explicit:

```yaml
governance:
  authority_inheritance: prohibited
```

A source may be well reconstructed while a later projection fails. A composite may be admissible while a reverse decomposition cannot recover its microhistory. A chain may contain several bounded successes and still end in claim reduction. The authority ceiling applies independently to every occurrence and to the integrated chain view.

The practical consequence is a disciplined asymmetry between preservation and warrant. Earlier source material, Loss, failure, and uncertainty must remain visible in later records. Their visibility constrains successor claims; it does not authorize them. This is how the method retains analytical continuity without turning continuity into inherited legitimacy.

# 7. Chains, Stop, and Non-Capture

Transformations can be sequenced, but a chain is not a mechanism for averaging local results or converting narrative completion into admissibility. Each link has its own source, operation, target, continuity burden, Loss, claim ceiling, and possible failure. The governing anti-immunization rule is:

```text
new transformation = new claim
```

A later operation may be legitimate even when an earlier claim failed. What it may not do is erase, reinterpret away, or inherit authority from that failure.

## 7.1 Failure preservation and legitimate re-entry

The `C50-FP1` chain begins with a failed `PROJECT_AS` claim. A source object `T50`, labelled a stabilization trajectory, is said to perform a target function, but removing it changes no target interpretation or access. The original projection therefore maps to `failed_transformation`.

The chain then opens three successor claims:

```text
failed PROJECT_AS claim
→ DECOMPOSE same reference as a narrower Path
→ COMPOSE that Path with an external gate
→ PROJECT_AS the new composite as a bounded threshold function
```

The decomposition reconstructs `T50` as `request → hold/non-event → repair → release` and is retained with a bounded claim. A continuation into arbitrary timestamp subdivision is separately stopped because it changes no praxis reconstruction. The narrower Path is then composed with an external gate into a new composite `K50`. Only this new composite is projected as a reopening-threshold function under gate-window variation.

The final projection may succeed without repairing the first one because its source object, constitutive relations, and target dependency are different. The chain's core identity is:

```text
K50 ≠ T50 repaired
```

The original failed projection remains visible in every successor record. Re-entry is legitimate because the grounds changed: a new same-reference claim was tested, a new source element entered the composition, and a new target function was evaluated. Re-entry would be illegitimate if the analyst merely changed granularity or target vocabulary until the objection disappeared.

## 7.2 Stop as a positive result

Stop does not mean that the source is worthless, that the analyst failed, or that no later inquiry is possible. It states that continuation under the present conditions would violate a methodological boundary or add no justified difference. General triggers include:

```text
no additional PraxisPurchase
loss of TraceableLoad
source ceiling reached
TypeIntegrity cannot be maintained
reference continuity lost
calibration no longer possible
anti-immunization violation
```

A **mandatory Stop** applies when continuation would be inadmissible and maps to `mandatory_stop`. An **optional Stop** applies when continuation remains possible but unnecessary for the present claim; it is a methodological decision, not an additional Output Class.

Re-entry after Stop requires a new record and materially changed conditions—for example new sources, a new claim, a new relevant countercase, a newly justified granularity, or a new calibration basis. Silent continuation is prohibited. The stopped result and its Loss remain available to constrain the successor record.

The `C50-FP1X` branch demonstrates the distinction. Additional timestamp subdivision is not prohibited because small units are intrinsically invalid. It is stopped because, on the current grounds, further detail changes neither the objection nor the warranted reconstruction. A new source-supported internal relation could justify a later record; repetition of the same subdivision cannot.

## 7.3 Non-Capture without failure avoidance

`C52-NC1` tests whether one compressed handoff occurrence admits a single, complete, decisive finer reconstruction. The source packet supports at least two bounded candidates. One emphasizes archive-access blockage; the other emphasizes a responsibility transition with no accepted owner. Each has real PraxisPurchase and source trace. Neither can establish comparative priority because access and ownership change during the same interval. A third integrated model would require inventing direction, hierarchy, or joint necessity not supplied by the source.

The result is `non_capture`, specifically granularity Non-Capture. What is captured remains explicit: the same reference occurrence, its coarse coordination-breakdown and recovery function, the access-topology reconstruction, the responsibility-transition reconstruction, their overlap, and the source gaps. What remains uncaptured is one decisive constitutive model and the priority relation among the candidates.

This result is not `failed_transformation`. `DECOMPOSE` is the correct operation, the reference remains stable, and the rival partial reconstructions are valid. It is not `partially_admissible`, because the two partial claims must remain separately bounded rather than being treated as an adequate retained portion of one unique-decomposition claim. It is not `claim_reduction_required`, because selecting either candidate or coarse retention would create a different claim requiring a new record. It is not `mandatory_stop`, because no independent stop trigger decides the executed capture claim, although repetition on unchanged grounds is blocked.

Non-Capture is therefore not a protective label for missing information or weak analysis. It becomes available only when the capture object is delimited, relevant alternatives have been tested, partial achievements are preserved, and the remaining limitation belongs to the same claim. A simple unsupported decomposition must still map to failure, reduction, or Stop as appropriate. Non-Capture cannot be used to shelter a claim from defeat.

## 7.4 Chain-level results do not overwrite local results

The optional integrated chain `C53-CPD1` makes this discipline explicit. A bounded `COMPOSE` occurrence forms composite `Q47`; a bounded `PROJECT_AS` occurrence assigns it an access function; a final `DECOMPOSE` claim then asks whether the chain permits complete, exact, lossless recovery of the composite's constituent history. It does not. A narrower reopening is supported, but contribution weights, compressed microhistory, and prior Loss remain unavailable. The final local route is `claim_reduction_required`.

The chain view is `partially_admissible` because it contains two bounded local successes, one final reduction, and a separately preserved source-insensitive projection failure. No chain average converts those results into global success, and no reverse operation restores neutral identity.

Together, the cases establish a methodological negative capability:

```text
later success ≠ repair of prior failure
Stop ≠ substantive finding
Non-Capture ≠ immunity from criticism
chain completion ≠ authority inheritance
```

The method remains productive because it can continue under changed grounds. It remains criticizable because every continuation opens a new claim, every local result remains visible, and the system permits positive terminal outcomes where further transformation would overreach or where the object remains inadequately captured.

# 8. Formalization without Automated Authority

The STRATA formal model makes transformation claims more inspectable by requiring explicit fields, controlled vocabularies, operation-specific burdens, Loss disclosure, audit stages, Output Classes, and governance prohibitions. Schemas can test whether a record is structurally complete; registries can test whether operation names and result classes are canonical; package checks can test whether component versions, references, and integrity bindings agree. These capabilities reduce omission and make disagreement easier to locate.

They do not decide whether a source is truthful or sufficient, whether a reconstruction is semantically adequate, whether a counterfactual is empirically plausible, whether a causal relation exists, or whether an application is legitimate. A schema-valid transformation may still be substantively wrong, under-sourced, badly calibrated, or outside the method's authority. The formal model therefore preserves a division of labor:

```text
machine-readable conformance
≠
substantive passage
```

Machine assistance can expose missing declarations, inconsistent routes, invalid spellings, undeclared Loss, and broken dependencies. Human and domain judgment remain necessary for source evaluation, interpretation, calibration, rival comparison, and every external decision. The checked example records demonstrate formal routes and boundary behavior; they are not empirical evidence for the claims they encode.

# 9. Discussion

The method's first implication is **resolution neutrality**. Finer analysis may reveal relations hidden by a coarse reconstruction, but it may also exceed source support, fragment a praxis-relevant function, or multiply distinctions without changing the warranted claim. Coarser reconstruction is not inferior merely because it compresses detail. Granularity must be assessed against the declared question, source basis, and praxeological purchase rather than ranked by fineness.

The same restraint applies in the opposite direction. A composite can disclose a path, trajectory, or distributed relation that no isolated occurrence carries, yet the wider object receives no automatic privilege of completeness or explanation. Its warrant depends on constitutive source trace and declared selection. Composition creates an analytical object, not an ultimate whole.

`PROJECT_AS` adds a different implication. Higher-level or cross-context function is contextual rather than ontological. An origin-typed object can function as a gate, frame, threshold, attractor, or access condition in a declared target context without becoming that function as a new primitive. This permits functional comparison while preventing useful projection from hardening into a claim about what the source object fundamentally is.

Explicit Loss accounting makes these transformations auditable. The five-part record does not merely acknowledge that something was omitted; it distinguishes preservation, compression, exclusion, uncertainty, and irrecoverability. That distinction allows later users to see which conclusions remain available, which require new sources, and which cannot be reconstructed from the transformed result. Loss therefore constrains reuse without automatically invalidating every selective reconstruction.

STRATA also creates conditions for later comparative, domain-specific, or machine-assisted analysis without deciding those analyses in advance. It can make the source object, operation, coordinates, alternatives, and claim ceiling explicit. It cannot supply the empirical warrant, semantic expertise, normative authority, or institutional competence required by a later application. The method enables disciplined handoff; it does not transfer authority through the handoff.

For machine-assisted analysis, this distinction is especially important. Formal tooling can preserve provenance, enforce closed operation inventories, detect missing fields, and retain failed or stopped claims across chains. It should not rank persons, infer diagnosis, decide legitimacy, or convert formal completion into substantive approval. The contribution is therefore not automated judgment but **auditable transformation under retained human and domain accountability**.

# 10. Limitations

STRATA is a structurally bounded method, not a substitute for source quality or external warrant. A transformation cannot carry more evidential load than its source basis permits, and formal precision cannot repair missing observations, unreliable records, contested semantics, or absent domain knowledge. Counterfactual sensitivity tests declared dependence; it does not prove causation.

The method also provides no universal scale, fixed micro–meso–macro hierarchy, or context-free threshold for relevant detail, valid composition, or functional projection. Calibration remains claim-, source-, frame-, and domain-dependent. Different reconstructions may be co-valid, competing, partially translatable, or incomparable for the predicate under examination.

STRATA does not decide empirical truth, legal or political legitimacy, moral correctness, clinical status, person type, institutional action, or policy. Its formal artifacts cannot automate those decisions. Nor does the closed inventory of three operations prove that every relevant transformation is representable or that every object can be adequately captured.

Non-Capture therefore remains a necessary result. Where sources, calibration, semantic distinctions, or the available transformation set cannot preserve the required object without distortion, the method must retain the limit rather than force success. Rival models and no transformation remain live alternatives.

# 11. Conclusion

Controlled structural transformation is possible without authority inflation. `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, and `PROJECT_AS` make different analytical movements available, but each remains bounded by source trace, praxeological relevance, continuity, type integrity, Loss, alternatives, calibration, claim scope, Stop, and Non-Capture.

The resulting gain is increased legibility and criticizability—not a deeper ontology, stronger truth rank, or permission to act. A larger composite is not an ultimate whole; a finer reconstruction is not inherently truer; a contextual function is not a new origin type; and a formally valid record is not substantive proof.

PMS-STRATA therefore contributes a disciplined way to move among temporal structures, granular reconstructions, composites, and contextual functions while preserving the distinction on which the method depends:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```
