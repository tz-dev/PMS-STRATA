## 1. What STRATA Is

PMS-STRATA is a bounded transformation discipline within the Praxeological Meta-Structure (PMS). It specifies how already reconstructed praxis structures may be **composed**, **decomposed**, or **projected as performing a bounded contextual function** while preserving source traceability, reference and type integrity, declared transformation Loss, claim limits, Stop conditions, and the possibility of Non-Capture. STRATA makes analytical movement explicit; it does not turn larger composites, finer resolution, higher relative levels, formal models, or richer visualizations into greater truth or authority. Its governing distinction is:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

The canonical orientation is available in the repository [`README.md`](../README.md) and the integrated [`Minified Canonical`](../05_minified/PMS_STRATA_Minified_Canonical.md).

## 2. Relation to PMS Base

PMS Base supplies the unchanged Δ–Ψ operator grammar. STRATA neither adds a new operator nor modifies the canonical ordering or dependencies. It acts on **source-bound operator occurrences, composites, configurations, temporal objects, and derived analytical objects**; it does not open or rewrite the abstract operator types themselves.

The distinction is essential:

```text
operator type
≠
operator occurrence
≠
composite
```

STRATA is therefore not a Meta-PMS, an ontology of strata, or a superior validating layer. A derived path, trajectory, macro-event, frame-function, attractor-function, or higher-level composite function remains a derived analytical object or contextual function. It does not become a new PMS primitive. Likewise, an admissible transformation does not inherit permission to diagnose persons, settle causality, issue legal or political judgments, authorize intervention, or override PMS guardrails. The concise authority boundary is specified in the [`Claim Boundary Minified`](../05_minified/PMS_STRATA_Claim_Boundary_Minified.md).

## 3. The Problem STRATA Solves

Praxis analysis frequently moves between analytical positions. Separate events or transitions may be assembled into a path or trajectory. A compressed occurrence or composite may be opened under finer granularity. An object established in one frame may be treated as performing a function in another context. These movements are often useful, but they can silently change the object, source burden, resolution, relative level, frame, or claim.

Three recurring errors follow:

1. **Composition inflation:** an ordered list or broad label is treated as a warranted path, trajectory, or total history without declaring the constitutive relations that form the composite.
2. **Resolution inflation:** a finer reconstruction is treated as automatically deeper or truer even when it does not change a warranted praxis finding or no longer preserves the source object.
3. **Type inflation:** a contextual function, analogy, or convenient label is treated as a new origin type or as evidence of what the source object “really is.”

STRATA controls these movements by requiring every transformation to declare its analytical coordinates: reference object, frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, source scope, transformation context, and claim scope. The coordinates must remain distinct:

```text
frame
≠
granularity
≠
relative level
```

A changed coordinate is not a cosmetic reformulation. It creates a new testable claim.

## 4. Exactly Three Operations

STRATA recognizes exactly three core operations. Their directions and burdens cannot be collapsed into a generic “level change.”

| Operation | Direction | Core burden | Central failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `COMPOSE` | many or sequential source structures → new composite analytical object | disclose source selection, order, formation rule, constitutive relations, preservation, and Loss | chronology, aggregation, or macro-label without traceable formation |
| `DECOMPOSE` | compressed occurrence or composite → finer reconstruction of the same reference object | preserve the reference burden, component relations, source function, granularity change, and relevance of added distinctions | fragmentation, detail accumulation, or source-object loss |
| `PROJECT_AS` | origin-typed source object → bounded contextual target function | preserve origin type and source trace while declaring target context, function, validity scope, alternatives, and projection Loss | analogy drift, label substitution, or invalid type jump |


`COMPOSE` does not automatically create a target function. `DECOMPOSE` never decomposes the Δ–Ψ operator types. `PROJECT_AS` adds a contextual function without replacing the origin type. Full signatures and decision gates are provided in the [`Operation Signatures Minified`](../05_minified/PMS_STRATA_Operation_Signatures_Minified.md).

## 5. The Four-Part Map

The corpus organizes the method into four mutually constraining Parts:

```text
PATH
→ temporal ordering, transitions, sequences, paths, trajectories,
  branches, non-events, sedimentation, and path dependence
→ principal operation: COMPOSE

SUB
→ finer reconstruction of provisionally compressed occurrences
  and composites
→ principal operation: DECOMPOSE

RETYPE
→ bounded contextual function with preserved origin type
→ principal operation: PROJECT_AS

LIMITS
→ relevance, traceability, continuity, type integrity, Loss,
  source and claim ceilings, anti-immunization, Stop,
  failure preservation, and Non-Capture
→ not an operation
```

`PATH`, `SUB`, and `RETYPE` organize different transformation problems. `LIMITS` applies throughout them and through every operation chain. It is not a fourth operation, an optional warning layer, or a position above the other Parts.

## 6. The Admissibility Band

A transformation is not admissible merely because it can be formulated. It must lie within a non-metric operating band:

```text
Praxeological Traceability Ceiling
─────────────────────────────
admissible transformation
─────────────────────────────
Praxeological Relevance Floor
```

The diagram is topological and procedural rather than metric. The Traceability Ceiling is shown above the admissible range and the Relevance Floor below it; this orientation names the upper and lower bounds without implying a numerical scale.

The **Praxeological Relevance Floor** is the lower bound. Below it, added detail, distinction, composition, or functional language produces no warranted change in the praxis reconstruction. It is **distinction without praxeological purchase**.

The **Praxeological Traceability Ceiling** is the upper bound. Above it, a composite, decomposition, or projection no longer carries reconstructible load from its source objects. It is **abstraction without traceable load**.

The band is relational rather than a universal scale. There is no fixed smallest relevant distinction, finest admissible granularity, largest legitimate composite, or universal threshold at which a sequence becomes a path, a path becomes a trajectory, or an analogy becomes a projection. Admissibility depends on the declared source object, frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, source basis, transformation context, and claim. The detailed boundary rules are collected in the [`Admissibility Band Minified`](../05_minified/PMS_STRATA_Admissibility_Band_Minified.md).

## 7. Required Checks

Every transformation claim must pass a set of non-compensatory checks. The compact test families are:

- **Praxeological Purchase:** What warranted reconstruction changes because of the transformation?
- **Traceable Load:** Which source features, relations, temporal structures, costs, asymmetries, non-events, or bindings actually constitute the result?
- **Type Integrity:** Does the origin type remain visible, without being overwritten by a component list, higher-level label, profile, or target function?
- **Reference and Functional Continuity:** Does the result remain dependent on the declared referent and on concrete source features?
- **Temporal Continuity:** Where temporal claims are made, are order, duration, transition structure, branch loss, and historical load adequately preserved?
- **Contextual Boundedness:** Are frame, granularity, relative level, target context, validity scope, and claim ceiling explicit?
- **Counterfactual Sensitivity:** Would a relevant change in the stated source load alter or defeat the result?
- **Source and Calibration Limits:** Does formal precision stay within source precision, and are open thresholds preserved as calibration-sensitive?
- **Loss and Selection:** Are selection decisions and all five Loss fields disclosed?
- **Alternatives and No-Transformation:** Are rival routes, analogy-only results, or the option not to transform still available?
- **Stop and Anti-Immunization:** Would continuation merely relocate, soften, or hide a failed claim?
- **Claim and Authority Ceiling:** Does the result assert only what the sources and operation support?

The checks are non-compensatory. More detail cannot compensate for no praxis purchase; formal elegance cannot compensate for absent source trace; good sources cannot repair an invalid type jump; and a bounded context cannot repair loss of reference identity.

## 8. Output Classes, Loss, Stop, and Non-Capture

Each operation occurrence is documented in a Shared Transformation Record that keeps the source object, analytical coordinates, operation identity, source trace, preservation duties, Loss, local result, canonical Output Class, claim ceiling, and Stop or Non-Capture disposition inspectable together. The record supports auditability; completing it does not validate the substance of the claim.

Operation-specific findings are mapped to one of ten canonical Output Classes:

```text
admissible
admissible_with_bounded_claim
admissible_but_provisional
resolution_neutral
analogy_only
partially_admissible
claim_reduction_required
mandatory_stop
failed_transformation
non_capture
```

These are canonical result classes, not scores or ranks. `resolution_neutral` means that a valid refinement adds no new praxis finding. `analogy_only` preserves a useful similarity without claiming functional projection. `claim_reduction_required` retains a weaker supportable claim while recording the stronger unsupported one. `mandatory_stop`, `failed_transformation`, and `non_capture` remain distinct results.

Every transformation must also record five forms of Loss:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

Loss is not automatically failure. A transformation may be legitimately lossy when the loss is explicit, constitutive structures remain traceable, and the claim is correspondingly bounded. Hidden Loss can invalidate the result.

**Stop** is a positive methodological result. A mandatory Stop means that continuation under the current conditions would violate relevance, traceability, source, type, calibration, continuity, or anti-immunization constraints. Re-entry requires a new record and a materially changed condition, such as new sources, a new claim, a new countercase, a justified granularity, or a new calibration basis.

**Non-Capture** records that STRATA does not adequately capture the relevant object, relation, or function under the present conditions. It preserves the possibility that no admissible composition, decomposition, or projection exists, that another model is superior, or that the present grammar is semantically inadequate. Non-Capture is not a way to protect a weak claim from failure.

The full class definitions and routing rules are available in the [`Output Class Index`](../04_reference/Output_Class_Index.md).

## 9. A Short Worked Example

Two synthetic `COMPOSE` records show how the same operation family can pass or fail.

### Admissible route: bounded service-activation Path

In [`C17-LINEAR-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C17-LINEAR-01_Simple_Linear_Path.md), validated intake, verification, authorization, and activation are connected by three declared continuation relations and a warranted order. The formation rule requires each transition to change the next available continuation. Removing verification or authorization, reversing their order, or replacing activation with non-activation changes or defeats the Path claim.

```text
typed source structures
+ explicit selection
+ warranted order
+ constitutive relations
+ complete Loss
→ admissible bounded Path
```

The result is `admissible`. It remains limited to one synthetic episode and does not establish a trajectory, causal explanation, institutional judgment, or contextual target function.

### Failed route: source-indifferent macro-Trajectory

In [`C17-MACRO-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C17-MACRO-01_Macro_Label_without_Traceable_Path.md), heterogeneous adoption, divergence, delayed review, reversal, and repair structures are labelled one progressive national modernization Trajectory. The label remains unchanged when decisive branch, non-event, reversal, or repair load is removed or inverted. The sources support regional structures or chronology, but not the asserted unitary direction.

```text
relevant macro distinction
+ no constitutive source dependency
+ counterfactual insensitivity
→ failed transformation
```

The result is `failed_transformation`. Potential relevance cannot compensate for a result above the Traceability Ceiling. The source field remains available for new bounded records, but a new route cannot retroactively repair the failed macro claim.

## 10. What STRATA Does Not Provide

STRATA does not provide:

- an ontology of real strata or a universal hierarchy of levels;
- a fourth operation or a generic level-change operator;
- automatic truth, causality, explanation, prediction, or empirical validation;
- a universal STRATA scale or fixed relevance and traceability thresholds;
- decomposition of the PMS operator types;
- conversion of target functions into new origin types or PMS primitives;
- person typing, psychological or clinical diagnosis, moral ranking, dignity hierarchy, or irreversible labels;
- political, legal, forensic, institutional, or legitimacy decisions;
- sanctions, automatic recommendations, intervention permission, or application authority;
- authority inherited from schemas, YAML, corpus size, diagrams, Readers, formal elegance, or implementation detail;
- guaranteed total capture of every praxis structure.

Machine-readable components can check declared fields, controlled vocabularies, dependencies, record shape, and cross-file consistency. They cannot decide source truth, semantic validity, causal adequacy, normative legitimacy, diagnosis, or whether an application is authorized.

A changed frame, granularity, relative level, composition, or target function does not erase a failed claim:

```text
new transformation
=
new testable claim
```
