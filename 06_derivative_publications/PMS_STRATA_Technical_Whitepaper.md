## Abstract

PMS-STRATA specifies a bounded transformation discipline for reconstructions made with the Praxeological Meta-Structure (PMS). It addresses analytical movements that are often necessary but methodologically underdeclared: multiple or sequential structures are formed into a composite object; a provisionally compressed occurrence or composite is opened under finer granularity; or an origin-typed source object is assigned a bounded function within another context. STRATA renders these movements explicit through exactly three operations: `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, and `PROJECT_AS`.

The method is organized into four Parts. `PATH` governs temporal and structural composition, with `COMPOSE` as its principal operation. `SUB` governs finer reconstruction of occurrences and composites, with `DECOMPOSE` as its principal operation. `RETYPE` governs contextual functional projection without origin-type replacement, with `PROJECT_AS` as its principal operation. `LIMITS` is not a fourth operation or a superior meta-level; it is the constitutive control structure applying across all Parts and operation chains.

Every transformation must declare its source and target, analytical coordinates, expected praxeological difference, source trace, preservation duties, five-part Loss, claim ceiling, Stop condition, and possible Non-Capture. Admissibility is assessed within a band bounded below by the Praxeological Relevance Floor and above by the Praxeological Traceability Ceiling. Additional detail without a changed praxis-relevant reconstruction falls below the Floor. Abstraction, fragmentation, or projection without reconstructible source load exceeds the Ceiling. The gate is non-compensatory: formal elegance, corpus size, source abundance, or model consistency cannot repair failure of praxis purchase, traceable load, type integrity, reference continuity, or contextual boundedness.

STRATA depends entirely on the unchanged Δ–Ψ operator grammar and guardrails of PMS Base. It transforms operator occurrences, composites, and derived analytical objects; it does not decompose or redefine operator types. A larger composite is not automatically more explanatory, a finer reconstruction is not automatically truer, and a contextual target function is not a new origin type or PMS primitive. The governing distinction is therefore:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

The repository includes canonical prose, contracts, minified controls, references, appendices, cases, Transformation Records, YAML, JSON Schemas, decision structures, derivative publications, and a Reader. These artifacts increase inspectability but do not establish empirical truth, causality, semantic or normative validity, person-level judgment, or application authority. Machine-readable validation can test declared form and consistency only. STRATA remains criticizable through explicit failure, claim reduction, mandatory Stop, rival reconstruction, and Non-Capture. A new frame, granularity, relative level, composite, or target function creates a new testable claim; it does not erase a previously failed one.

# Executive Summary

## 1. The transformation problem

PMS supplies a formal operator grammar for rendering praxis structurally legible. A reconstruction, however, does not remain analytically stationary merely because it continues to use PMS vocabulary. It may move from separate occurrences to a sequence, path, trajectory, or another composite. It may open an apparently unitary event, non-event, occurrence, path, or trajectory into finer internal relations. It may treat an established source object as performing a different function within a wider, narrower, or otherwise changed context. Each movement can be useful. Each can also silently alter the object, frame, resolution, relative position, source burden, Loss profile, and claim.

PMS-STRATA makes the movement itself an object of method. A transformation is not a cost-free bridge: it carries its own source, operation identity, target, admissibility burden, Loss, result, claim ceiling, and Stop conditions. Each occurrence in a chain remains separately criticizable.

## 2. Exactly three operations

STRATA recognizes exactly three core operations:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

`COMPOSE` forms a new composite analytical object from multiple or sequential source structures. It must disclose selection, order, constitutive relations, compression, exclusions, uncertainty, irrecoverable Loss, and the difference made by the resulting composite. Chronology alone is not yet a path; aggregation alone is not yet a composite with warranted formation logic; and a large retrospective label is not yet a traceable trajectory.

`DECOMPOSE` opens a provisionally compressed occurrence or composite under finer granularity while retaining the burden of reconstructing the same reference object. It applies to occurrences and composites, not to the formal Δ–Ψ operator types. Additional detail is not sufficient: the finer reconstruction must preserve or critically revise the relation between its components and the source object, and it must produce a warranted praxeological difference. Atomization, source-object loss, and resolution drift are failures rather than deeper truth.

`PROJECT_AS` assigns an origin-typed source object a bounded contextual target function. The source retains its origin type. A trajectory may, for example, function as a frame-function or macro-event in a declared context, but it does not thereby become the PMS operator `□`, an event as a new origin identity, or a new primitive. Projection requires a constitutive source trace, counterfactual sensitivity, contextual boundedness, an explicit validity scope, and declared projection Loss. Analogy, metaphor, label similarity, or target usefulness alone do not establish projection.

Complex workflows are represented as finite chains of separately recorded occurrences. The closed vocabulary is a methodological control, not a theorem of complete capture.

## 3. Four Parts, one continuous limits discipline

The corpus has four mutually constraining Parts:

| Part | Principal function | Principal operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PATH` | Temporal order, transition, sequence, path, trajectory, branches, non-events, sedimentation, and path dependence | `COMPOSE` |
| `SUB` | Finer reconstruction of provisionally compressed occurrences and composites | `DECOMPOSE` |
| `RETYPE` | Bounded contextual function without origin-type replacement | `PROJECT_AS` |
| `LIMITS` | Admissibility, continuity, type integrity, Loss, source and claim ceilings, anti-immunization, Stop, and Non-Capture | none |

`LIMITS` is neither an operation nor a warning added after productive work is complete. It is constitutive of every transformation from the beginning. A `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, or `PROJECT_AS` occurrence that cannot satisfy the relevant limits is not rescued by its formal sophistication or explanatory appeal.

## 4. The STRATA Admissibility Band

The admissible operating range lies between two boundaries in the following order:

```text
Praxeological Traceability Ceiling
─────────────────────────────
admissible transformation
─────────────────────────────
Praxeological Relevance Floor
```

The display is topological, not metric: the Traceability Ceiling is shown above the admissible range and the Relevance Floor below it.

The **Praxeological Relevance Floor** asks whether the proposed change creates warranted praxis-relevant purchase. Relevant differences may concern action corridors, roles, asymmetries, costs, exposure, expectations, non-events, temporal order, delay, irreversibility, integration, binding, or Stop conditions. Detail without a changed reconstruction, chronology without path gain, or renaming without functional gain falls below the Floor.

The **Praxeological Traceability Ceiling** asks whether the result remains reconstructibly dependent on its constitutive source structures. A trajectory without path trace, a macro-event without internal duration, a higher-level asymmetry without distributed load, or a projection that remains unchanged despite material source variation exceeds the Ceiling.

Admissibility is non-compensatory. More detail cannot compensate for no praxis purchase. Good sources cannot compensate for an invalid type jump. Formal elegance cannot compensate for missing source trace. A bounded context cannot compensate for loss of reference identity. Full assessment therefore includes PraxisPurchase, TraceableLoad, TypeIntegrity, Reference and Functional Continuity, Contextual Boundedness, Counterfactual Sensitivity, Source and Claim Ceiling, Loss, alternatives, Stop, and Non-Capture.

## 5. Records, Loss, and bounded results

Every transformation uses a shared record spine. The record distinguishes the source object, operation occurrence, target object or function, analytical coordinates, claim, admissibility checks, alternatives, governance, and result. Loss is mandatory and uses five canonical fields:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

Loss disclosure prevents compression, finer resolution, or contextual foregrounding from being presented as neutral. It also makes non-invertibility explicit. Decomposing a prior composition does not restore the original source set without remainder. Re-composing a decomposition does not reproduce the source object automatically. Projecting an object does not convert its origin type.

Local results are mapped to exactly ten canonical Output Classes:

```text
admissible
admissible_with_bounded_claim
admissible_but_provisional
resolution_neutral
analogy_only
partially_admissible
claim_reduction_required
mandatory_stop
failed_transformation
non_capture
```

They preserve productive, reduced, neutral, failed, stopped, and uncaptured outcomes. Local results must map to this closed inventory.

## 6. Stop, re-entry, anti-immunization, and Non-Capture

Stop is a positive methodological outcome. **Mandatory Stop** applies where continuation would be inadmissible—for example because source support is exhausted, traceable load has been lost, type integrity cannot be preserved, the source object disappears under decomposition, or a new transformation is being used mainly to rescue a failed claim. **Optional Stop** applies where further work may be possible but is unnecessary for the present bounded claim; it is a methodological option, not an additional Output Class.

Re-entry after Stop requires a genuinely changed condition and a new record, such as new sources, a new claim, a newly justified granularity, a relevant countercase, or a new calibration basis. Silent continuation is prohibited.

Anti-immunization preserves prior failure: changing frame, granularity, relative level, composition, or target function does not erase an unsuccessful claim.

```text
new transformation
=
new testable claim
```

Non-Capture records that the present sources, granularity, composition, projection, calibration, or grammar do not adequately capture the relevant object or relation. It may also preserve the possibility that a rival grammar performs better. Non-Capture is not an appeal to ineffable depth and cannot protect a weak claim from an available counterexample.

## 7. Formal model boundary

The formal model can represent and check declared structure: permitted operation names, required fields, object classes, analytical coordinates, record completeness, type-preservation declarations, admissibility procedure, Loss fields, claim ceilings, Stop conditions, and allowed Output Classes. YAML and JSON Schema can expose omissions and internal inconsistency.

They cannot decide empirical truth, actual causality, semantic adequacy, normative validity, exhaustive capture, person-level status, clinical or legal judgment, superiority over rival grammars, or application permission. A valid machine-readable record is not a valid world claim. Formalization increases consistency pressure and inspectability only.

The corpus and its tooling likewise have distinct roles and acquire no backward authority through technical detail. The system remains governed by one distinction:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

# Position within PMS

## 1. PMS Base remains unchanged

PMS-STRATA depends exclusively on PMS Base as represented by the current repository-reference `PMS.yaml`. PMS Base supplies the eleven Δ–Ψ operator types, their canonical functions, the default dependency path, derived structural handles, and application guardrails. STRATA neither revises nor completes that grammar.

This dependency fixes the first boundary of the project:

```text
PMS Base
→ operator grammar and guardrails

PMS-STRATA
→ transformation discipline for PMS reconstructions
```

STRATA may operate on concrete operator occurrences, configurations, events, non-events, transitions, composites, paths, trajectories, and other derived analytical objects. It may not decompose an operator type as though the type were an empirical object. It may not rename, reorder, extend, or replace Δ–Ψ. It may not treat a trajectory, macro-event, frame-function, attractor-function, higher-level composite function, or modulating profile as a new PMS primitive.

The distinction is foundational:

```text
operator type
≠
operator occurrence
≠
composite
```

An operator type is an abstract element of the base grammar. An occurrence is a local, source-bound analytical typing within a declared frame. A composite is a formed relation among multiple occurrences or other source structures. The latter two may be transformed under STRATA; the formal identity of the operator type may not.

## 2. Transformation discipline, not Meta-PMS

STRATA does not occupy a superior level above PMS and does not validate PMS by recursively organizing its applications. It adds explicit method for movements that can otherwise remain implicit: formation of composite objects, finer reconstruction of compressed objects, and bounded contextual function attribution.

The project is therefore a methodological extension in the limited sense of making transformation rules explicit. It is not a base-grammar extension, an ontology of strata, or an independent source of validity. Relative levels are analytical relations declared for a particular transformation. Granularity is selected resolution. Frames are contextual boundaries. None is an ontological depth scale.

The correct formulation is local and claim-bound:

```text
Within frame C and granularity g,
source object X may be transformed by operation T
for bounded claim Q.
```

The incorrect formulation promotes analytical coordinates into world structure:

```text
X belongs to a fundamentally higher, deeper, or more real stratum.
```

STRATA consequently provides no final constituents, ultimate composite, universal micro–meso–macro hierarchy, or guaranteed endpoint of recursive analysis. Finer and coarser reconstructions may be locally co-valid. Higher composition may increase explanatory organization for a bounded claim, but not authority.

## 3. Relation to lenses, corpora, implementations, and workflows

The PMS ecosystem contains domain lenses, corpus-form repositories, implementation workspaces, application-discipline layers, workflow tooling, and self-critical projects. These materials can supply source objects, cases, target contexts, rival reconstructions, or implementation pressure for STRATA analysis. They do not become sources of STRATA rules unless those rules are explicitly incorporated through the canonical corpus process.

Optional PMS add-ons or lenses may therefore be used as test vectors where a bounded question warrants them. Their vocabulary cannot silently redefine `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, `PROJECT_AS`, the four Parts, the Admissibility Band, or the Output Classes. Conversely, a successful STRATA reconstruction of material from an add-on does not validate that add-on, PMS Base, or the domain claim at issue.

The same separation applies downstream. A YAML record, JSON Schema, Reader view, software validator, graph, or executable implementation may test representation, completeness, or consistency. It may not supply missing semantic support or transform structural validity into empirical warrant. Workflow completion cannot make an operation true, and technical execution cannot grant application authority.

## 4. Claim Ceiling and Authority Ceiling

A **Claim Ceiling** limits the strongest assertion supported by a particular transformation record. An **Authority Ceiling** blocks forms of authority that STRATA cannot license at all, even where the structural transformation is admissible.

This distinction prevents a recurrent escalation:

```text
successful structural reconstruction
→ stronger descriptive claim
→ person-level, normative, causal, legal, political, or intervention authority
```

The final arrow is not licensed by STRATA. More explicit composition, finer decomposition, contextual projection, formal consistency, or corpus detail does not authorize diagnosis, person ranking, moral judgment, policy enforcement, legal conclusion, or intervention. Application remains subject to the existing PMS guardrails of Distance, reversibility, Dignity-in-Practice, scene-boundedness, calibration, and external accountability.

The governance rule is therefore explicit:

```yaml
governance:
  authority_inheritance: prohibited
```

No result inherits authority from occupying a higher relative level, using finer granularity, passing schema validation, appearing in a large corpus, or being implemented in software. Authority must come from the appropriate independent evidential, normative, legal, institutional, or application source—not from STRATA transformation alone.

## 5. Bounded contribution

Within these limits, STRATA makes a precise contribution. It allows PMS-based analysis to state not only *what* is being reconstructed, but *how the analytical object or function changed between claims*. It supplies explicit operation identity, analytical coordinates, source trace, Loss, criticizability, Stop, and Non-Capture. This makes second-order and cross-level work more inspectable without turning it into a higher-order authority.

The project can therefore be summarized as follows:

> PMS-STRATA extends the explicit method of PMS reconstruction while leaving the PMS operator grammar, its application guardrails, and its epistemic status unchanged.

# The Problem Class

## 1. Uncontrolled movement between analytical positions

The central problem addressed by STRATA is not that PMS lacks an operator grammar. The problem is that a reconstruction can retain PMS terminology while moving to a materially different analytical position without declaring the movement. The target statement can then appear to follow directly from its source even though the reference object, source set, frame, granularity, temporal scope, relative level, function, Loss profile, or claim has changed.

A transformation is therefore not merely a change in wording. It is a new analytical occurrence that may require new evidence and may fail independently of the source claim. STRATA concentrates on five recurrent pressure zones.

## 2. Source multiplicity and composite formation

Praxis is often reconstructed from multiple events, non-events, occurrences, roles, transitions, and source fragments. Analysts may need to form these into a sequence, path, trajectory, event cluster, branch structure, or another composite object.

The risk is that temporal succession or thematic similarity is mistaken for constitutive composition. A list of events may be presented as a path without a selection rule, transition structure, or evidence that the order changes the reconstruction. A retrospective label may absorb heterogeneity and unrealized alternatives. A trajectory may be asserted without showing the path, sedimentation, changed action corridors, asymmetry accumulation, or binding load that make it more than chronology.

The problem class can be stated as:

```text
many or sequential source structures
+
undeclared selection, order, or formation
→
apparent composite without controlled composition claim
```

STRATA does not prohibit composition. It requires the composite to be formed through a declared `COMPOSE` occurrence and remain traceable to its constitutive sources and relations. The difference between a chronology and a path is therefore not terminological; it is a burden of reconstruction.

## 3. Compression and finer reconstruction

Analytical work necessarily compresses. An event, non-event, operator occurrence, transition, path, trajectory, or composite may be treated provisionally as a unit so that a wider claim can be made. Later work may need to open that unit under finer granularity.

The recurrent error is to equate more detail with better explanation. Finer distinctions may merely restate the source, fragment the object, exceed available evidence, dissolve a non-event into unrelated positive events, or introduce a new path or target function while still being described as decomposition.

The problem class is:

```text
provisionally compressed source object
+
more detail without source-object relation
→
fragmentation, resolution drift, or hidden operation change
```

A valid `DECOMPOSE` occurrence reconstructs the same reference object under finer granularity and states how the components, relations, internal temporality, and source support alter a warranted claim. Where no additional praxeological purchase is gained, `resolution_neutral` may be the correct result. Where continued opening would lose the source object or fall below the Relevance Floor, Stop is required.

This is also why `SUB` and `RETYPE` must remain distinct. Opening the internal structure of an object is not the same as attributing a function to that object in a new target context. A finer account that quietly changes the question from “what constitutes this source object?” to “what does this object function as elsewhere?” has crossed operations and must be recorded accordingly.

## 4. Contextual function projection

PMS-based reconstruction frequently recognizes that an object established under one type or object class performs a useful function in another analytical context. A trajectory may organize a target field as a frame-function; a recurrent trajectory form may function as an attractor; a composite may carry a distributed asymmetry or access function.

The risk is origin-type replacement. A useful analogy, repeated label, formal correspondence, or target fit may be treated as evidence that the source object *is* the target type. This converts contextual function into identity and can silently create new primitives.

The problem class is:

```text
origin-typed source object
+
undeclared target context or insufficient source trace
→
analogy, label substitution, or invalid type jump presented as projection
```

A valid `PROJECT_AS` occurrence preserves the source object and origin type, identifies the target context and function, reconstructs the constitutive source trace, specifies validity scope, tests counterfactual sensitivity, and discloses projection Loss. The test is not whether the label is illuminating; it is whether material changes to the source structures would alter or defeat the attributed function.

## 5. Coordinate conflation

Frame, granularity, and relative level are independent analytical coordinates even though more than one may change in a single workflow.

- A **frame** is the contextual boundary that determines the object, relevance, inside/outside relations, expected non-events, source relevance, and claim scope.
- **Granularity** is the resolution or distinction density used in the reconstruction.
- A **relative level** is an object’s local position within a declared compositional or functional relation.

The recurring error is to treat these as interchangeable. Finer granularity is described as a deeper level. A wider frame is described as higher composition. A change of relative relation is treated as proof that `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, or `PROJECT_AS` has occurred. “Micro,” “meso,” and “macro” labels are then used as if they carried universal boundaries or truth ranks.

The problem class is:

```text
changed frame, granularity, or relative relation
+
undeclared coordinate change
→
ambiguous object, operation, source burden, and claim
```

Coordinate change alone does not identify a STRATA operation. Several or sequential structures must actually be formed for `COMPOSE`; the same compressed object must actually be opened for `DECOMPOSE`; an origin-typed object must actually be assigned a bounded contextual function for `PROJECT_AS`. Declaring the coordinates is necessary but not sufficient.

The separation also prevents false reconciliation. Two reconstructions at different granularities may be locally co-valid, but incompatible answers to the same bounded predicate do not become compatible merely because one is called micro and the other macro. Comparability, source trace, and claim scope must be tested rather than neutralized by scale language.

## 6. Structure and authority inflation

The most consequential failure pressure is the transfer of analytical success into stronger authority. A composite may appear more comprehensive than its sources. A finer reconstruction may appear more truthful. A higher relative level may appear more fundamental. A contextual projection may appear to establish a new type. A schema-valid record may appear externally warranted.

None of these implications is licensed:

```text
larger composite
≠
more complete history

finer reconstruction
≠
stronger truth

higher relative level
≠
ontological superiority

valid projection
≠
new origin type

valid record
≠
valid world claim
```

Authority inflation matters because it can convert a bounded structural claim into causal proof, person typing, diagnosis, moral ranking, institutional judgment, legal conclusion, or intervention permission. STRATA is designed to expose this transition and stop it. Its records therefore include both a claim ceiling and the prohibition of authority inheritance.

## 7. The common structure of the problem

Across all five pressure zones, the same methodological pattern recurs:

1. A source reconstruction exists under declared or implicit coordinates.
2. The analysis moves to another object, resolution, relation, or function.
3. The movement is narrated as though the target claim were already contained in the source.
4. Selection, Loss, source trace, type preservation, alternatives, and changed claim burden remain hidden.
5. The target statement acquires apparent inevitability or authority.

STRATA interrupts this pattern by requiring the movement to be typed and tested. The problem class is therefore not “verticality” in the abstract. It is **uncontrolled analytical transformation under hidden coordinate, type, source, Loss, and authority changes**.

The bounded contribution is correspondingly precise:

```text
implicit transformation
→ hidden claim shift

explicit operation occurrence
→ separately testable claim
```

Wider composition, finer reconstruction, and contextual function projection remain available. What is removed is their presumption of costlessness.

# Object Model and Analytical Coordinates

## 1. Why a transformation needs an object model

A STRATA operation cannot be identified only by directional language such as “up,” “down,” “inside,” “outside,” “micro,” or “macro.” Before an analysis can state that something has been composed, decomposed, or projected, it must identify **what kind of object enters the operation**, which identity is to be preserved or newly formed, and under which analytical coordinates the source and target claims are made.

This burden protects two boundaries at once. First, it prevents formal PMS operator types from being treated as empirical objects that can be opened into parts. Second, it prevents a changed label, frame, resolution, or relative relation from being mistaken for a completed STRATA operation. The object model is therefore not an added ontology. It is a declaration discipline for source-bound reconstruction.

The minimum distinctions are summarized below.

| Domain | Term | Compact definition | Transformation relevance | Must not collapse into |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PMS Base | **operator type** | A canonical abstract function in the unchanged Δ–Ψ grammar | Supplies a typing resource; is not transformed by STRATA | occurrence, composite, empirical constituent |
| Reconstruction | **operator occurrence** | A concrete, frame-bound, source-bound analytical typing | May enter a composite or be reconstructed as an occurrence | formal operator type, fixed person property |
| Reconstruction | **composite structure** | A formed relation among multiple occurrences or analytical objects | Principal target of `COMPOSE`; possible source of `DECOMPOSE` | set, list, chronology, totality |
| Reconstruction | **configuration** | A temporally situated, frame-bound praxis arrangement | May carry events, non-events, roles, asymmetries, bindings, and action corridors | isolated state label |
| Temporal object | **event** | A positively marked relevant structural change within a frame | May enter temporal composition or later finer reconstruction | mere date, every change, complete transition |
| Temporal object | **non-event** | A warranted non-realization relative to an expectation frame | May be constitutive of a path or trajectory | missing information, invented absence |
| Temporal object | **transition** | A structured relation between configurations that preserves more than endpoint difference | May be composed into sequences and paths or decomposed under finer granularity | before/after difference alone |
| Derived object | **sequence** | An ordered set of relevant objects | May be a valid `COMPOSE` target without yet being a path | unordered list, path automatically |
| Derived object | **path** | An actual or reconstructed traversal through configurations and transitions | `COMPOSE` target whose order and relations change available continuations | chronology, trajectory automatically |
| Derived object | **trajectory** | A path with sedimented historical load that alters later continuation conditions | Higher-burden `COMPOSE` target; may later be projected contextually | directionality, teleology, path dependence automatically |
| Derived relation | **path dependence** | A counterfactually sensitive claim that historical route materially conditions the present field | Separate claim burden attached to a path or trajectory | path, trajectory, determinism |
| Derived function | **contextual target function** | A bounded function performed by an origin-typed object in a declared target context | Principal result relation of `PROJECT_AS` | new origin type, analogy, renaming |

The table is intentionally selective. The full corpus also treats branches, phases, event clusters, recurrent forms, macro-events, frame-functions, attractor-functions, modulation profiles, and other bounded derived objects or functions. Their availability does not make them PMS primitives, and their labels do not remove operation-specific burdens.

## 2. Operator type, occurrence, and composite

The most important type boundary is:

```text
operator type
≠
operator occurrence
≠
composite
```

An operator type such as `Λ`, `Ω`, or `Θ` is defined by PMS Base. An occurrence is a revisable analytical typing of a concrete source structure: for example, a source-supported `Λ` occurrence in which a promised review did not occur within the warranted expectation window. A composite may then include that occurrence together with configurations, transitions, and other source objects.

This permits STRATA to reconstruct how operator-typed occurrences participate in larger structures without claiming that the formal operator itself has empirical subparts. A `DECOMPOSE` operation may open the internal organization of a compressed `Λ` occurrence—its expectation basis, time window, relevant actors, and changed action corridor—while preserving that the operation concerns the **occurrence**. It may not claim that the PMS operator type `Λ` has been decomposed into more fundamental PMS operators.

The same boundary applies to derived objects. A path, trajectory, or frame-function may become an independently referenced source object in a later operation. It remains derived and source-dependent. Reuse does not promote it into the base grammar.

## 3. Configuration, event, non-event, and transition

Configurations provide the scene-relative arrangements within which events and non-events become meaningful. An event marks a relevant realized change. A non-event marks a warranted non-realization under an expectation frame. A transition relates configurations through events, non-events, timing, changed costs, altered asymmetries, binding shifts, or changed action possibilities.

These categories must remain distinguishable because they support different transformation claims. A positive event can occur without completing a transition. A transition may include a central non-event. A missing later source may leave a transition underdetermined without itself constituting a non-event. A list of events may establish chronology while failing to establish a path.

The governing reduction is:

```text
state difference
≠
transition reconstruction
```

Likewise:

```text
missing information
≠
non-event
```

This preserves source discipline. STRATA may record uncertainty or lack of evidence without manufacturing an expected-but-absent occurrence.

## 4. Analytical coordinates

Every operation declares at least seven analytical positions:

```yaml
analysis_position:
  reference_object:
  frame:
  granularity:
  relative_level:
  temporal_scope:
  source_scope:
  claim_scope:
```

A **frame** fixes the contextual boundary of relevance: which object is being reconstructed, which relations count as internal or external, which expectations are warranted, which sources matter, and what the claim is about.

**Granularity** fixes resolution or distinction density. A finer reconstruction may distinguish more components and relations, but this does not give it a truth or authority privilege.

A **relative level** identifies an object’s local position within a declared compositional or functional relation. It is relational and operation-dependent, not an objective stratum of reality. The same path may be relatively higher than its constituent transitions and relatively lower than a later composite in which it participates.

**Temporal scope** declares the relevant interval, beginning, endpoint or open continuation, prior conditions, later effects, and uncertainty of periodization. **Source scope** declares the materials that directly support the claim, the structure inferred from them, known gaps, uncertain relations, and the Source Ceiling. **Claim scope** states the exact proposition and reach permitted by the reconstruction.

The coordinates are independent enough that one may change while another remains stable:

```text
stable frame + finer granularity
≠
changed frame + stable granularity
≠
changed relative level
```

Several may also change in one workflow. Their declaration still does not identify the operation by itself. `COMPOSE` requires actual composite formation; `DECOMPOSE` requires same-reference finer reconstruction; `PROJECT_AS` requires an added contextual function.

## 5. Reference identity and continuity

A transformation must say what it is preserving when it calls the target “the same,” “derived from,” or “a function of” the source. Reference identity is claim-relative but not arbitrary. Depending on the operation, relevant identity dimensions may include:

- a documentary or historical source reference;
- the source object class and origin type;
- temporal lineage;
- constitutive members and relations;
- frame and scope;
- prior disposition and inherited Loss;
- the function under examination.

`COMPOSE` creates a new composite object while preserving the lineage of its constitutive sources. `DECOMPOSE` retains the source object as the reconstruction target while opening internal structure. `PROJECT_AS` preserves source reference and origin type while adding a bounded target function.

These are different continuity relations. Nominal sameness is insufficient. Two objects may share a label while failing reference continuity; one historical object may retain reference identity while its function changes; a source type may be revised by new evidence without retrospectively rewriting an earlier bounded record.

Where identity cannot be stabilized without inventing succession, suppressing conflict, or erasing constitutive change, the correct result may be claim reduction, mandatory Stop, competing reconstructions, or Non-Capture.

## 6. Transformation context

The target context is the context within which a projected function is claimed. The **transformation context** is wider: it states why this operation is being performed, what comparison or decision problem it addresses, which alternatives remain live, and which authority ceiling applies.

A changed frame may motivate a new operation but is not itself an operation. Recontextualization may alter relevance without forming a composite, opening a source object, or establishing a contextual target function. STRATA therefore keeps the following distinction explicit:

```text
recontextualization
≠
PROJECT_AS
```

The object model and coordinate declaration together make each transformation occurrence separately criticizable. They do not settle admissibility. That further burden is carried by the operation signatures and the Admissibility Band.

# The Three Operations

## 1. Closed operation vocabulary

PMS-STRATA contains exactly three canonical transformation operations:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

They are not three names for generic movement between “levels.” Each answers a different question, preserves a different identity relation, and produces a different kind of result.

| Operation | Question | Source-to-target movement | Primary preservation duty | Characteristic failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `COMPOSE` | How may multiple or sequential sources form a declared composite? | many / sequential → new composite object | constitutive source lineage, selection, order, formation relations | chronology, aggregation, teleological or untraceable macro-label |
| `DECOMPOSE` | How may a compressed occurrence or composite be reconstructed under finer granularity? | compressed source → finer same-reference reconstruction | source object, component relations, source-function relation | atomization, operator-type decomposition, resolution drift, new path disguised as opening |
| `PROJECT_AS` | What bounded function may an origin-typed source perform in a declared target context? | origin-typed source → contextual target function | source reference, origin type, source-dependent function trace | analogy, label substitution, recontextualization alone, origin-type replacement |

The distinction among operation type, operation occurrence, and result remains active:

```text
operation type
≠
operation occurrence
≠
resulting object or function
```

The operation type supplies the canonical signature. A particular occurrence carries source, target, coordinates, justification, Loss, alternatives, Stop, and an admissibility result. The resulting composite, finer reconstruction, or contextual function does not become another operation.

A workflow may require more than one operation, but each link must receive its own record and may pass, fail, stop, or remain non-captured independently. Operation chains are treated in the section on chains, re-entry, and anti-immunization.

## 2. Shared operation envelope

All three operations use a common minimum envelope. At technical level, every occurrence must declare:

- the source object, typing, reference, frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, and source basis;
- the operation kind, justification, expected praxeological difference, and transformation context;
- the target object or target function, target coordinates, and validity scope;
- the claim type, scope, and Claim Ceiling;
- the exact five-part Loss account;
- Relevance Floor, constitutive source trace, counterfactual sensitivity, Traceability Ceiling, Type Integrity, continuity, and Contextual Boundedness results;
- rival transformations, no-transformation, and non-translation options;
- Stop condition, reached status, and Non-Capture status;
- `authority_inheritance: prohibited`.

The common envelope makes the operations comparable without making them interchangeable. Operation-specific fields add burdens; they do not remove the shared ones.

## 3. `COMPOSE`

### 3.1 Definition and signature

`COMPOSE` forms a declared composite analytical object from multiple temporally or structurally related source objects while making selection, ordering, formation, compression, and Loss explicit.

```text
many or sequential source structures
→
new composite analytical object
```

A compact signature is:

```text
COMPOSE:
(S, Fr_s, g_s, ℓ_s, τ, R, J)
→
(X_c, K_c, Fr_t, g_t, ℓ_t, L, V)
```

Here `S` is the declared source set or sequence, `R` the warranted relation and ordering structure, `X_c` the resulting composite, `K_c` its declared analytical object class, `L` the Loss profile, and `V` the validity scope.

Typical targets include a sequence, path, trajectory, event cluster, branch structure, phase, or another explicitly declared composite. A contextual target function—such as frame-function, macro-event function, or attractor-function—is **not** produced by `COMPOSE` alone. It requires a separate `PROJECT_AS` claim.

### 3.2 Entry burdens

Source plurality is necessary but insufficient. A `COMPOSE` occurrence must state:

1. which source objects enter and how they are typed;
2. why each included object is included and why relevant objects are excluded;
3. whether order is linear, partial, parallel, overlapping, uncertain, or retrospectively periodized;
4. which relations form the target rather than merely connecting or co-locating the sources;
5. what praxeological discrimination the composite adds;
6. which source load remains traceable;
7. which alternatives include another composition or no composition.

The **selection rule** separates load-bearing from illustrative material. The **ordering rule** states the temporal or structural order actually warranted. The **formation rule** explains why the target is a sequence rather than a list, a path rather than chronology, or a trajectory rather than a path.

```text
source plurality
≠
composition
```

```text
connected representation
≠
formed composite
```

### 3.3 Preservation, compression, and Loss

The composite is new in analytical organization but remains dependent on reconstructible source lineage. Depending on the claim, `COMPOSE` may need to preserve:

- source identity and typing;
- load-bearing order and transitions;
- central events and non-events;
- asymmetry, cost, and binding relations;
- branch points and unrealized alternatives;
- internal heterogeneity without which the target claim changes.

Preservation is not total retention. Every composition compresses. It may reduce local variation, fine timing, minor parallel paths, source separability, or recoverability of alternatives. Those changes must be distributed across the five canonical Loss fields rather than hidden beneath the composite label.

A composite that cannot be reopened to its load-bearing sources exceeds the Traceability Ceiling. A composite that adds only summary convenience, graph density, narrative smoothness, or a shorter label falls below the Relevance Floor.

### 3.4 Counterfactual sensitivity and claim limit

The central source-change question is:

> Would removing, replacing, or materially changing a load-bearing source element or relation alter the composite object or defeat its warranted claim?

Strong or partial sensitivity supports constitutive dependence. Insensitivity to arbitrarily large source changes signals that the target may be a template label rather than a formed object.

A successful composition supports only the bounded composition claim. It does not automatically establish causal necessity, intention, optimality, teleology, strong path dependence, a completed totality, or a contextual target function.

### 3.5 Representative pressure cases

[`C17-LINEAR-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C17-LINEAR-01_Simple_Linear_Path.md) shows a valid bounded Path. Four typed episode structures and three continuation-sensitive relations are selected and ordered; changing verification, authorization, or activation defeats the Path claim. The result is `admissible`, with no trajectory or target-function reach.

[`C17-LAMBDA-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C17-LAMBDA-01_Trajectory_with_Central_Non_Event.md) tests whether a source-supported Non-Event remains constitutive within a trajectory rather than disappearing into a positive-event narrative. Its inclusion demonstrates that composition must preserve absence where absence changes later praxis.

[`C17-MACRO-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C17-MACRO-01_Macro_Label_without_Traceable_Path.md) provides the negative pressure. A large retrospective label cannot substitute for selection, formation, path trace, and source-sensitive historical load. Macro vocabulary does not create a macro object.

### 3.6 `COMPOSE` completion boundary

A `COMPOSE` occurrence is complete only when it can answer:

```text
Which sources are selected?
What order or relation is warranted?
What formation rule creates the target?
What praxeological difference does it add?
Which source changes alter or defeat it?
What is preserved, compressed, excluded, uncertain, or irrecoverable?
Which rival composition or no-composition route remains?
When must composition stop?
```

The availability of more sources or a larger possible composite does not create an obligation to continue.

## 4. `DECOMPOSE`

### 4.1 Definition and signature

`DECOMPOSE` reconstructs a provisionally compressed occurrence or composite under finer granularity while retaining the source object as the reconstruction target.

```text
compressed occurrence or composite
→
finer reconstruction of the same reference object
```

A compact signature is:

```text
DECOMPOSE:
(X, T_o, F_s, Fr_s, g_s, ℓ_s, Q_d, J)
→
(K, R_K, F_s', Fr_t, g_t, ℓ_t, L, V)
```

`X` is the identifiable source object, `T_o` its origin type, `F_s` its source or coarser function under examination, `Q_d` the decomposition question, `K` the reconstructed finer components, `R_K` their relations and internal temporal structure, and `F_s'` the post-decomposition status of the source function.

The direction commonly moves toward finer granularity, but granularity change alone is not enough. The source object must remain the question.

### 4.2 Valid sources and the operator-type prohibition

Valid sources include operator occurrences, events, non-events, transitions, configurations, sequences, paths, trajectories, branch structures, and other composites or derived objects. The operation may open their internal organization where the source supports additional distinction.

It may not decompose PMS operator types.

```text
operator occurrence may be reconstructed
operator type may not be decomposed by STRATA
```

Thus an analyst may open a concrete frame occurrence into the source distinctions, exclusions, expectations, and boundaries through which it was reconstructed. The analyst may not claim that `□` as a formal PMS operator consists of more basic empirical parts.

### 4.3 Decomposition question and expected difference

A valid occurrence begins with an explicit decomposition question, for example:

- Which components and relations constitute this compressed path?
- How is this Non-Event reproduced across expectation, delay, and changed action corridors?
- Which internal temporal phases support the claimed trajectory?
- Does the finer reconstruction preserve, revise, narrow, or defeat the coarser source function?

“Add more detail” is not a sufficient question. The operation must name the additional praxeological difference expected from finer granularity: a changed role relation, cost distribution, temporal dependency, expectation structure, source-function status, or Stop condition.

### 4.4 Components, relations, and source-function return

Component identification is necessary but insufficient. A list of fragments does not explain how the source object is constituted. `DECOMPOSE` therefore requires relation identification: sequence, dependence, containment, recurrence, asymmetry, temporal ordering, mutual constraint, or another warranted relation.

The source-function test is decisive. The finer reconstruction must state whether the coarser object or function is:

- preserved;
- refined;
- revised;
- only partially supported;
- rendered resolution-neutral;
- defeated;
- or no longer safely reconstructible.

This prevents decomposition from silently abandoning the source object. If a different path is selected and formed, the operation has become a new `COMPOSE`. If the question becomes what the source object does in another context, a `PROJECT_AS` occurrence is required.

### 4.5 No truth privilege and non-invertibility

Finer resolution has no automatic epistemic privilege:

```text
more detail
≠
more truth
≠
more relevance
≠
more authority
```

The result may legitimately be `resolution_neutral` where the finer account adds no warranted praxis difference. It may require claim reduction where some distinctions are supported but the original precision is not. It must stop where source support is exhausted, the source object dissolves, or added distinctions fall below the Relevance Floor.

Decomposition is not the inverse of composition:

```text
DECOMPOSE(COMPOSE(X))
≠
X restored without Loss
```

The composite may already have compressed or irrecoverably excluded information. New evidence, rival periodizations, and a changed decomposition question may also produce a reconstruction that was not available before composition.

### 4.6 Representative pressure cases

[`C28-TRAJECTORY-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C28-TRAJECTORY-01_Admissible_Trajectory_Decomposition.md) opens an identifiable trajectory under finer granularity while preserving its reference and testing how constituent structures reproduce the trajectory-level claim. It illustrates same-reference reconstruction rather than a replacement path.

[`C28-OPTYPE-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C28-OPTYPE-01_Operator_Decomposition_Error.md) enforces the formal boundary: an operator type is not an empirical composite. Attempting to decompose it confuses the base grammar with an occurrence-level object and fails the operation classification.

[`C28-OVERFINE-01`](../03_cases/markdown/C28-OVERFINE-01_Overfine_Analysis_below_the_Relevance_Floor.md) tests detail below the Praxeological Relevance Floor. Added distinctions that do not alter roles, action possibilities, costs, expectations, temporal relations, or claim scope do not become admissible merely because they are finer.

### 4.7 `DECOMPOSE` completion boundary

A complete occurrence must answer:

```text
What identifiable source object remains the reconstruction target?
What finer granularity is declared?
Which components and relations are source-supported?
What additional praxeological difference is expected and obtained?
How does the finer account preserve, revise, limit, or defeat the source function?
What inherited and new Loss remains?
Which rival decomposition or no-decomposition route exists?
Where does source-bounded opening stop?
```

The correct endpoint may be a bounded finer reconstruction, `resolution_neutral`, claim reduction, mandatory Stop, failure, or Non-Capture.

## 5. `PROJECT_AS`

### 5.1 Definition and signature

`PROJECT_AS` assigns an independently identified, origin-typed source object a bounded function within a declared target context while preserving source reference and origin type.

```text
origin-typed source object
→
bounded contextual target function
```

A compact signature is:

```text
PROJECT_AS:
(X_g, T_o, C_t)
→
(F_t, g', J, L, V)
```

`X_g` is the source object as reconstructed at a declared granularity, `T_o` its retained origin type, `C_t` the target context, `F_t` the claimed contextual function, `g'` the target-side granularity, `J` the justification, `L` the projection Loss, and `V` the validity scope. The expanded relational record additionally declares source and target frames, granularities, and relative levels.

The result is a contextual relation, not a rewritten source identity:

```text
X as F_t in C_t
≠
X becomes T_t as a new origin type
```

### 5.2 Source declaration and target declaration

The source must be identifiable before the target function is accepted. Its declaration retains source reference, origin type, source frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, source basis, prior disposition, uncertainty, and inherited Loss.

The target declaration names:

- the target context;
- the exact target function;
- target frame, granularity, and relative level;
- the difference the function makes in that context;
- its validity scope and expiry conditions;
- the Claim and Authority Ceilings.

A broad noun such as “frame,” “attractor,” “macro-event,” or “gate” is not enough. The function must be stated as a discriminating relation: what is organized, constrained, enabled, delayed, exposed, selected, or made unavailable because this source object performs the function in this context?

### 5.3 Constitutive source trace

Projection requires more than target usefulness. It must identify which source features carry the target function and how the function depends on them. Relevant carriers may include temporal order, recurring relations, non-events, asymmetry, exposure, branch closure, sedimentation, or binding load.

The constitutive-source question is:

> Which features of the source object make this target-side function warranted, and would the function change or fail if those features changed?

A function that survives arbitrary mutation of load-bearing source structures is source-indifferent. It is likely a label, analogy, or template assignment rather than `PROJECT_AS`.

### 5.4 Contextual boundedness and validity scope

A valid projection is local to a declared target context. It may coexist with other compatible functions, rival projections, or no projection. The target function does not automatically transfer to another context, another period, another source object with the same label, or a wider institutional claim.

```text
valid in context C₁
≠
valid in context C₂
```

Projection also remains loss-bearing. It foregrounds source features relevant to the target function and backgrounds others. It may compress internal history, exclude locally irrelevant variation, retain uncertainty, and inherit irrecoverable source Loss. None of these are reset by a new context.

### 5.5 Projection, recontextualization, analogy, and label substitution

The main boundary distinctions are:

```text
new frame or reading only
→
recontextualization
```

```text
formal or structural resemblance without semantic and praxeological continuity
→
analogy
```

```text
new terminology without source-dependent discriminative gain
→
label substitution
```

```text
source-dependent bounded function in a declared context
→
PROJECT_AS candidate
```

Analogy may be a legitimate result and maps to the canonical class `analogy_only` where useful correspondences and breaking points are retained. It is not a weaker wording for projection. Label substitution fails where the target term merely restates category membership or technical compatibility.

### 5.6 Representative pressure cases

[`C40-P1`](../03_cases/markdown/C40-P1_Trajectory_as_Bounded_Frame_Function.md) tests a trajectory as a bounded frame-function. The trajectory remains a trajectory; the target claim concerns how its source-supported history structures relevance and available interpretation in one declared context.

[`C40-X6`](../03_cases/markdown/C40-X6_Projection_versus_Structural_Analogy.md) separates projection from structural analogy. Where resemblance remains useful but source-dependent functional continuity is not established, the positive terminal route is `analogy_only`, not a failed attempt to force projection.

[`C40-N3`](../03_cases/markdown/C40-N3_PROJECT_AS_Label_Substitution_Failure.md) shows a machine-readable registry assigning an “eligibility-gate function” label to any path containing an authorization token. The label remains unchanged when the source path’s load-bearing order and continuation relations change. Technical translation succeeds, but the declared `PROJECT_AS` claim fails as source-indifferent label substitution.

### 5.7 `PROJECT_AS` completion boundary

A complete occurrence must answer:

```text
What independently identified source object enters projection?
What origin type is retained?
What target context and exact function are declared?
Which source features carry that function?
What source change would alter or defeat it?
What remains preserved, foregrounded, backgrounded, or lost?
How far and how long does the function apply?
Which rival projection, analogy, or no-projection route remains?
Why is the result more than recontextualization or renaming?
When must projection stop?
```

A valid target function increases bounded analytical legibility. It creates no new PMS primitive and carries no automatic authority beyond its claim type and independently permitted application conditions.

## 6. Classification and non-invertibility

Before recording an operation, the analyst asks in order:

1. Are multiple or sequential source structures being formed into a new composite? → `COMPOSE` candidate.
2. Is an identifiable compressed occurrence or composite being opened under finer granularity while remaining the reconstruction target? → `DECOMPOSE` candidate.
3. Is an independently identified origin-typed object being assigned a bounded function in a target context? → `PROJECT_AS` candidate.
4. Has only the frame, vocabulary, or point of view changed? → no STRATA operation yet; possible recontextualization.
5. Is only resemblance asserted? → analogy candidate.
6. Has only a label been attached? → label-substitution candidate.

The operations are not symmetric or lossless inverses:

```text
DECOMPOSE(COMPOSE(X)) ≠ X
COMPOSE(DECOMPOSE(X)) ≠ X
PROJECT_AS(X) ≠ X as a new origin type
```

A reverse movement is a new occurrence with a new record, Loss, admissibility result, and claim. This rule prevents later operations from laundering earlier failure.

# PATH, SUB, RETYPE, and LIMITS

## 1. Four Parts, not four operations

The canonical corpus is organized into four Parts:

```text
PATH
SUB
RETYPE
LIMITS
```

These are analytical and production architectures. They are not four core operations, four ontological strata, four ranks of explanation, or four sources of authority.

| Part | Primary object and question | Principal operation | Core burden | Does not provide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PATH` | How do configurations, transitions, events, non-events, branches, and alternatives form sequences, paths, trajectories, and path-dependence claims? | `COMPOSE` | selection, order, formation, sedimentation, counterfactual historical load, Loss | automatic target function, teleology, causal necessity |
| `SUB` | How may a compressed occurrence or composite be opened under finer granularity while retaining the same reference object? | `DECOMPOSE` | component and relation support, source-function return, resolution gain, source-bounded Stop | operator-type decomposition, final constituents, finer-truth privilege |
| `RETYPE` | What bounded contextual function may an origin-typed object perform in a declared target context? | `PROJECT_AS` | origin-type preservation, constitutive source trace, target-function precision, contextual validity | origin-type replacement, analogy as projection, automatic cross-context transfer |
| `LIMITS` | Under what conditions may any operation or chain continue, pass, reduce, fail, stop, or remain non-captured? | none | Relevance Floor, Traceability Ceiling, continuity, Type Integrity, Loss, source and claim ceilings, anti-immunization | a fourth operation, superior layer, substantive authority |

The Parts constrain one another through declared handoffs. No handoff transfers theoretical rank or application authority.

## 2. `PATH`

`PATH` develops the temporal object chain from configurations and transitions through sequences, paths, trajectories, and path-dependence claims. It also preserves branch structures, unrealized alternatives, expectation-grounded non-events, sedimentation, irreversibility, and weak or strong historical dependence.

Its central question is not whether events occur in time, but whether source-supported order and relations form a temporal object that changes the reconstruction. The Part therefore separates:

```text
sequence
≠
path
≠
trajectory
```

and:

```text
trajectory
≠
path dependence
```

`COMPOSE` is the principal operation because temporal objects are formed from multiple or sequential sources. The operation remains bounded by explicit selection, ordering, formation, Loss, and counterfactual sensitivity. `PATH` does not, by itself, establish that the completed path or trajectory functions as a frame, attractor, macro-event, threshold, or another contextual function. Such a claim is handed to `RETYPE` as a separately testable `PROJECT_AS` occurrence.

A formed path or trajectory may also become the source of later `DECOMPOSE`, but the reverse movement is not presumed lossless. `PATH` therefore hands `SUB` a referenced composite and its prior Loss, not an untouched original waiting to be recovered.

## 3. `SUB`

`SUB` governs finer reconstruction of provisionally compressed occurrences and composites. It asks what additional source-supported distinctions and relations become visible when granularity changes, and whether those distinctions preserve, revise, limit, or defeat the coarser source object or function.

The Part treats finer granularity as a methodological possibility rather than an epistemic rank. It maintains the distinction between:

```text
finer reconstruction of the same object
≠
formation of a different path
≠
contextual function attribution
```

`DECOMPOSE` is therefore the principal operation. `SUB` develops component identification, relation identification, internal temporality, source-function return, residual binding, resolution gain and neutrality, limits of decomposition, and the point at which the source object disappears or source support is exhausted.

Its output may strengthen discrimination within a bounded claim, but it may also be `resolution_neutral`, provisional, partially admissible, claim-reduced, stopped, failed, or non-captured. The Part does not discover final constituents and does not treat operator types as composites.

The finer constitutive trace produced in `SUB` may support a later `PROJECT_AS` claim. That handoff supplies additional source structure; it does not pre-authorize the target function.

## 4. `RETYPE`

`RETYPE` governs bounded contextual function. Its name does not authorize type replacement. The Part preserves the fundamental relation:

```text
origin type
≠
target function
```

An established source object may organize, constrain, enable, or stabilize another context in a way that warrants a function claim. `PROJECT_AS` makes that claim explicit while retaining source reference and origin type. The Part develops target declaration, constitutive source trace, counterfactual sensitivity, functional and temporal continuity, validity scope, competing projections, projection visibility and Loss, analogy boundaries, and label-substitution failures.

Because multiple functions may be compatible or rival, `RETYPE` does not seek one context-free final identity. A trajectory may perform one bounded frame-function in one context and another compatible function elsewhere. Each function requires its own context, source trace, Loss, and claim ceiling. A later projection cannot be used to rewrite the source object retrospectively.

`RETYPE` also carries a strict authority boundary. Even an admissible contextual function does not authorize diagnosis, person typing, institutional classification, policy enforcement, causal proof, or intervention.

## 5. `LIMITS`

`LIMITS` is constitutive across all operations and chains. It is not a cautionary appendix added after productive analysis; it determines whether the analysis may proceed at all.

Its responsibilities include:

- the Praxeological Relevance Floor and Traceability Ceiling;
- PraxisPurchase and TraceableLoad;
- Type Integrity;
- reference, functional, and temporal continuity;
- Contextual Boundedness;
- Counterfactual Sensitivity;
- source, calibration, Claim, and Authority Ceilings;
- exact Loss preservation;
- competing transformations and no transformation;
- anti-immunization and failure preservation;
- Optional and Mandatory Stop;
- re-entry as a new claim;
- Non-Capture.

`LIMITS` also integrates operation chains. Every link keeps its own record and result. A successful later operation cannot repair an earlier failed claim, and a new frame or granularity cannot move a counterexample out of reach.

The Part supplies the discipline that makes the three operations genuinely bounded:

```text
available transformation
≠
admissible transformation
```

## 6. Inter-Part handoffs

The architecture is directional for production and dependency, not hierarchical in authority.

- Foundations hand `PATH` the temporal object chain, analytical coordinates, operation identities, Shared Record, and initial admissibility logic.
- `PATH` hands `SUB` composed paths and trajectories that may be opened without an inverse presumption.
- `PATH` and `SUB` hand `RETYPE` independently referenced source objects and constitutive traces.
- `PATH`, `SUB`, and `RETYPE` hand `LIMITS` local failures, Loss profiles, alternatives, Stop conditions, output mappings, and chain conflicts.
- `LIMITS` hands the Conclusion an integrated boundary; the Conclusion may summarize but not strengthen it.

The Reader and reference routes mirror these dependencies for navigation. They do not become additional theory layers.

## 7. Architectural boundary

The four-Part organization permits concentrated development without reopening definitions in every chapter:

```text
each concept is defined once,
operationalized locally,
tested repeatedly,
and not re-derived without necessity
```

The architecture is complete only while the distinctions remain active:

```text
Part
≠
operation
≠
object type
≠
relative level
≠
authority rank
```

`PATH`, `SUB`, and `RETYPE` provide three distinct transformation families. `LIMITS` prevents their availability from becoming compulsory recursion, totalization, or authority inflation.

# Admissibility Band and Mandatory Tests

## 1. Admissibility is claim-specific

A STRATA operation is not admissible merely because its syntax can be stated or its result can be represented. Admissibility concerns one delimited transformation claim:

```text
operation T
applied to source object X
within context C
for claim Q
under source basis S
```

The same source object may support one operation, require reduction under another, yield a resolution-neutral result at a finer granularity, or remain non-captured under the present sources. Objects and concepts are therefore not globally admissible. The assessed unit is the declared transformation occurrence or separately delimited chain claim.

## 2. The operating band

The Admissibility Band is bounded below by praxeological relevance and above by source traceability:

```text
Praxeological Traceability Ceiling
─────────────────────────────
admissible transformation
─────────────────────────────
Praxeological Relevance Floor
```

The diagram is topological and procedural rather than metric. The Traceability Ceiling is shown above the admissible range and the Relevance Floor below it; this orientation names the upper and lower bounds without implying a numerical scale.

Below the **Praxeological Relevance Floor**, an added distinction, decomposition, composite, or function claim produces no warranted change in the reconstruction of praxis. More information may be accurate while remaining irrelevant to the tested claim. The decisive question is:

> Which warranted statement must be formulated differently because of this transformation?

A finer reconstruction may reveal a constitutive relation, redistribute reconstructed costs or roles, revise a path, expose a countercase, or change the Claim Ceiling. Where no relevant reconstruction changes, the correct result may be `resolution_neutral`; further recursion may require Stop.

Above the **Praxeological Traceability Ceiling**, the result no longer carries reconstructible load from its declared sources. Citation is insufficient. Traceable load requires identifiable source objects, structural mapping, result dependence, relevant temporality and relations, disclosed selection and Loss, and a claim limited to what the source trace can support. A macro-label, fragment list, or target function that remains unchanged when its alleged load-bearing source changes exceeds the Ceiling.

The Band is relational rather than metrically fixed. STRATA supplies no universal smallest relevant distinction, finest permissible granularity, largest legitimate composite, or fixed threshold at which a path becomes a trajectory or an analogy becomes a projection.

## 3. Mandatory checks

The operational burden can be summarized in seven connected check families:

| Check family | Required question |
|---|---|
| PraxisPurchase and TraceableLoad | Does the transformation change a warranted praxis reconstruction, and does the result remain dependent on reconstructible source load? |
| TypeIntegrity | Are operator type, occurrence, composite, derived object, origin type, and contextual function kept distinct? |
| Reference and Functional Continuity | Is the required reference preserved, and is the relevant function retained, revised, rejected, or newly projected with warrant? |
| Contextual Boundedness | Are frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, target context, validity scope, and excluded reach explicit? |
| Counterfactual Sensitivity | Would a source-supported change in a load-bearing feature alter the result in the expected direction? |
| Source and Claim Ceiling | Does the assertion remain within source precision, calibration, inferential distance, generality, and permitted authority? |
| Stop and Non-Capture | Is continuation necessary, optional, prohibited, failed, or unable to preserve an adequate capture claim? |

The complete formal audit coordinates these burdens through twelve ordered stages: source and claim entry; operation classification; Relevance Floor; Traceability Ceiling; continuity and type integrity; Counterfactual Sensitivity; Loss and selection; alternatives; source and calibration limits; anti-immunization; Stop and Non-Capture; and Claim and Authority Ceiling. Output routing follows the audit and is not a thirteenth stage.

The stages coordinate exactly sixteen Rule carriers:

```text
praxis_purchase
traceable_load
type_integrity
reference_continuity
functional_continuity
temporal_continuity
contextual_boundedness
counterfactual_sensitivity
selection_and_loss
alternatives
source_ceiling
calibration
stop
non_capture
claim_ceiling
authority_ceiling
```

Anti-immunization is a mandatory Stage-10 contract across these findings, not a seventeenth Rule.

## 4. Non-compensation and decision order

Admissibility is not a score. The load-bearing conditions cannot compensate for one another:

```text
high detail            ≠ PraxisPurchase
many citations         ≠ TraceableLoad
source trace           ≠ valid type transition
schema completeness    ≠ bounded context or claim
formal elegance        ≠ substantive passage
```

A later success likewise does not erase an earlier failed gate. Where a load-bearing condition fails, the route must preserve the failure through claim reduction, Mandatory Stop, `failed_transformation`, or `non_capture`, as appropriate. A weak or provisional result may remain available, but an unqualified admissibility claim may not be assembled from strengths elsewhere in the record.

## 5. Formal carriers and their limit

`Admissibility_Rules.yaml` represents the Rule inventory and operation-specific overlays. `Boundary_Decision_Tree.yaml` represents controlled routing questions. Appendix N joins the twelve stages, sixteen Rules, ten Output-Class candidates, claim segmentation, collision adjudication, Loss, Stop, and chain handoff into a reusable workflow.

These carriers can require fields, controlled values, explicit acknowledgments, and route consistency. They cannot determine whether a stated source trace is substantively adequate, whether a counterfactual is empirically sound, or whether the selected Output Class is true of the world. Formal consistency remains one audit layer rather than an authority upgrade.

# Shared Transformation Record, Loss, and Outputs

## 1. One semantic envelope, two record scopes

The Shared Transformation Record makes the commitments of a transformation inspectable. It has a common semantic envelope:

```text
source
operation or chain
target
admissibility
loss
alternatives
governance
status and result
```

An **operation-occurrence record** contains exactly one `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, or `PROJECT_AS` occurrence. An **integrated-chain record** contains no operation object; it references independently adjudicated occurrence records in order and states a separate chain claim. A human-readable package narrative may explain shared context, but it cannot replace either record type.

The serialization adds identity, provenance, claim lineage, controlled extensions, and formal-diagnostic routing. An incomplete packet is not automatically a failed transformation, Stop, or Non-Capture. It may remain a formal diagnostic that preserves available material without fabricating a canonical Output Class.

## 2. Record duties

A routed occurrence record declares:

- one bounded claim, Claim Ceiling, validity scope, and authority boundary;
- source object, origin typing, analytical coordinates, Source Basis, Constitutive Source Trace, known gaps, and Source Ceiling;
- one registered operation occurrence and its operation-specific payload;
- target object and, for `PROJECT_AS`, a bounded contextual function;
- all twelve audit stages and sixteen Rule assessments;
- alternatives, no-transformation, and non-translation routes;
- exact five-part Loss;
- governance fields, including `authority_inheritance: prohibited`;
- operation-specific result, separate status axes, candidate assessment, one selected canonical class, and current Claim Disposition.

This separation matters:

```text
operation-specific result
≠ support status
≠ resolution result
≠ capture statement
≠ Claim Disposition
≠ canonical Output Class
```

A valid `DECOMPOSE`, for example, may reject a prior source-function claim while remaining an admissible decomposition. The operation result and the disposition of the earlier claim must be recorded separately.

## 3. Five-part Loss

Every routed record declares exactly:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

The categories are not interchangeable. `compressed` does not guarantee later recovery; `uncertain` is not concealed exclusion; `irrecoverable` states a record-relative recovery limit and does not prove that the source never contained the information. Empty categories must be declared and justified rather than omitted.

Loss is occurrence-specific. In a chain, the ordered sequence `⟨L1, L2, …, Ln⟩` travels forward as burden. It is never summed, averaged, or overwritten by a later successful operation.

## 4. Compact illustrative record view

The following non-schema-complete view compresses the existing paired case `C47-CP1A`; the linked YAML remains the authoritative record:

```yaml
record_id: case.c47-cp1a.compose.01
record_scope: operation_occurrence
claim: A1–A4 and their declared relations form bounded composite Q47
operation:
  kind: COMPOSE
  expected_difference: distributed access and load transfer become one relational object
target:
  object: Q47
  contextual_function: null
loss:
  preserved: [occurrence boundaries, relation topology, no-function boundary]
  compressed: [non-load-bearing micro-actions]
  excluded: [automatic target function, person or application authority]
  uncertain: [relative contribution weights]
  irrecoverable: [unencoded microhistory]
result:
  operation_specific: bounded relational composite formation
  canonical_class: admissible_with_bounded_claim
governance:
  authority_inheritance: prohibited
```

The record makes visible why `COMPOSE` succeeds and why its success remains narrower than a later function claim.

## 5. Closed output system

Every routed claim selects exactly one of ten canonical Output Classes:

```text
admissible
admissible_with_bounded_claim
admissible_but_provisional
resolution_neutral
analogy_only
partially_admissible
claim_reduction_required
mandatory_stop
failed_transformation
non_capture
```

All ten candidates are assessed before selection. Candidate plurality does not create a class stack. First, heterogeneous claims are segmented into separate claims and records. If several candidates genuinely address the same claim, their constitutive conditions are compared, non-selected findings are preserved, and exactly one class is selected with a rationale. There is no default precedence, weighted score, majority vote, or first-match rule.

Stop, failure, and Non-Capture remain distinct. `mandatory_stop` governs inadmissible continuation; `failed_transformation` records that the proposed operation did not establish its defining result; `non_capture` records an adequate bounded attempt whose whole claim cannot be retained without distortion. Optional Stop is a methodological concept, not an eleventh Output Class.

## 6. Validation layers

The record participates in five separate validation layers: parseability, schema conformance, package consistency, substantive audit, and external warrant. The lower layers do not entail the higher ones:

```text
parseable or schema-valid
≠ substantively admissible

substantively audited
≠ externally warranted application
```

# Chains, Re-entry, and Anti-Immunization

## 1. A chain is not a fourth operation

STRATA permits operation sequences including:

```text
COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS
COMPOSE → DECOMPOSE
DECOMPOSE → COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE → PROJECT_AS
PROJECT_AS → DECOMPOSE
COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS → DECOMPOSE
```

A chain is an ordered relation among already separate operation occurrences. Every occurrence retains its own source, target, claim, coordinates, justification, expected difference, audit, Loss, Output Class, Claim Disposition, and possible Stop or Non-Capture. The chain record states an additional chain-level claim and audits each handoff. It does not merge local operations or average their results.

For every handoff `Hi,i+1`, the audit checks that the target reference of `Ti` matches the declared source reference of `Ti+1`; origin type and contextual function remain distinct; coordinate changes are declared; prior Loss travels forward; prior failure and Stop remain visible; and the successor claim is independently delimited.

```text
admissible step 1
≠ admissible step 2

class(chain claim)
≠ average of local classes
```

## 2. Complete continuity chain

`C47-CP1` provides the compact positive model. `C47-CP1A` composes four bounded local asymmetry occurrences and their load-transfer relations into derived relational composite `Q47`. The result is `admissible_with_bounded_claim` and explicitly supplies no target function.

The handoff preserves the exact `Q47` referent, origin type, source occurrences, relation topology, temporal order, uncertainty, Loss, and no-function boundary. `C47-CP1B` then tests a new `PROJECT_AS` claim: within target field `H47`, does the same composite carry a bounded higher-level access function? Source variation and a comparison field pressure that function. The local projection is again `admissible_with_bounded_claim`.

A required countercase, `C47-CP1C`, reuses the name while changing the referent and removing load-bearing relations. Its target remains insensitive to the alleged source. The projection fails. This later failure neither invalidates the earlier bounded successes nor becomes repairable through nominal sameness.

## 3. Failure-preservation chain

`C50-FP1` demonstrates anti-immunization. The original claim `C50-FP1A` projects `T50` as a stabilization trajectory, but removing `T50` changes no target interpretation or access. The projection remains `failed_transformation`.

A successor `DECOMPOSE` claim legitimately reopens the same reference as a narrower request–hold/non-event–repair–release path. Arbitrary timestamp subdivision, however, adds no praxis difference and receives `mandatory_stop`. A later `COMPOSE` combines the warranted path with external gate `G50` into new composite `K50`, and a new `PROJECT_AS` claim tests a bounded reopening-threshold function.

The later operations may succeed because they test new objects and claims. They do not repair the original projection:

```text
K50
≠ T50 repaired

later success
≠ erased failure
≠ inherited claim ceiling
≠ inherited authority
```

## 4. Re-entry after Stop or failure

Re-entry is not silent continuation. It requires a new record and changed grounds, such as new sources, a different claim, a newly warranted granularity, a material counterstructure, or a new calibration basis. The new record must point to the prior claim and preserve its disposition through lineage fields.

A changed frame, granularity, relative level, composite, or target function may be analytically useful. It also changes the claim under test:

```text
new transformation
=
new testable claim
```

The analyst must therefore state whether the new operation answers the earlier objection or merely moves it. Unanswered counterexamples, prior Claim Reduction, Mandatory Stop, failure, and Non-Capture remain part of the record history.

## 5. Integrated chain and apparent reversal

`C53-CPD1` extends the valid `COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS` chain with a `DECOMPOSE` claim. The attempted claim is complete, exact, lossless recovery of `Q47`'s constituent history after projection. The source supports only a bounded reopening. Contribution weights remain unknown, compressed microhistory remains unavailable, prior Loss remains attached, and the contextual function is not a source container.

The final occurrence therefore requires claim reduction, while the integrated chain view is `partially_admissible`. Two bounded local successes do not make the chain lossless or invertible. The earlier source-insensitive failure also remains failed.

## 6. Chain governance

Chain visibility improves auditability, not authority. A complete graph, successful schema validation, or coherent narrative cannot confer empirical proof, causal proof, legitimacy, person typing, recommendation, sanction, or application authority. The universal governance field remains:

```yaml
governance:
  authority_inheritance: prohibited
```

## 5. `C28-OVERFINE-01` — detail below the Relevance Floor

This case begins after an adequate review-occurrence reconstruction has already exposed the relevant access, threshold, closure, and repair relations. A further `DECOMPOSE` adds source-supported presentation and microtiming distinctions. The added details are not false, and they remain traceable. The question is whether they change a warranted praxis reconstruction.

They do not. Removing the added microdetail leaves the relevant access topology, responsibility relation, closure condition, repair corridor, and claim ceiling unchanged. The proposed continuation therefore has no additional PraxisPurchase. Its failure lies below the Praxeological Relevance Floor, not above the Traceability Ceiling.

The correct result is `mandatory_stop`. The Stop does not erase the earlier adequate reconstruction and does not classify the factual microdetail as invalid. It prevents supported but analytically non-purchasing distinctions from accumulating merely because finer resolution remains technically possible. Repeated presentation detail is compressed; Resolution Gain, stronger source function, person inference, and authority are excluded; and future materially new evidence remains possible.

The record is especially important because it defeats a common resolution bias:

```text
source-supported detail
≠ warranted continuation

finer granularity
≠ deeper truth
```

A future decomposition may re-enter only on changed grounds—for example, new evidence that makes one of the microdistinctions relevant to access, burden, timing, responsibility, or another declared praxis difference. Repeating the same subdivision under a new label would not satisfy re-entry.

## 6. `C40-P1` — bounded `PROJECT_AS`

A comparison context `Z` contains a role reset and released binding. There the same formal reopening rule carries the target reconstruction without additional historical framing. The contrast prevents the claim from collapsing into the unrestricted assertion that “history matters.”

The origin type remains `trajectory`. The target function is contextual, role-specific, interval-bounded, and counterfactually defeasible. Timely review, released residual binding, complete repair, an unaffected role relation, or removal of the `C1`/`Z` difference narrows or defeats the projection. Other roles, periods, contexts, deterministic reach, causal monopoly, Frame identity, person typing, legitimacy, recommendation, and authority are excluded.

## 7. `C40-X6` — analogy is not projection

Four formal correspondences are retained: ordered phases resemble ordered states; a missed expected review resembles a skipped check node; persistent workaround resembles repeated fallback; and partial repair with residual burden resembles rollback with residual state.

Neither is present.

# Formal Model and Implementation Boundary

## 1. Function of the formal layer

The formal model makes the declared STRATA architecture machine-readable and cross-checkable. It registers operation identities, admissibility rules, Output Classes, post-audit routes, record syntax, dependency relations, inventory bindings, and integrity conditions. Its value is exactness of declared form: a validator can detect a fourth operation, an eleventh Output Class, a missing Loss field, an unresolved identifier, a prohibited scoring structure, an invalid record shape, or a checksum mismatch.

That exactness does not turn the model into an independent theory source. The formal layer is downstream of PMS Base, the STRATA architecture, the canonical Blocks, and the binding compact controls. It mirrors owner-controlled semantics and tests their declared relations. It may not revise them through a more convenient YAML structure or schema constraint.

```text
machine-readable operationalization
→ inspectable inventories, dependencies, records, and routes

≠ new PMS primitive
≠ substitute for canonical prose
≠ substantive judgment engine
```

The root model therefore includes its own non-authority statement. Manifest validity, dependency consistency, checksum agreement, schema validity, and workflow completeness do not establish substantive admissibility, empirical truth, actual causality, semantic or normative validity, person judgment, legitimacy, application authority, or completeness of capture.

## 2. Root assembly and dependency graph

[`PMS-STRATA.yaml`](../07_model/PMS-STRATA.yaml) is the formal-model root. It is a manifest and assembly contract, not a sixth semantic component. It registers five active components:

| Component | Owned formal role |
|---|---|
| [`Operation_Registry.yaml`](../07_model/Operation_Registry.yaml) | the three operation identities, signatures, applicability, occurrence burdens, Loss contract, and chain constraints |
| [`Output_Classes.yaml`](../07_model/Output_Classes.yaml) | the closed ten-class inventory, class meanings, semantic boundaries, and claim/continuation effects |
| [`Admissibility_Rules.yaml`](../07_model/Admissibility_Rules.yaml) | the sixteen Rule semantics, controlled local findings, non-compensation, and twelve-stage audit contract |
| [`Boundary_Decision_Tree.yaml`](../07_model/Boundary_Decision_Tree.yaml) | post-audit candidate generation, claim segmentation, class-collision adjudication, and unique final route selection |
| [`Transformation_Record.schema.json`](../07_model/Transformation_Record.schema.json) | concrete syntax, requiredness, conditional payloads, pointers, and structural consistency for occurrence and chain records |

[`PMS-STRATA.schema.json`](../07_model/PMS-STRATA.schema.json) is a validation companion for the root instance. It is not counted as a semantic component and may not duplicate component semantics.

The dependency graph is directional and owner-preserving:

```text
Operation Registry
→ Output Classes
→ Admissibility Rules
→ Boundary Decision Tree
→ Transformation Record Schema
→ Root assembly
```

The compact arrow sequence is only an orientation. The registered graph contains explicit semantic-dependency, record-encoding, assembly-handoff, and validation-relation edges. An upstream component owns a semantic inventory; a downstream component may consume that inventory without acquiring ownership. The root receives assembly handoffs from the components, while the components do not semantically depend on the root merely because they are registered there.

```text
dependency order
≠ authority rank

assembly sink
≠ superior semantic owner
```

This distinction prevents two forms of drift. First, the root cannot become Meta-PMS by collecting the components. Second, a downstream schema cannot redefine the operation or class whose spelling it encodes.

## 3. Component responsibilities

### 3.1 Operation Registry

The Operation Registry fixes the closed vocabulary:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

It carries the shared operation envelope, operation-specific applicability, source/target burdens, five-field Loss inventory, and chain constraints. It also preserves the prohibition on decomposing operator types and on treating a chain as a fourth operation. It does not decide whether a concrete occurrence is substantively admissible.

### 3.2 Output Classes

The Output Classes component owns exactly ten canonical classes and their semantic boundaries. It distinguishes, among other collisions, bounded admissibility from provisionality, partiality from Non-Capture, `mandatory_stop` from `failed_transformation`, and `analogy_only` from a failed projection claim.

The classes are not scores, ranks, or points on a better-to-worse scale. One delimited tested claim receives exactly one canonical class after the applicable audit pattern and class-boundary questions have been addressed. A selected label remains a formalized result statement, not a truth certificate or application instruction.

### 3.3 Admissibility Rules

The Admissibility Rules component owns the sixteen canonical Rule carriers and their controlled local vocabularies. It binds them into the twelve-stage substantive audit while preserving non-compensation. One strong dimension cannot offset a missing constitutive condition, and one Rule finding cannot mechanically select the final Output Class.

The component may represent local results, triggered payloads, Stop conditions, re-entry burdens, Non-Capture conditions, and operation-specific overlays. It cannot perform the source interpretation or domain judgment needed to justify those entries.

### 3.4 Boundary Decision Tree

The Boundary Decision Tree begins **after** the applicable substantive audit pattern is available. It does not create a thirteenth audit stage. Its task is to:

1. check routing readiness and claim delimitation;
2. generate one or more supported class candidates;
3. keep claim disposition separate from record status;
4. resolve declared class collisions through constitutive boundary questions;
5. select one canonical Output Class or emit a non-canonical formal diagnostic when the packet is incomplete or unresolved.

Candidate multiplicity is not class stacking. Branch order is evaluative, not a ranking. First-match selection, weighted scoring, threshold sums, and default class precedence are prohibited.

### 3.5 Transformation Record Schema

The record schema encodes one operation occurrence or one integrated chain record. Its top-level carriers include record identity and scope, routing state, metadata, claim, source, operation or chain, target, admissibility, Loss, alternatives, governance, result, relations, extensions, and formal diagnostics.

The schema can require fields and conditional payloads appropriate to a declared record scope. It can reject invalid enum values or structurally inconsistent combinations. It cannot determine whether a source trace is truthful, whether a relation is constitutive, whether a counterfactual is substantively adequate, or whether a claim deserves its declared result.

```text
schema-valid record
≠ admissible transformation

complete record shape
≠ complete capture
```

## 4. Integrity bindings and validation layers

The root binds the current external PMS dependency, governing controls, supporting reference inputs, active formal components, and validation companion through registered SHA-256 fingerprints. It also mirrors closed inventories such as the three operations, ten Output Classes, sixteen Rules, twelve audit stages, and five Loss fields.

Three validation layers must remain distinct.

### 4.1 Static schema validation

JSON Schema can test the root and record instance shapes, required fields, allowed values, descriptor structures, formats, and selected cross-field constraints. This is local structural validation.

### 4.2 Package-aware validation

A package resolver or repository audit is required for facts that JSON Schema cannot establish alone, including:

- file existence at registered paths;
- actual checksum calculation;
- component ID, version, and status agreement;
- cross-file inventory equality;
- dependency endpoint resolution and cycle checks;
- claim, record, segment, and chain-reference resolution;
- consistency of source/target identities across linked records;
- correspondence between final routes and the ten canonical classes.

The checked examples under [`07_model/examples/`](../07_model/examples/) exercise this layer as smoke records. They demonstrate that representative formal packets can be parsed and validated against the current model. They do not add empirical evidence or establish that the represented transformations are true outside their synthetic test conditions.

### 4.3 Substantive judgment

No formal executor is assigned to substantive judgment. Human and domain-grounded inquiry remains responsible for source adequacy, semantic interpretation, praxeological relevance, constitutive relations, counterfactual plausibility, calibration, rivalry, and claim scope.

The formal model may validate declarations concerning these matters. It cannot originate their warrant.

## 5. Implementation ceiling

Formal and software artifacts may validly support:

- inventory and identifier consistency;
- record and route completeness;
- declared dependency and ownership checks;
- syntax and type constraints;
- integrity verification;
- reproducible inspection of the transformation path;
- detection of prohibited formal structures.

They may not decide:

- empirical truth or actual causality;
- semantic or normative validity;
- psychological state, diagnosis, or person evaluation;
- political or legal legitimacy;
- material source sufficiency;
- superiority of one reconstruction beyond the supplied grounds;
- completeness of capture;
- intervention, sanction, recommendation, or application authority.

```text
formal validity
≠ empirical adequacy

cross-artifact consistency
≠ semantic correctness

selected route
≠ application authorization

implementation completeness
≠ PMS completeness
```

The formal layer is strongest when it makes an error locatable. It can show that a record omits a required Loss channel, that a route cites an unknown class, or that a component checksum changed without a root update. It cannot manufacture the missing source, settle a disputed meaning, or turn internal legibility into external warrant.

# Governance, Limits, and Non-Capture

## 1. Limits are constitutive

STRATA's limits are not disclaimers placed after an otherwise expansionary method. They determine whether an analytical movement may occur, what it may claim, how it must preserve objection and Loss, and when it must stop. `LIMITS` is therefore one of the four Parts and a cross-cutting control structure, while remaining neither a fourth operation nor a superior meta-level.

The governing form is:

```text
analytical availability
≠ admissibility

successful transformation
≠ unrestricted claim

more structure
≠ more authority
```

A bounded method must be able to return successful, restricted, provisional, neutral, analogical, partial, reductive, stopped, failed, and non-capturing results. If every new distinction or transformation had to be retained as progress, STRATA would become an anti-objection mechanism rather than a transformation discipline.

## 2. No ontology of strata

STRATA uses level, part/whole, local/composite, sequence/trajectory, and source/function language because its operations require relational coordinates. It does not infer discrete layers of reality from those coordinates.

An analytical level is a declared relation involving a reference object, Frame, granularity, source/target relation, temporal scope, and Claim Scope. “Micro,” “meso,” “macro,” “higher,” and “lower” are recoverable local shorthand, not universal classes. A stable or repeatedly successful reconstruction may be robust for its claim without becoming an independently existing stratum.

```text
analytical relation
≠ ontological layer

DECOMPOSE toward finer reconstruction
≠ descent to fundamentals

COMPOSE toward a wider object
≠ ascent to a more real whole

PROJECT_AS into a target context
≠ ontological promotion
```

The anti-ontology boundary does not imply that reconstructed structures are unreal or merely linguistic. Source-supported arrangements, trajectories, asymmetries, and constraints can be materially consequential. The denied inference concerns intrinsic layer status, not the seriousness of the reconstructed praxis structure.

## 3. No privilege of direction, resolution, or composition

Finer reconstruction can expose a hidden relation; it can also create noise, exceed the sources, fragment the reference object, or fall below the Praxeological Relevance Floor. A coarser reconstruction can compress important detail; it can also preserve the decisive relation more economically and traceably for a declared claim.

Likewise, a broader composite can expose distributed relations while concealing contradiction, excluded alternatives, or relation topology. Higher composition carries no automatic explanatory, causal, truth, or authority privilege.

```text
finer
≠ truer automatically

coarser
≠ inferior automatically

higher composition
≠ greater explanation automatically
```

Two reconstructions at different granularities may remain co-valid for distinct claims. They may also be compatible, competing, incomparable, or non-translatable for a specific predicate. STRATA does not force a merger merely to produce one scale-independent representation.

The correct comparison is performance-relative and claim-specific. It asks which reconstruction preserves the relevant relations, carries adequate source load, changes the warranted praxis reconstruction, exposes its Loss, and stays within its Claim Ceiling. Direction itself answers none of those questions.

## 4. Source Ceiling and calibration limits

A transformation cannot carry more source support than its inputs and reconstruction path provide. The Source Ceiling limits what the available material can warrant. Formal completeness, repeated coding, technical precision, or an elaborate composite cannot raise that ceiling.

Missing information must also remain distinct from a PMS Non-Event. A source gap becomes evidence for Λ only when the relevant expectation, Frame, opportunity, temporal condition, and structural significance are independently supported. Otherwise it remains missing or unavailable information.

Calibration concerns the thresholds and comparison conditions under which a distinction or transformation becomes discriminating. A method may preserve a bounded invariant while leaving its exact threshold open. Such a result can be `admissible_but_provisional`, require claim reduction, or support Stop, depending on the complete audit pattern. Calibration openness is not permission to use an arbitrary threshold and is not automatically Non-Capture.

```text
source abundance
≠ source sufficiency

formal precision
≠ calibrated threshold

missing information
≠ non-event
```

Where the source basis cannot support the requested claim, the method must reduce, stop, fail, retain rivals, or record Non-Capture rather than allowing structural elegance to fill the gap.

## 5. Claim Ceiling and Authority Ceiling

The Claim Ceiling states the strongest assertion warranted by a record's sources, coordinates, continuity, sensitivity, Loss, alternatives, and calibration. The Authority Ceiling blocks categories of authority that STRATA cannot generate even when a transformation is structurally admissible.

```text
claim ceiling
≠ authority ceiling

structural admissibility
≠ empirical or causal proof
≠ normative validity
≠ person judgment
≠ legal or political legitimacy
≠ intervention warrant
≠ application authority
```

Authority inheritance is prohibited. No claim becomes more authoritative because it is finer, more composite, higher-level, formally validated, implemented in software, shown in a Reader, or placed later in a successful chain. A target function does not inherit the authority of an origin type; a composite does not inherit more warrant than its source records can carry; and a derivative publication does not transmit authority back into the corpus.

The person boundary follows directly. STRATA reconstructs scene-bound structures and functions. It does not diagnose, type, rank, sanction, or assign irreversible identities to persons.

```text
configuration performs an asymmetry-function
≠ person is asymmetry
```

## 6. Stop as a positive result

Stop preserves method integrity by refusing continuation that is unnecessary or inadmissible.

**Optional Stop** is available where further transformation could be formally possible but adds no needed praxeological purchase for the declared task. It prevents analytical excess without asserting that further detail is false.

`mandatory_stop` applies where continuation would itself violate the method—for example by exceeding source support, losing the reference object, repeating an unchanged failed route, or continuing below the Relevance Floor. It is one of the ten canonical Output Classes and requires a recorded reason and re-entry condition.

```text
Stop
≠ substantive finding about the source object
≠ diagnosis
≠ proof that no other reconstruction exists
```

Re-entry is not “continue anyway.” It requires a separately bounded record and materially changed grounds, such as new sources, a different claim, a warranted granularity, a new comparison, or a changed calibration basis. The prior Stop, Loss, objection, and disposition remain visible.

## 7. Non-Capture as an available output

`non_capture` is available where adequate bounded attempts do not preserve the required capture object without unacceptable distortion, erasure, unsupported closure, or forced translation. It protects the possibility that relevant structure remains outside the current reconstruction, operation vocabulary, source basis, or grammar.

Non-Capture is not a default for missing data, an honorific for complexity, or a shield for a weak claim. Before it is selected, the audit must distinguish it from:

- an invalid or insufficiently supported preferred transformation;
- a claim that can be narrowed to a warranted form;
- a merely provisional calibration state;
- a partial result whose captured and uncaptured parts can be separated;
- a Mandatory Stop on an unchanged continuation route;
- an ordinary operation failure.

```text
failed candidate
≠ non_capture automatically

missing source
≠ non_capture automatically

complexity
≠ non_capture automatically
```

The positive role of Non-Capture is methodological openness. It permits STRATA to retain rival reconstructions, non-translation, unresolved remainder, or an outside-the-current-grammar result without forcing false completeness. It does not prove that the object is unknowable or permanently uncapturable.

## 8. Governance symmetry

The final scope of STRATA is defined by positive capabilities and matching negative ceilings:

| Positive capability | Negative ceiling |
|---|---|
| controlled composition | no ultimate totality or automatic macro-authority |
| finer reconstruction | no final constituents or finer-resolution privilege |
| bounded functional projection | no automatic retyping or primitive creation |
| explicit formal model | no empirical, semantic, normative, or application judgment engine |
| Stop and re-entry discipline | no Stop-as-finding or silent continuation |
| Non-Capture | no immunity for weak claims or metaphysical impossibility claim |
| increased internal legibility | no conversion of legibility into truth, completeness, or authority |

These ceilings do not withdraw the method's capabilities. They state what the capabilities do not entail. That symmetry is the final governance condition of the architecture.

# Conclusion

PMS-STRATA contributes a controlled method for analytical movements that PMS reconstructions may require but must not perform implicitly. It recognizes exactly three operations:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

They are organized through `PATH`, `SUB`, and `RETYPE`, while `LIMITS` governs every occurrence and chain without becoming a fourth operation. The method distinguishes operator type from occurrence and composite, Frame from granularity and relative level, sequence from path and trajectory, origin type from target function, and projection from analogy or label substitution.

Its principal technical contribution is not analytical expansion by itself. It is **bounded transformability**. Every operation must expose its source, object, coordinates, expected difference, source trace, continuity burdens, counterfactual sensitivity, five-field Loss, alternatives, calibration and source limits, Claim Ceiling, Authority Ceiling, Stop conditions, and possible Non-Capture. Each chain occurrence remains separately recorded, and no later transformation erases an earlier failure.

The Admissibility Band prevents two symmetrical forms of excess:

```text
distinction without praxeological purchase
← Praxeological Relevance Floor

Praxeological Traceability Ceiling →
abstraction without traceable load
```

Within the Band, successful transformation still produces no authority inflation. Finer detail is not automatically truer. A larger composite is not an ultimate whole. A bounded target function is not a new origin type or PMS primitive. Formal validity, schema conformance, Reader visibility, and implementation completeness remain distinct from empirical adequacy, semantic or normative validity, person judgment, and application authority.

Stop and Non-Capture are therefore positive capacities of the method. Stop prevents unnecessary or inadmissible continuation. Non-Capture preserves relevant remainder, rivalry, or non-translation after adequate bounded attempts rather than forcing a false result. Neither outcome licenses metaphysical conclusions, and neither may be used to protect a weak claim from reduction or failure.

The bounded final contribution can be stated as follows:

> **PMS-STRATA specifies how praxis structures may be composed, decomposed, and functionally projected across declared granularities and relative levels while preserving source traceability, origin-type integrity, transformation Loss, claim boundaries, Stop, and Non-Capture. It increases the internal legibility and criticizability of transformation claims. It does not turn analytical recursion, formalization, implementation, or repository scale into ontological totality, greater authority, or immunity from failure.**

This whitepaper is one downstream route into that method. The canonical return path remains the repository itself:

```text
PMS Base
→ STRATA Structure
→ canonical Blocks
→ binding Minified Controls
→ formal, operational, case, and reference carriers
```

The durable reduction is unchanged:

```text
more structure
≠ more authority
```
