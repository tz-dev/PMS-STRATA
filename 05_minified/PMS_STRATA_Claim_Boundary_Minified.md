# PMS-STRATA — Claim Boundary Minified

**Status:** canonical control artifact for corpus production  
**Dependency:** PMS Base as represented by the repository-reference `PMS.yaml`  
**Function:** compact boundary against which all STRATA prose, cases, formalizations, derivatives, and reader functions must be checked

---

## 1. Governing Claim

> **PMS-STRATA specifies how praxis structures may be composed, decomposed, and functionally projected across declared granularities and relative levels—while preserving source traceability, origin-type integrity, transformation loss, claim boundaries, stop, and non-capture. It does not turn analytical recursion into ontological totality, greater authority, or immunity from failure.**

STRATA is a bounded transformation discipline within PMS. It makes vertical analytical operations explicit, typed, inspectable, and criticizable.

It extends the explicit method available to PMS. It does not extend the authority, ontology, operator inventory, or application permissions of PMS.

---

## 2. Positive Scope

PMS-STRATA may specify:

- temporal composition from configurations and transitions into sequences, paths, trajectories, and other declared composites;
- decomposition of provisionally compressed operator occurrences and composite structures under finer granularity;
- bounded functional projection of origin-typed objects within declared target contexts;
- preservation, compression, exclusion, uncertainty, and irrecoverable loss across transformations;
- admissibility boundaries for analytical movement;
- competing transformations and non-translation;
- claim reduction, failure, mandatory stop, and non-capture;
- formal records and machine-readable representations of these operations and their boundary conditions.

Its three canonical operations are:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

No additional core STRATA operation is assumed unless explicitly versioned into the project.

---

## 3. Dependency and Base-Grammar Boundary

PMS Base remains the sole theoretical foundation of STRATA.

STRATA uses the existing Δ–Ψ grammar and must not silently:

- add, remove, rename, or reorder base operators;
- alter the default PMS dependency path;
- decompose a base operator as though it were an empirical composite;
- convert a derived analytical object into an independent primitive;
- redefine a PMS operator through a higher-level function;
- override PMS application guardrails while claiming PMS conformity.

The following distinction is mandatory:

```text
operator type
≠
operator occurrence
```

STRATA may decompose concrete occurrences and composites. It does not decompose the formal operator types of PMS Base.

---

## 4. Object and Level Boundary

STRATA operates on declared analytical objects and relations, including:

- configurations;
- events and non-events;
- transitions;
- sequences, paths, and trajectories;
- operator-typed occurrences;
- composite structures;
- derived analytical objects;
- contextual target functions.

It must preserve the distinctions:

```text
frame
≠
granularity
≠
relative level
```

A frame is a contextual boundary of reconstruction.  
Granularity is the resolution of distinctions.  
Relative level is an object's position within a declared compositional or functional relation.

No one of these implies the others automatically.

Micro, meso, and macro may be used only as local relational shorthand. They are not universal classes or ontological layers.

---

## 5. No Meta-PMS

PMS-STRATA is not a superior layer above PMS Base.

It does not:

- validate PMS from an external meta-position;
- correct or replace the Δ–Ψ grammar;
- inherit authority from analytical height or recursive reach;
- convert multi-level reconstruction into meta-authority;
- require a further project above it to make its operations legitimate.

Its credibility depends on internal consistency, explicit records, discriminating cases, countercases, source sensitivity, inspectability, and preserved failure.

---

## 6. No Ontology of Strata

STRATA does not claim that reality consists of discrete analytical layers.

It asserts no:

- final constituents;
- ultimate composite;
- universal micro–meso–macro hierarchy;
- objectively fixed level boundaries;
- metaphysical depth relation;
- universally smallest praxis-relevant element;
- universally largest admissible composite.

Canonical corrective form:

```text
Within frame C and granularity g,
X can be reconstructed through Y
for claim Q.
```

Not:

```text
X is fundamentally or ultimately made of Y.
```

Relative level is an analytical relation, not a real stratum.

---

## 7. Type and Projection Boundary

Every `PROJECT_AS` operation must distinguish:

```text
origin type
≠
target function
```

An origin-typed object may perform a bounded function in a declared target context without becoming that function as a new origin type.

Examples:

```text
trajectory
PROJECT_AS
frame-function

≠

trajectory becomes frame as origin type
```

```text
functions as Α
≠
is Α as a new primitive
```

A target function must remain:

- context-bound;
- source-traceable;
- counterfactually sensitive;
- claim-limited;
- revisable;
- non-primitive.

Structural analogy, metaphor, translation success, and label similarity do not establish a valid functional projection.

---

## 8. Admissibility Boundary

Every STRATA operation must remain within the STRATA Admissibility Band.

### Lower bound — Praxeological Relevance Floor

Below the lower bound, additional distinctions do not alter a warranted praxis-relevant reconstruction.

```text
additional detail
+
no changed reconstruction
=
distinction without praxeological purchase
```

### Upper bound — Praxeological Traceability Ceiling

Above the upper bound, a composition, decomposition, or projection no longer carries reconstructible structural load from its source objects.

```text
material source change
+
arbitrarily unchanged or source-independent result
=
abstraction without traceable load
```

### Minimal admissibility rule

```text
Admissible(T, X, C)
iff
PraxisPurchase
and TraceableLoad
and TypeIntegrity
and ContextualBoundedness
```

A complete assessment must additionally consider:

- source support and Source Ceiling;
- reference continuity;
- functional continuity;
- counterfactual sensitivity;
- declared loss;
- alternative transformations;
- calibration limits;
- claim scope and Claim Ceiling;
- Authority Ceiling;
- stop conditions;
- non-capture.

Availability of an operation does not establish its admissibility.

---

## 9. Loss and Non-Invertibility Boundary

No STRATA transformation is presumed lossless or perfectly reversible.

```text
DECOMPOSE(COMPOSE(X))
≠
X
```

```text
COMPOSE(DECOMPOSE(X))
≠
X
```

```text
PROJECT_AS(X)
≠
X as a new origin type
```

Every transformation must disclose, where applicable:

- what is preserved;
- what is foregrounded;
- what is compressed;
- what is excluded;
- what remains uncertain;
- what is irrecoverable.

Compression is permitted. Concealed compression is not.

---

## 10. Authority Boundary

The governing distinction of PMS-STRATA is:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

This applies equally to:

- finer decomposition;
- larger composition;
- higher relative level;
- formal precision;
- technical implementation;
- cross-level projection;
- successful validation of a record.

Therefore:

```text
successful transformation
≠
permission to intervene
```

```text
higher-level function
≠
higher authority
```

```text
finer reconstruction
≠
stronger judgment
```

### Authority ceiling

The **authority ceiling** is the limit beyond which a structural transformation would illegitimately inherit or generate empirical, causal, normative, person-evaluative, legal, political, diagnostic, intervention, or application authority.

```text
claim ceiling
≠
authority ceiling

successful transformation
≠
application authority
```

The Claim Ceiling bounds the maximum warranted assertion in a record. The Authority Ceiling blocks forms of authority that STRATA cannot license even when a transformation is structurally admissible.

No STRATA result inherits authority beyond the claim type, evidence, scope, reversibility, and application conditions already permitted by PMS.

---

## 11. Person-, Psychology-, and Judgment Boundary

STRATA reconstructs structures of praxis. It does not establish psychological or ontological truths about persons.

It is not intended for:

- clinical diagnosis;
- personality typing;
- mental-state inference;
- individual person evaluation;
- automated moral judgment;
- forensic decision-making;
- irreversible labeling;
- policy enforcement;
- ranking human beings;
- converting configuration-level functions into person-level essences.

Canonical prohibition:

```text
configuration performs asymmetry-function
≠
person is asymmetry
```

All findings remain scene-bound, claim-typed, revisable, and subject to distance, self-binding discipline, dignity-in-practice, calibration, and external accountability.

---

## 12. Empirical and Formalization Boundary

STRATA may be represented through prose, diagrams, YAML, JSON Schema, decision trees, records, software, and reader interfaces.

Such representations may test:

- syntactic completeness;
- declared operation type;
- required fields;
- internal consistency;
- type-integrity declarations;
- admissibility procedure;
- permitted output classes.

They do not by themselves establish:

- empirical truth;
- actual causality;
- semantic adequacy;
- exhaustive capture;
- ontological reality;
- normative validity;
- superiority over rival grammars;
- implementation of praxis or subjectivity.

```text
formal validity
≠
empirical adequacy
```

```text
machine-readable representation
≠
implementation of praxis
```

```text
translation success
≠
proof of exhaustive capture
```

## 13. Failure, Claim Reduction, and Stop

A transformation must be narrowed, reduced, stopped, or rejected when:

- it creates no additional praxis-relevant difference;
- source support is exhausted;
- source reference becomes unstable;
- traceable load disappears;
- origin type and target function collapse;
- frame, granularity, or relative level are mixed without declaration;
- the target context becomes arbitrary;
- calibration deteriorates;
- further detail becomes resolution drift;
- further abstraction becomes label substitution;
- a new level is introduced primarily to rescue a failed claim;
- counterexamples are absorbed by repeated redefinition.

Stop is a positive methodological result. It is not an embarrassment or an unfinished proof.

Re-entry after stop requires a genuinely new source basis, frame, question, or transformation claim. It may not occur through silent continuation.

---

## 14. Non-Capture Rule

PMS-STRATA must preserve the possibility that:

- no stable path or trajectory can be composed;
- no source-supported decomposition is available;
- no bounded target function can be justified;
- available sources do not support the required granularity;
- competing transformations remain equally plausible;
- every available transformation destroys decisive structure;
- another grammar captures the object more adequately.

`non_capture` is a bounded output, not a rescue device.

It must not be used to:

- immunize a weak claim;
- evade an available counterexample;
- imply ineffable depth;
- preserve authority after analytical failure.

---

## 15. Explicit Non-Goals

PMS-STRATA is not:

- a superior PMS layer;
- a meta-authority;
- a new operator grammar;
- an ontology of reality or layers;
- a theory of final constituents;
- a universal scale system;
- a lossless transformation grammar;
- an unlimited decomposition method;
- an automatic generator of macro-structures;
- a mechanism for dissolving counterexamples;
- a source of person-level judgment;
- a source of additional application authority;
- a proof that every structure can be translated into PMS;
- a proof that PMS is complete, uniquely ordered, or beyond criticism.

---

## 16. Canonical Transformation Rule

A STRATA operation is legitimate only if it:

1. identifies its source object;
2. declares frame, granularity, relative level, temporal scope, and claim scope;
3. specifies `COMPOSE`, `DECOMPOSE`, or `PROJECT_AS`;
4. states the expected praxeological difference;
5. remains traceable to constitutive source structures;
6. preserves reference and type integrity;
7. discloses selection and loss;
8. permits counterfactual failure;
9. defines its validity scope;
10. preserves claim reduction, stop, and non-capture;
11. prohibits authority inheritance.

---

## 17. Compact Audit

A STRATA claim must be rejected or revised unless all relevant questions can be answered:

```text
What is the source object?
Which operation is being performed?
What are the source and target frames?
What changes in granularity or relative level?
What is the expected praxis-relevant gain?
What source structures carry the result?
Would a material source change alter the result?
Is origin type preserved?
What is compressed, excluded, or lost?
What rival transformation remains possible?
Where does the claim stop?
Can the result be non-capture?
Has any additional authority been implied?
```

---

## 18. Final Boundary

> PMS-STRATA does not claim that every praxis structure can be placed on a final map. It claims that where PMS moves between temporal structures, granularities, composites, and relative levels, those movements can be made explicit, typed, bounded, and criticizable.

Its value lies not in allowing PMS to speak at every scale.

Its value lies in specifying when PMS should not move further.
