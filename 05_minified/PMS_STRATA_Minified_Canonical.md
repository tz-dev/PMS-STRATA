## 2. Governing Claim

> **PMS-STRATA specifies how praxis structures may be composed, decomposed, and functionally projected across declared granularities and relative levels—while preserving source traceability, origin-type integrity, transformation loss, claim boundaries, stop, and non-capture. It does not turn analytical recursion into ontological totality, greater authority, or immunity from failure.**

STRATA is a bounded transformation discipline within PMS.

It increases explicit analytical control over vertical movement.

It does not increase:

- the number of PMS base operators;
- the authority of PMS;
- the truth status of a reconstruction;
- the permission to intervene;
- the right to classify persons;
- or the completeness of the grammar.

Canonical governing distinction:

```text
more structure
≠
more authority
```

## 3. Base-Grammar Dependency

PMS Base remains the sole theoretical foundation of STRATA.

STRATA uses the existing Δ–Ψ grammar and must not silently:

- add, remove, rename, reorder, or redefine PMS operators;
- alter the default PMS dependency path;
- treat a derived object as a new base primitive;
- decompose an operator type as though it were an empirical composite;
- override PMS application guardrails;
- convert a contextual function into a new operator identity;
- or claim unqualified PMS conformity while changing the base grammar.

The mandatory distinction is:

```text
operator type
≠
operator occurrence
```

An operator type is a formal element of PMS Base.

An operator occurrence is a concrete, frame-bound, source-bound analytical typing.

STRATA may transform occurrences and composites.

STRATA does not transform the formal identity of PMS operators.

## 5. Object Model

### 5.1 Operator type

A canonical function within the Δ–Ψ grammar.

It is:

- abstract;
- base-defined;
- non-empirical as a type;
- and not decomposable by STRATA.

### 5.2 Operator occurrence

A concrete analytical typing of a source-bound structure within a declared frame.

It is:

- local;
- revisable;
- criticizable;
- and potentially deconstructible as an occurrence.

### 5.3 Composite structure

A structured relation among multiple occurrences, configurations, events, non-events, transitions, paths, or derived objects.

A composite is not merely a set.

It requires:

- selection;
- ordering or relation;
- formation logic;
- and loss disclosure.

### 5.4 Configuration

A frame-bound reconstruction of a temporally situated praxis structure.

A configuration may contain:

- positive events;
- relevant non-events;
- roles;
- asymmetries;
- bindings;
- frames;
- action corridors;
- and unresolved residue.

### 5.5 Event

A positively marked relevant structural change within a frame.

An event may be punctual or temporally extended.

### 5.6 Non-event

A structured non-realization relative to a warranted expectation frame.

```text
missing information
≠
non-event
```

### 5.7 Transition

A structured relation between configurations that preserves more than state difference.

It may include:

- events;
- non-events;
- ordering;
- delay;
- changed costs;
- changed asymmetries;
- changed bindings;
- and changed action corridors.

### 5.8 Derived analytical objects

STRATA may derive objects such as:

- sequence;
- path;
- trajectory;
- macro-event;
- frame-function;
- attractor-function;
- modulating profile;
- higher-level composite function.

These are not new PMS primitives.

Canonical distinction:

```text
derived analytical object
≠
new base operator
```

### 6.1 Frame

The contextual boundary that determines:

- the object of reconstruction;
- relevant distinctions;
- internal and external relations;
- expected non-events;
- source relevance;
- and claim scope.

### 6.2 Granularity

The resolution of distinctions used in a reconstruction.

Granularity is not:

- truth rank;
- ontological depth;

The position of an object within a declared transformation relation.

- relational;
- local;
- operation-dependent;
- and non-ontological.

### 6.4 Temporal scope

The time range relevant to the reconstruction, including:

- beginning;
- endpoint or open continuation;
- relevant prior conditions;

It must distinguish:

- direct support;
- inferred structure;

- uncertain structure;
- and source ceiling.

No claim may inherit authority merely because its object occupies a relatively higher or finer analytical position.

## 7. Four-Part Architecture

PMS-STRATA consists of four mutually constraining Parts.

```text
PATH
→ temporal composition and trajectory formation

SUB
→ decomposition under finer granularity

RETYPE
→ bounded cross-level functional projection

LIMITS
→ admissibility, continuity, loss, anti-immunization, stop, and non-capture
```

### 7.1 PATH

PATH reconstructs:

- temporal order;
- transitions;
- sequences;
- paths;
- trajectories;
- branches;
- unrealized alternatives;
- non-events;
- sedimentation;
- and path dependence.

Its principal operation is `COMPOSE`.

PATH does not by itself assign a completed trajectory a new target function.

### 7.2 SUB

SUB reconstructs the internal structure of provisionally compressed occurrences and composites.

Its principal operation is `DECOMPOSE`.

SUB does not decompose PMS operator types.

SUB must preserve or critically revise the relation between finer structures and the coarser source function.

### 7.3 RETYPE

RETYPE specifies the bounded function an origin-typed object may perform within a declared target context.

Its principal operation is `PROJECT_AS`.

RETYPE preserves origin type and source reference.

It adds a contextual function; it does not replace the source identity.

### 7.4 LIMITS

LIMITS applies throughout PATH, SUB, RETYPE, and all operation chains.

It governs:

- Relevance Floor;
- Traceability Ceiling;
- Type Integrity;
- continuity;
- loss;
- source limits;
- calibration;
- anti-immunization;
- claim reduction;
- stop;
- and non-capture.

LIMITS is constitutive, not a later warning layer.

## 8. The Three Canonical Operations

PMS-STRATA contains exactly three canonical operations:

```text
COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE
PROJECT_AS
```

No fourth core operation is assumed.

### 8.1 COMPOSE

Question:

> How may multiple or sequential source structures form a declared composite object?

Direction:

```text
multiple / ordered source structures
→
composite analytical object
```

Typical outputs:

- sequence;
- path;
- trajectory;
- event cluster;
- branch structure;
- other declared composite.

COMPOSE must specify:

- source objects;
- ordering rule;
- selection rule;
- formation rule;
- target object;
- preserved structure;
- compressed structure;
- excluded structure;
- irrecoverable loss;
- expected praxeological difference;
- and claim scope.

COMPOSE does not automatically produce:

- a target function;
- an operator identity;
- a causal law;
- or a total history.

Central failure mode:

```text
macro-label without traceable path or constitutive relation
```

### 8.2 DECOMPOSE

Question:

> How may a provisionally compressed occurrence or composite be reconstructed under finer granularity?

Direction:

```text
compressed source object
→
finer relational reconstruction of that source object
```

DECOMPOSE applies to:

- operator occurrences;
- events;
- non-events;
- transitions;
- paths;
- trajectories;
- composite structures;
- and derived analytical objects.

DECOMPOSE does not apply to PMS operator types as formal primitives.

DECOMPOSE must specify:

- source object;
- source function or current typing;
- decomposition question;
- granularity change;
- expected additional difference;
- components;
- component relations;
- internal temporality;
- source support;
- preserved or revised source function;
- and stop condition.

Central failure mode:

```text
fragmentation or detail accumulation without reconstructive relation to the source object
```

### 8.3 PROJECT_AS

Question:

> What bounded function may an origin-typed object perform within a declared target context?

Direction:

```text
origin-typed source object
→
contextual target function
```

Canonical form:

```text
X retains origin type T_o
and
within target context C_t
performs bounded function F_t
```

PROJECT_AS must specify:

- source object;
- origin type;
- source frame and level;
- target context;
- target function;
- target frame and level;
- constitutive source trace;
- counterfactual sensitivity;
- validity scope;
- projection loss;
- alternative projections;
- and claim ceiling.

Central prohibition:

```text
target function
≠
new origin type
```

Central failure mode:

```text
label substitution, analogy drift, or invalid type jump
```

### 8.4 PMS occurrence anchoring for RETYPE

Where a source object or target-function claim materially depends on PMS operator-typed occurrences, the claim remains reconstructible through the already warranted occurrence Records, relevant canonical dependency context, material occurrence-level variation, and exact five-part Loss. Operator names alone do not supply Source Trace.

```text
operator-occurrence anchoring
≠ mandatory full Δ–Ψ inventory
≠ new schema field
≠ new audit stage
≠ target-fit inference of source typing
```

The abstract operator type and canonical grammar remain unchanged. Only concrete claim-relevant occurrences and relations are varied or traced.

## 9. Operation Identity Matrix

| Criterion | COMPOSE | DECOMPOSE | PROJECT_AS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary movement | formation | opening | contextual function |
| Typical direction | many/sequential → composite | compressed → finer | source object → target function |
| Reference identity | source objects remain traceable in new composite | same source object remains reconstruction target | same source object and origin type remain |
| Main output | new analytical object | finer relational reconstruction | bounded target function |
| Primary loss | compression and selection | source fragmentation or relation loss | foregrounding and backgrounding |
| Main test | does ordering/formation create a warranted composite? | does finer resolution alter a warranted reconstruction? | does the source object actually carry the target function? |
| Central confusion | chronology as path | detail as decomposition | renaming or analogy as projection |

Compact decision rule:

```text
Are several or sequential structures being formed into a new analytical object?
→ COMPOSE

Is the internal structure of the same source object being opened?
→ DECOMPOSE

Is an origin-typed object being assigned a bounded function in a target context?
→ PROJECT_AS

None of the above?
→ no STRATA operation established
```

## 11. The STRATA Admissibility Band

A possible transformation is not thereby an admissible transformation.

```text
available operation
≠
admissible operation
```

The admissible operating range lies between two limits.

### 11.1 Praxeological Relevance Floor

The lower bound asks whether the added distinction or transformation changes a warranted reconstruction of praxis-relevant structure.

Canonical formulation:

> **Below the Praxeological Relevance Floor, additional resolution or transformation is not an additional praxis finding. It is distinction without praxeological purchase.**

Possible praxis-relevant dimensions include:

- action corridors;
- costs;
- exposure;
- roles;
- asymmetries;
- expectations;
- non-events;
- bindings;
- temporal order;
- delay;
- irreversibility;
- integration;
- and stop conditions.

Lower-bound failure includes:

- detail without changed reconstruction;
- chronology without path gain;
- renaming without functional gain;
- event inflation;
- profile inflation;
- or formal precision without praxis difference.

### 11.2 Praxeological Traceability Ceiling

The upper bound asks whether the result remains reconstructibly dependent on its source structures.

Canonical formulation:

> **Above the Praxeological Traceability Ceiling, abstraction no longer carries reconstructible praxis load. It becomes a label without traceable source structure.**

Traceable load requires:

- identifiable constitutive source features;
- preserved relevant relations;
- preserved historical or internal temporality where applicable;
- preserved cost and asymmetry structure where relevant;
- source-target dependency;
- and declared compression.

Upper-bound failure includes:

- trajectory without path trace;
- macro-event without internal duration;
- attractor-function without recurrent stabilizing structure;
- macro-asymmetry without distributed load trace;
- projection insensitive to source changes;
- or a large label that survives contradictory sources unchanged.

### 11.3 Operating range

```text
BELOW
insufficient praxis purchase

WITHIN
warranted difference with traceable source load and bounded claim

ABOVE
abstraction, fragmentation, or projection without reconstructible source load
```

### 11.4 No universal scale

STRATA does not define:

- a universal smallest praxis element;
- a universal largest legitimate composite;
- fixed micro, meso, or macro classes;
- or context-free thresholds.

The band is relational to:

- source object;
- frame;
- granularity;
- temporal scope;
- source basis;
- transformation context;
- and claim scope.

A strong result in one dimension does not cancel failure in another.

```text

cannot compensate for
```

- adequate source support;
- declared selection and loss;
- counterfactual criticizability;
- operation-specific preservation duties;
- alternatives or no-transformation option;
- claim ceiling;
- stop condition;
- and preserved non-capture possibility.

## 13. Continuity and Integrity

### 13.1 Reference continuity

The transformed reconstruction must remain related to the same historical or structural referent where reference preservation is claimed.

Name continuity alone is insufficient.

### 13.2 Type integrity

The origin type must remain visible and must not be overwritten by a target function, component set, profile, or higher-level label.

### 13.3 Functional continuity

A target or source function must remain dependent on concrete source features.

Relevant changes to those features must be capable of changing the function claim.

### 13.4 Temporal continuity

Where temporal objects are involved, the reconstruction must preserve enough of:

- order;
- duration;
- transition structure;
- sedimentation;
- branch loss;

### 13.5 Contextual boundedness

Every result must be limited to a declared:

- frame;

- granularity;
- relative level;

## 14. Counterfactual Sensitivity

Core question:

> **Would a relevant change in the source structure alter the transformation result?**

The test must modify a feature declared as:

- constitutive;
- strongly modulating;
- temporally load-bearing;
- or functionally necessary.

Operation-specific forms:

```text
COMPOSE
Would changed order, omission, or branch structure alter the composite claim?

DECOMPOSE
Would changed or removed components alter the reconstructed source function?

PROJECT_AS
Would a relevant change in the source object alter or dissolve the target function?
```

Canonical sensitivity classes:

```text
strongly_sensitive
partially_sensitive
weakly_sensitive
insensitive
underdetermined
untestable
```

Counterfactual Sensitivity is a load test.

It is not:

- causal proof;
- empirical experimentation by itself;
- or complete validation.

An untestable result may remain provisional or source-limited, but it may not be represented as strongly established.

Every transformation selects.

### 15.1 COMPOSE selects

- source elements;
- sequence or relation;
- periodization;
- branch relevance;
- target object;
- and compression level.

### 15.2 DECOMPOSE selects

- added distinctions;
- components;
- relations;
- internal scales;
- and source-function tests.

### 15.3 PROJECT_AS selects

- target context;
- target function;
- load-bearing source features;
- foregrounded relations;
- and validity scope.

Every record must distinguish:

```yaml
loss:
  preserved:
  compressed:
  excluded:
  uncertain:
  irrecoverable:
```

Loss is not automatic failure.

Hidden loss is a failure of disclosure and may invalidate the claim.

A legitimate transformation may be lossy if:

- the loss is declared;
- constitutive structures remain traceable;
- the claim is correspondingly bounded;
- and no false completeness is asserted.

## 16. Source and Calibration Limits

Formal precision must not exceed source precision.

```text
precise schema
≠
precise reality claim
```

Every transformation must distinguish:

- direct support;
- inferred structure;
- unsupported detail;
- missing structure;
- source ceiling;
- and calibration-sensitive thresholds.

Missing information is not a non-event.

```text
no record
≠
recorded non-occurrence
```

Calibration remains open where STRATA cannot establish universal thresholds, including:

- when repetition forms an attractor-function;
- when duration forms a trajectory;
- when local asymmetries support a higher-level function;
- when analogy supports projection;
- and when finer resolution ceases to provide purchase.

A legitimate output may state:

```text
structural form identified,
threshold remains calibration-sensitive
```

## 17. Canonical Output System

STRATA distinguishes between:

1. **operation-specific results**, which describe what happened within PATH, SUB, or RETYPE;
2. **canonical output classes**, which normalize the governance result across the system.

The canonical output classes are:

```text
admissible
admissible_with_bounded_claim
admissible_but_provisional
resolution_neutral
analogy_only
partially_admissible
claim_reduction_required
mandatory_stop
failed_transformation
non_capture
```

### 17.1 Canonical meanings

#### admissible

The operation passes all required gates for its declared claim without a further output-relevant restriction beyond ordinary STRATA boundedness.

#### admissible_with_bounded_claim

A material narrowing of validity scope, reach, or generality relative to the tested claim is itself the decisive governance result. Ordinary Contextual Boundedness required of every STRATA claim does not automatically produce this class.

#### admissible_but_provisional

The operation is coherent and sufficiently supported for provisional use, but source, counterfactual, calibration, temporal, or rival-reconstruction limits remain material.

#### resolution_neutral

Additional resolution is valid but does not change the warranted praxis reconstruction.

#### analogy_only

A useful structural similarity is present, but semantic preservation or functional projection is not established.

#### partially_admissible

Some parts of the transformation are warranted while other parts require reduction, separation, or unresolved status.

#### claim_reduction_required

The current claim is too strong, but a weaker claim remains supportable.

#### mandatory_stop

Further transformation under the present conditions would violate relevance, traceability, source, type, calibration, or anti-immunization constraints.

#### failed_transformation

The proposed operation does not satisfy its defining or admissibility conditions.

#### non_capture

The present STRATA reconstruction does not adequately capture the object, relation, or function, and this limitation is preserved as a legitimate result.

### 17.2 Mapping rule

```text
operation-specific result
→
canonical output class
```

The operation-specific result must not replace the governance class, and the governance class must not erase the operation-specific meaning.

For `DECOMPOSE`, operation admissibility and source-function effect are separate. A valid decomposition may reject the prior source function without becoming a `failed_transformation`; the prior source claim is recorded separately as failed, withdrawn, or reduced.

### 17.3 Representative mapping

| Operation-specific result | Canonical output class |
|---|---|
| admissible sequence | admissible |
| admissible path | admissible |
| admissible trajectory | admissible |
| admissible declared composite | admissible |
| admissible path-dependence claim | admissible_with_bounded_claim or admissible |
| provisional path | admissible_but_provisional |
| competing path constructions | admissible_but_provisional, partially_admissible, or non_capture |
| failed composition | failed_transformation |
| admissible decomposition | admissible, admissible_with_bounded_claim, or admissible_but_provisional |
| heterogeneous source object | admissible_with_bounded_claim, admissible_but_provisional, or partially_admissible |
| competing internal models | admissible_but_provisional, partially_admissible, or non_capture |
| resolution-neutral result | resolution_neutral |
| competing decompositions | admissible_but_provisional, partially_admissible, or non_capture |
| unsupported decomposition | failed_transformation |
| resolution drift | mandatory_stop or failed_transformation |
| admissible functional projection | admissible |
| admissible narrow projection | admissible_with_bounded_claim |
| provisional projection | admissible_but_provisional |
| context-dependent projection | admissible or admissible_with_bounded_claim |
| compatible multiple projections | admissible or admissible_with_bounded_claim |
| competing projections | admissible_but_provisional, partially_admissible, or non_capture |
| useful structural analogy | analogy_only |
| label substitution | failed_transformation |
| invalid type jump | failed_transformation |
| unmarked level mixing | failed_transformation |

For `DECOMPOSE`, source-function effects remain a separate axis:

| Source-function effect | Control rule |
|---|---|
| confirmed | does not by itself determine the operation class |
| refined | may require a revised source claim but does not automatically determine the operation class |
| internally differentiated | does not by itself determine bounded, provisional, or partial output |
| partially preserved | requires separate claim-effect recording |
| rejected | may coexist with an admissible DECOMPOSE; the prior claim is recorded separately |
| underdetermined | may contribute to provisionality or non-capture, but requires operation-level rationale |

### 17.4 Mapping discipline

Where more than one canonical class is possible, the record must state why. No default precedence applies between bounded, provisional, partial, failure, stop, and capture-boundary results. Ordinary contextual boundedness does not automatically produce `admissible_with_bounded_claim`.

Example:

```yaml
result:
  operation_specific: competing_projections
  canonical_class: non_capture
  rationale: >
    The candidate projections remain equally supported and cannot be integrated
    without erasing their distinct target functions.
```

## 18. Operation Chains

STRATA operations may form chains, including:

```text
COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS
COMPOSE → DECOMPOSE
DECOMPOSE → COMPOSE
DECOMPOSE → PROJECT_AS
PROJECT_AS → DECOMPOSE
COMPOSE → PROJECT_AS → DECOMPOSE
```

Every link in a chain requires:

- its own operation declaration;
- source and target identity;
- justification;
- expected praxis difference;
- loss record;
- admissibility result;
- validity scope;
- and possible failure.

No successful operation transfers admissibility automatically to the next operation.

```text
admissible step 1
≠
admissible step 2
```

No later operation erases the failure of an earlier claim.

## 19. Non-Invertibility

STRATA transformations are not losslessly reversible.

```text
DECOMPOSE(COMPOSE(X))
≠
X

COMPOSE(DECOMPOSE(X))
≠
X

PROJECT_AS(X)
≠
X as a new origin type
```

Reasons include:

- selection;
- compression;
- changed granularity;
- changed relation structure;
- new source information;
- foregrounding;
- backgrounding;
- and irrecoverable loss.

A reverse operation is a new transformation, not restoration of neutral identity.

---

## 20. Anti-Immunization

STRATA may not use analytical movement to dissolve criticism.

Prohibited rescue forms include:

```text
The objection appears only because the analysis is too coarse.

The objection disappears at the higher level.

The original type failed here, but it functions as that type elsewhere.

The contradiction is only a frame difference.
```

Such responses are legitimate only when they produce a new, separately testable transformation record.

Canonical rule:

> **A change of granularity, level, composition, frame, or target function constitutes a new criticizable reconstruction. It does not erase the failure of the claim from which the transformation began.**

Every anti-immunization check asks:

1. What original claim was challenged?
2. Was it corrected or merely moved?
3. Which new operation was introduced?
4. Does the new operation create a separate claim?
5. Does the original countercase remain visible?
6. Can the new claim also fail?

## 21. Claim Reduction

A transformation need not be accepted or rejected only in its strongest form.

Possible reductions include:

```text
trajectory
→
path

→
sequence

strong path dependence
→
weak order dependence

functional projection
→
structural analogy

strong target function
```

## 22. Stop

Stop is a positive methodological result.

A stop may indicate that:

- the relevant distinction has been reached;
- the sources are exhausted;
- further abstraction would lose traceability;
- further decomposition would lose the source object;
- calibration has collapsed;
- or additional transformation would only repeat or relocate the claim.

### 22.1 General stop triggers

```text
no additional praxis purchase
loss of traceable load
source ceiling reached
type integrity cannot be maintained
reference continuity lost
calibration no longer possible
anti-immunization violation
```

### 22.2 Mandatory and optional stop

```text
mandatory stop
→ continuation would be inadmissible
→ canonical output: mandatory_stop

optional stop
→ continuation may be possible but is unnecessary for the present claim
→ methodological concept; no additional canonical output class
```

### 22.3 Re-entry

Re-entry after stop requires a new record and a changed condition, such as:

- new sources;
- a new claim;
- a new relevant countercase;
- a newly justified granularity;
- or a new calibration basis.

Re-entry is not silent continuation.

## 23. Non-Capture

Non-capture records that PMS-STRATA does not adequately capture the relevant object, relation, or function under the present conditions.

Possible forms include:

```text
source_non_capture
granularity_non_capture
compositional_non_capture
projection_non_capture
calibration_non_capture
semantic_non_capture
partial_capture
```

Non-capture must specify:

- what was attempted;
- what was captured;
- what remained uncaptured;
- why the limitation occurred;
- which alternatives or rivals remain relevant;
- and what would permit later re-entry.

Non-capture is not a shield against criticism.

It preserves the possibility that:

- the source is insufficient;
- no admissible granularity exists;
- no composition preserves the relevant heterogeneity;
- no target function satisfies the gates;
- another model is superior;
- or the present grammar is semantically inadequate.

Canonical statement:

> **PMS-STRATA preserves the possibility that a structure remains only partially reconstructible, non-composable, non-decomposable, non-projectable, or better captured outside the present grammar.**

## 27. Canonical Audit

- Does it avoid new hidden primitives?

### 27.2 Object and coordinate clarity

- Are derived objects distinguished from operator types?

### 27.6 Rival and failure discipline

- Are relevant alternatives considered?
- Can the transformation fail?

### 27.7 Governance

- Is the operation-specific result mapped to a canonical output class?
- Is the claim ceiling explicit?
- Is a stop condition present?
- Is non-capture possible?
- Is authority inheritance prohibited?
